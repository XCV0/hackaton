Substrate Preparation: The NPs were injected through the PDMS device
channels on a typical SLG/Mo substrate coated with 10 nm of Al2O3.To
prepare for the colloidal solution injection into the device, holes for tube
connections were made. Subsequently, the substrate surface underwent
an O2 plasma treatment to enhance adhesion at the interface when it
bonded to the PDMS device. Afterward, the PDMS device was attached
to the substrate and subjected to 20 min of heating at 65 °C. The ﬁnal step
consisted of injecting the NPs solution into the microﬂuidic device using a
syringe through microtubes. Following an optimized drying period, the
PDMS device was separated from the substrate. The Au NPs were then
encapsulated with Al2O3. For the Al2O3 layers deposition the ALD Beneq
TFS 200 system was employed at 200 °C, involving 143 cycles of trimethy-
laluminium (TMA) and DI water, with pulse/purge step durations of 150/
650 ms (TMA) and 150/1000 ms (water). For the contact scheme pattern-
ing, NIL was utilized. The developed pattern consisted of trenches with a
nominal width of 700 nm and a nominal pitch of 2800 nm. A hexamethyl-
disilizane (HMDS) vapor priming stage was conducted to enhance pho-
toresist adhesion on the substrate. A 200 nm layer of mr.NIL60002E resist
was spin coated onto the substrate using the Karl Suss Optical Track sys-
tem. The trench pattern was subsequently imprinted onto the substrate
through NIL. Initially, the temperature was raised to 65 °C for 10 s, fol-
lowed by increasing the pressure to 15 bar for 100 s. UV light exposure
for 180 s (/C255500 mJ cm/C02) was initiated. An O2 strip process to remove
residual resist was conducted using the SPTS Pegasus system, with an O2
ﬂow rate of 115 sccm at 13.56 MHz. RIE process was then performed
using the STPS APS tool with a He, H2, and C4F8 plasma chemistry.
Finally, the unexposed resist was removed using a PVA TEPLA plasma
asher. Besides the prepared PPS with the structure SLG/Mo/Al2O3/Au
NPs/Al2O3, a reference (Ref ) one (SLG/Mo) was also used for device
comparison purposes.
Solar Cell Development: In both the Ref and PPS substrates, a 15 nm
layer of sodium ﬂuoride (NaF) was evaporated on top of the complete
prepared substrates. In the same batch, an ultrathin ACIGS layer with
an average thickness of /C25450 nm, determined through SEM cross-section
analysis, was coevaporated onto both substrates. Elemental composition
analysis using X-ray ﬂuorescence with the Panalytical Epsilon 5 equipment
yielded: [Cu]/([Ga]þ[In]) = 0.83, [Ga]/([Ga]þ[In]) = 0.29, and [Ag]/
([Ag]þ[Cu]) = 0.11. Further details regarding the ACIGS deposition pro-
cess can be found in other studies.[102–105] The subsequent deposition
of the remaining layers (CdS/i-ZnO/ZnO:Al) followed the standard base-
line as reported elsewhere.[50] A chemical etching method was employed
to pattern individual 0.1 cm2 solar cells in a 1.5 /C2 1.5 cm2 square array.[106]
Characterization: TEM employing the JEM-2100-HT instrument was
used to analyze the synthesized Au NPs, with their size determined using
ImageJ software. For the TEM, solutions were deposited onto a carbon
substrate supported by a Cu mesh grid. Relative absorbance and diffuse
reﬂectance measurements were conducted via a Perkin-Elmer Lambda
950 ultraviolet-visible-near-infrared spectrophotometer. An integrat
ing sphere was used for the diffuse reﬂectance measurements. A
FeiNovaNanoSEM 650 high-resolution SEM operating at 5 kV was
employed. A morphological assessment of the patterned sample was per-
formed utilizing the AFM Dimension Icon system. Tapping mode was
used with a scan rate of 0.5 Hz. Illuminated and dark J–V measurements
under AM1.5 G conditions were carried out using an in-house measure-
ment system. The J–V measurements were conducted after a 20 min
period of light exposure. EQE measurements were performed using a
QEX10 equipment, employing a chopped monochromatic light scan
covering wavelengths from 300 to 1100 nm, with a step interval of 5 nm.
Optoelectronic Simulations: For simulating the scattering and absorp-
tion cross sections of the Au NPs, the FDTD numerical method, which
is accessible within the Lumerical software, was used.[64,107] The simula-
tion setup incorporated a total-ﬁeld-scattered-ﬁeld (TFSF) source with a
bandwidth spanning from 300 to 1100 nm. The choice of a TFSF source
allows the decoupling of the scattering behavior of the simulated Au NPs,
as it effectively separates the scattered ﬁeld from the incident ﬁeld. The
optical performance of the developed novel CIGS solar cells was simulated
using the FDTD numerical method to comprehensively investigate the
impact of the new architecture on the optical performance of solar cells
with regard to light-trapping effects. Simulations were performed using a
random polarized broadband plane wave light source. To ensure compu-
tational efﬁciency without compromising accuracy, the smallest mesh size
feasible while meeting time-memory requirements was employed. Critical
interfaces were resolved with a 1 nm mesh size. The Violas et al. ultrathin
solar cell baseline[14,20] was employed to estimate the effect of the
improved charge carrier’s collection through the passivation layer integra-
tion on the JSC value. The optical constants for the several materials used
within this work were obtained from previously reported results.[108–111]
