material, Al2O3 was chosen as it has been previously used for
an effective passivation of active recombination mechanisms
at the rear interface,[26,27] as well as exploited as a barrier layer
for metals in ultrathin solar cells,[47,54–56] enabling the metallic
NPs integration. With respect to the contacting approach, a
high-passivating area is desired for a more substantial decrease
in the active recombination mechanisms, while allowing for the
utilization of the signiﬁcant passivation area for effective light
trapping through the encapsulation of metallic NPs.[20,23,57]
Nevertheless, the high passivation area needs to be counterbal-
anced with a contacting area which does not compromise the
charge extraction, as passivation areas higher than 90% have
shown an impact on ultrathin solar cells’ series resistance.[9,20,23]
Therefore, in this work, a line contact pattern with a nominal
passivation area of 75% was chosen to attain an effective passiv-
ation and allow a substantial dielectric coverage to couple with
light trapping, without hindering charge carrier collection.
The dielectric encapsulation layer thickness is as well critical,
since the encapsulation of the metallic nanostructures needs
to be ensured. However, the thickness of the dielectric layer
might have an impact in the resonant behavior of the metallic
NPs. The charge polarization in the NP through an external elec-
tromagnetic ﬁeld will induce a charge accumulation with the
opposite sign at the metallic NP/dielectric medium interface,
which will in turn screen the induced electric ﬁeld in the
NP.[58] The screening effect is dependent on the polarizability
of the dielectric medium, with a higher-refractive-index medium
leading to a redshift of the plasmonic resonance peak and a
stronger resonant intensity.[58,59] Therefore, the thickness of
the dielectric layer will have a signiﬁcant impact on the plas-
monic resonance as it will lower the medium’s local refractive
index at the NPs vicinity and suppress the resonant scatter-
ing.[48,60] A25nmAl2O3 layer has been previously used to effec-
tively encapsulate micrometer-large Au NPs aggregates.[54]
Nevertheless, to dampen the unwanted effects on the NPs’ scat-
tering efﬁciency and considering that individual NPs will be
encapsulated herein in comparison to micrometer-large NPs
aggregates, the encapsulation layer thickness value used was
of 15 nm. Regarding the plasmonic material, Au was chosen
due to the ease of achieving a NPs colloidal solution, high
free-electron density, and stability.[61–63] Furthermore, there is
a size dependence for the absorption and scattering cross section
of the NPs. Thus, the diameter of the used NPs is critical in hav-
ing an efﬁcient scattering substrate. For the selection of the NPs
diameter, the absorption (σabs) and scattering cross sections
(σscat) of Au NPs with diameters ranging between 40 and
140 nm, within a CIGS-based medium, were simulated through
the ﬁnite-difference time-domain (FDTD) method using the
Lumerical software.[64] The NPs were capped by a 15 nm
Al2O3 shell in these simulations, which was the chosen encap-
sulation thickness value within the passivation line contact
scheme. In Figure 1a the normalized scattering cross-section
(Qscat = σscat/G, with G being the geometric cross-section) spec-
