For an effective integration of the metallic NPs within the
ultrathin solar cell, it is essential to completely etch the dielectric
matrix with Au NPs in the desired regions to create line contacts.
If residual Au is left in the line contacts, it may diffuse during the
high-temperature deposition, hampering the solar cell perfor-
mance.[54] In this work, a reactive ion-etching process with
aC4F8-based chemistry was used, which allowed to obtain a
well-deﬁned line pattern without Au NPs in the contacts, as
shown in the SEM image of the patterned substrate in
Figure 7a. Nevertheless, the line pattern may etch through the
NPs, exposing a minor quantity of Au, which could negatively
impact the VOC. An atomic force microscopy (AFM) analysis
was conducted, as presented in Figure 7b, for an accurate mea-
surement of the pattern dimensions. The averaged measured
line width was of 660 nm with a pitch value of 2810 nm, very
close to the nominal desired values, resulting in a passivation
area of 77%. In addition, an overetch of 11 nm was measured,
assuring a complete etch of the Al2O3/Au NPs/Al2O3 stack.
An ultrathin ACIGS layer with a ﬂat [Ga]/([Ga]þ[In]) proﬁle
was deposited on top of the passivated plasmonic substrate
(PPS), as well as in a reference SLG/Mo substrate (Ref ) at a
growth temperature of 550 °C. The absorber deposition was
followed by chemical bath deposition of CdS and sputtering of
the window i-ZnO/Al:ZnO bilayer.[50] SEM cross-section images
of both prepared devices, Ref and PPS, prior to the window
deposition are shown in Figure 8a,b, respectively. The ACIGS
layer appears to be uniform for both devices, with large grains
and without any clear shunting paths, meaning that the presence
of the Au NPs did not affect the ACIGS layer growth. An absorber
thickness of /C25450 nm was obtained for both devices. The SEM
analysis suggests an effective encapsulation of the Au NPs with
the thin dielectric layer in the PPS device, with the NPs remain-
ing with a spherical shape even after the harsh ACIGS layer depo-
sition, that is, a 550 °C temperature and Se atmosphere. The NPs
remain intact and encapsulated after the absorber deposition,
which shows the viability of using a passivation layer for the
NPs encapsulation, enabling the exploitation of the resonant
scattering effects.
