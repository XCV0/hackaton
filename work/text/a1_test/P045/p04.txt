than 100 nm, the scattering efﬁciency only increases slightly, as
there is a broadening and dampening of the resonant Qscat peaks.
Nevertheless, a scattering efﬁciency value over 50% is an indica-
tion that the scattering cross section is overcoming absorption.
In ultrathin devices, the optical losses become more predomi-
nant for wavelength values over 600 nm.[17] Thus, for an efﬁcient
plasmonic architecture, it is desired to attain a scattering efﬁ-
ciency value higher than 50% in an optical window between
600 nm and the absorber bandgap wavelength value, which is
only ensured for diameter values equal to or above 100 nm.
Furthermore, in previous works, the integration of spherical
NPs with diameters values between 150 and 200 nm at the
absorber rear has hampered the optoelectronic performance of
ultrathin devices.[46] Consequently, to ensure a high scattering
efﬁciency and uncompromised device electrical performance,
the NP diameter value is a key parameter. Considering that
for diameter values higher than 120 nm the scattering
efﬁciency only improves slightly, a NP diameter range of
100–120 nm was deemed optimal for the effective integration
of plasmonic NPs within the line contact Al2O3 passivation
scheme at the rear contact of ultrathin devices. For an efﬁcient
scattering substrate, it is additionally important to ensure the
deposition of individualized metallic NPs with minimal aggre-
gates along with sufﬁcient surface coverage.[66] The aforemen-
tioned requirements are essential to attain a controlled and
strong scattering resonance, as the aggregation of metallic
NPs leads to a broadening and weakening of the plasmon
resonance, while the surface coverage inﬂuences the substrate’s
scattering efﬁciency.[37,66] Therefore, for the NPs synthesis, a
hydroxylamine seeding growth procedure was followed to
attain uniform 100–120 nm NPs.[67] A microﬂuidic device-based
deposition process that ensures a slow solvent evaporation was
adapted for the deposition of individualized metallic NPs on an
ultrathin solar cell substrate.[68] Deposition process optimiza-
tions were performed to increase the NPs surface coverage and
consequently an improved scattering performance. Following the
NPs synthesis and deposition optimization, those were encapsu-
lated within the Al2O3 passivation line contact scheme for the
integration in an ultrathin (Ag,Cu)(Ga,In)Se2 (ACIGS) device.
Adding Ag to the CIGS compound lowers the absorber melting
point, allowing for larger grains. It has been suggested that it will
enable a lower defect density within the absorber and reduced
surface roughness, which may facilitate the integration of struc-
tured rear contacts.[69,70] The plasmonic and passivated approach
allows for coupling the optical gains from the NPs with the
electrical ones arising from the integration within a passivation
scheme that has been successfully developed for ultrathin
devices.[20,23,27]
2.2. Nanoparticles Synthesis
To obtain a 100–120 nm Au NPs solution, a two-step approach
was used. First, a NPs seed solution (Seed NPs) was synthesized
following the Turkevich method[71]; then, the seeds were subse-
quently grown (Grown NPs) following a hydroxylamine seeding
procedure.[67] The hydroxylamine favors the Au ions reduction at
the NPs surface, therefore, effectively using the Seed NPs as a
growth template and avoiding new nucleation sites, enabling
solutions with better dispersity for diameters as large as
200 nm, in comparison to a one-step synthesis process.[67]
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis was con-
ducted to evaluate the average size and dispersity of the
Grown NPs solution, Figure 2a. Besides spherical NPs, a nano-
rod population is also present in the TEM image, which has been
previously reported in hydroxylamine seeding processes to
obtain large NPs (diameter > 40 nm).[67,72,73] The percentage
of nanorods in the total NP population was estimated by evalu-
ating the number of particles that have an aspect ratio higher
than 1.3, and a percentage of 10.3% was found. The metallic
NPs geometry strongly affects the plasmonic resonance, particu-
larly the resonant wavelength value.[58] In the particular case of
nanorods, as a result of the anisotropic shape of these NPs, mul-
tiple dipole resonant modes at distinct wavelength values are
present: transverse mode, the electron oscillations are perpendic-
ular to the nanorod long axis; and the longitudinal mode, which
corresponds to charge oscillation along the nanorod length.[58]
Thus, the plasmonic behavior may be impacted due to the pres-
ence of different NP populations. The diameter of 1300 spherical
NPs (aspect ratio < 1.3) was measured with the size distribution
histogram presented in Figure 2b. The spherical Grown NPs
have a modal distribution with an average diameter of 105 nm
and a standard deviation of around 12 nm, which encompasses
a diameter value within the desired range to attain an optical
improvement without degrading the electrical performance of
the solar cell device. To evaluate the impact of the presence of
different NPs populations on the resonant conditions of the
developed solution, the ideal absorbance of a solution containing
only spherical NPs with an average diameter of 105 nm was sim-
ulated. To do so, the extinction cross section of a 105 nm spheri-
cal NP was ﬁrst simulated through the FDTD method. The
