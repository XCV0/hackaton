of the passivation layer, by varying the rear surface recombina-
tion velocity parameter (SRV) regarding a reference solar cell. To
accurately simulate the VOC gain obtained with the PPS architec-
ture, SRV value decrease from 1 /C2 107 to 2 /C2 104 cm s/C01 was
required which ultimately led to a 1.2 mA cm/C02 JSC gain. The
improved charge carrier collection and the bandgap energy value
discrepancy between devices contribute to the observed disparity
between simulated and experimental values. Finally, it is impor-
tant to note the large standard deviation in all ﬁgures of merit in
the Ref device, in comparison to the PPS one, which has been
attributed to the shunting in several Ref solar cells that has been
mitigated with the PPS architecture.[22] Thus, a more effective
comparison might be done through the JSC value between the
best cells for each device in which shunting is not evident,
Figure 10b. The JSC gain for the best PPS with respect to the best
Ref cell is 2.4 mA cm/C02, closer to the simulated difference of
1.4 mA cm/C02. Besides the observed JSC improvement, there is
a 23 mV increase in the average VOC value for the PPS device
and a 31 mV improvement if the best cells are compared, that
is, without a Ref cell demonstrating a shunting effect. The
VOC enhancements further suggest that no Au has diffused to
the absorber and that an effective passivation of active recombi-
nation mechanisms in the PPS device is present.[26,27]
Considering the difference in the measured Eg values for both
devices, a better indicator of the passivation performance of
the new architecture is the Eg–VOC loss parameter. The Ref
device presents a Eg–VOC value of 728 mV and the PPS device
of 692 mV, with the difference between those being 36 mV, fur-
ther highlighting the passivation improvement through the addi-
tion of the novel architecture; yet it represents a moderate
increase, considering that VOC improvements above 100 mV
have been obtained in the past, likely meaning that this structure
still needs optimization in the passivation part as well, for exam-
ple, by increasing the passivation area from 77% to values above
90%.[20,27] The effective NPs introduction within the dielectric
passivation scheme with improved JSC and VOC values in com-
parison to a reference shows the potential of using plasmonic
architectures to further enhance the performance of ultrathin
devices and is a result of the combined passivation and light-
trapping strategies. In addition, this approach is transversal
for several solar cell technologies, in which the use of passivation
and light-trapping approaches has been explored.[16,37,97–101]
3. Conclusion
The obtained results show the viability of coupling plasmonic
scattering with rear dielectric passivation schemes to shorten
the efﬁciency gap between thin and ultrathin CIGS-based devi-
ces. Through the optimization of a microﬂuidic procedure to
deposit individualized 105 nm colloidal Au NPs, and a nanoli-
thography process to encapsulate these on an Al2O3 dielectric
scheme, a plasmonic and passivated substrate was developed that
allowed for optical and electrical improvements. A 3.7 mA cm/C02
enhancement of the JSC value was obtained for the plasmonic
and passivated substrate in comparison to a reference device,
notably without degradation of the VOC value, for which even
a 23 mV improvement was measured. The improved optoelec-
tronic performance was attributed to an enhanced rear
reﬂectance and scattering, as well as, an improved charge carrier
collection and rear surface recombination velocity value decrease,
shown through optical and electrical simulations. Overall,
an almost 2% light-to-power conversion efﬁciency value enhance-
ment was obtained for the developed plasmonic and
passivated architecture.
4. Experimental Section
Nanoparticles Solution Preparation: The synthesis of Au NP seeds
followed the Turkevitch method.[71] A 200.5 mL aqueous solution of chlor-
oauric acid (HAuCl4) with a 0.25 mM concentration was heated to its boil-
ing point. Upon boiling, a 20.1 mL solution of a previously warmed 40 mM
sodium citrate tribasic dihydrate was quickly added. The entire process
involved continuous heating and magnetic stirring. Instantly, the solution
became transparent, and in a period between 15 to 20 min, it gradually
transformed into a red wine color, indicating completion. The Au NP seeds
were subsequently grown using a hydroxylamine seeding method.[67]
A solution was prepared by combining 0.67 mL of an 80 mM aqueous
hydroxylamine–hydrochloride solution with 60.15 mL of an aqueous
0.25 mM HAuCl4 solution. After 15 min of magnetic stirring, 0.38 mL of
seed solution was added to grow the NPs to the nominal target between
100 and 120 nm. The solution was kept under constant stirring until a
cloudy brown color was achieved. The grown Au NPs were stabilized
by adding 0.9 mL of a 40 mM aqueous sodium citrate solution. The
NPs were washed through centrifugation at 3500 rpm for 15 min and
redispersed in milli-q water.
PDMS Device Preparation: To produce microﬂuidic devices, the
development of a Si device master mold was ﬁrst required. The device
was designed to ensure a uniform solution injection and avoid bubble
formation, by implementing multiple uniformly spaced microﬂuidic chan-
nels, as shown in Figure 11.
For the master mold, a Si wafer was coated with 2.2 μm-thick AZ P4110
resist coating through spin coating in a Karl Suss Optical Track system.
Afterward, the device design was imprinted onto the coated Si wafer using
a Heidelberg DWL 2000. Subsequently, the regions exposed to the design
underwent development with AZ400K. To etch the Si wafer into the desired
structure, a RIE process using a SF6 and C4F8 plasma chemistry with a
SPTS Pegasus system was performed. For the device production, a mixture
of PDMS and a crosslinker, Sylgard 184 elastomer curing agent, was pre-
pared in a 10:1 ratio, resulting in a uniform gel. The PDMS gel was then
poured into the Si master mold. To ensure consistent gel coverage, the Si
master mold and PDMS system were subjected to a vacuum environment
within a desiccator. The Si master mold and PDMS assembly underwent
curing at 65 °C for 2 h, following which the device was separated from the
Si master mold.
