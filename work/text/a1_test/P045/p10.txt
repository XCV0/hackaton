evanescent electric ﬁeld is present in the NP vicinity, which is an
indication of a near-ﬁeld localization effect that leads to a local
light absorption enhancement. A clear difference between the
Ref and PPS device electric ﬁelds proﬁle is the absence of
well-deﬁned interference fringes in the absorber, such effect
is a result of the far-ﬁeld scattering inherent to the 105 nm metal-
lic NPs.[48,88] In addition, there are areas with a higher electric
ﬁeld intensity value within the absorber bulk as compared to
the Ref device, which may have resulted from the coupling
between an enhanced reﬂectance from the developed substrate
due to the dielectric layer, with a total internal reﬂection effect
due to the large-angle scattering promoted by the metallic
NPs.[36,41,48] To decouple the total JSC optical gain, a passivated
solar cell with a 25 nm Al2O3 layer was simulated and a
0.9 mA cm/C02 JSC gain over the Ref architecture was found, mean-
ing that in the developed PPS device, a 0.4 mA cm/C02 enhance-
ment is expected from the metallic NPs. Nevertheless, the
proposed concept can still be further optimized, as an example,
the NPs surface coverage can be increased to allow for an
improved light interaction. The choice of dielectric layer within
the PPS architecture is also fundamental to achieving the best
scattering efﬁciency, as the resonant wavelength can be tuned
further to a wavelength region where optical losses are predomi-
nant in an ultrathin device.[17] However, the dielectric layer has to
fulﬁl several requirements, including an effective passivation
effect and diffusion barrier capabilities, both of which have been
successfully and repeatedly demonstrated by the selected Al2O3
passivation scheme in ultrathin CIGS-based devices.[26,27,47,54,56]
In Figure 9d, the Qscat of 105 nm Au NPs in a ACIGS medium,
with and without a 15 nm Al2O3 shell, are presented. The reso-
nant peaks of a Au NP without encapsulation have the highest
Qscat values and those are on the desired NIR wavelength range.
However, the encapsulation with an Al2O3 layer leads to signiﬁ-
cant changes in the NP plasmonic behavior, more speciﬁcally a
blueshift of the resonant peaks along with a dampening of the
Qscat, due to the encapsulation with a low-refractive-index dielec-
tric (n = 1.8 at 550 nm).[58] A promising alternative material for
the PPS architecture to provide further optical improvements is
TiO2 with a refractive index of 2.4 at 550 nm, which has been
recently exploited as an effective passivation layer for ultrathin
CIGS-based technology with a signiﬁcant efﬁciency improve-
ment[33] and has been previously used as a diffusion barrier
for metals.[89–91] By encapsulating the metallic structures with
TiO2, the resonant peak redshifts in comparison to the ones from
the NP with an Al2O3 shell, and an improved Qscat is obtained, as
shown in Figure 9d. In Figure 9a, the simulated ACIGS absor-
bance spectra for the solar cell with the Au NPs encapsulated by
TiO2 with a 17% surface coverage are compared to the simulated
absorbance of the Ref and PPS devices, along with the respective
JSC value. With the proposed optimization, a JSC value of
28.5 mA cm/C02 can be achieved due to an improved absorbance
for wavelength values higher than 800 nm. However, at around
800 and 950 nm, the previous architecture presents a slightly
higher ACIGS absorbance value. This is a consequence of the
encapsulating layer modiﬁcation in the ultrathin architecture
which has a negative impact in the rear interface reﬂectance,
as the refractive index is closer to the ACIGS one. Thus, it is
important to decouple the specular reﬂectance and scattering
gains in the presented architectures. In the developed PPS
architecture, the NPs scattering contributed with 0.4 mA cm/C02,
while the rear reﬂectance contribution allowed for a JSC gain
of around 0.9 mA cm/C02. Whereas with TiO2 and improved sur-
face coverage the reﬂectance contribution is of 0.7 with
1mAcm/C02 arising from the Au metallic NPs scattering. The pro-
posed concepts effectively led to a scattering improvement
although with a decrease in the rear reﬂectance. Thus, it is
expected to attain a better performance if this plasmonic strategy
is coupled with a metallic mirror, which would further increase
rear reﬂectance and provide an additional scattering beneﬁtas
the light backscattered by the NPs would be redirected to the
absorber layer.[37,53] Overall, the optoelectronic performance pro-
vided by the PPS architecture and the optical improvements
expected through slight architecture optimizations show a path-
way for the use of metallic NPs within ultrathin solar cells to
compensate incomplete absorption losses and shorten the gap
to the thin-ﬁlm counterpart.
2.6. Optoelectronic Performance
The developed devices’, Figure 10a, illuminated and dark repre-
sentative current density–voltage ( J–V ) characteristic curves are
presented in Figure 10b, as well as the best cell illuminated
curves. The ﬁgures of merit average and best cell values obtained
from the J–V curves are presented in Table 1, along with the JSC
values measured through the integration of the external quan-
tum efﬁciency (EQE) spectra, Figure 10c, multiplied by the
AM1.5 G solar spectrum. From the representative J–V curve
analysis, there is a notable difference between the Ref and
PPS curves, as the Ref shows signs of shunting. The shunting
in the Ref device is reﬂected in the lower Rp value, such effect
has been reported for ultrathin devices due to the possible for-
mation of pinholes.[35,54] On the other hand, passivated ultrathin
solar cells usually report Rp improvement due to the reduced
interface conductive area, preventing shunting paths.[22,23,30,92,93]
The Rp enhancement for the novel architecture might explain the
slightly higher ﬁll factor (FF) value for the PPS device. Despite a
77% contact area reduction, the integration of the line patterned
dielectric layer does not show a signiﬁcant impact on the Rs
value, which follows previously reported results.[94,95] In addi-
tion, for all ﬁgures of merit for the Ref, there is a large standard
deviation in comparison to the PPS device, meaning that the new
architecture improves the uniformity. The increase in uniformity
may be a consequence of the passivation architecture to decrease
the shunting paths in the PPS solar cells, as some cells in the Ref
device show signs of shunting, presenting a large discrepancy in
all ﬁgures of merit. Overall, the novel architecture led to an
improvement of the light-to-power conversion efﬁciency (η) value
of almost 2%, mostly standing from the 3.7 mA cm/C02 JSC value
increase. The JSC enhancement with the new architecture was
predicted through optical simulations, with an expected improve-
ment of 1.3 mA cm/C02 from the Ref to the PPS device. Thus, there
