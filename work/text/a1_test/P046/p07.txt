To chemically analyze the surface before the water rinse,
synchrotron-based X-ray photoemission electron microscopy
(XPEEM) measurements were conducted on the sample with
20 nm RbF-PDT (same sample as in Figure 4). This technique
allows to probe the chemical species in only the upper ≈2nm
of the sample surface (excitation photon energies = 100–455 eV)
and to map the elemental composition with a lateral resolution
in the order of tens of nanometers. Figure 5a shows again
an SEM image of the sample surface for comparison, while
Figure 5b illustrates the XPEEM maps for the absorber elements
(Ga3d, In4d, Se3d, Ag3d) and Rb (3d) for a selected region of the
measurement (different area as compared to SEM image). The
entire scanned area can be found, together with a corresponding
low-energy electron microscopy image, in Figure S5 in the sup-
porting information. Due to differences in the photoelectron
kinetic energies of 1s lines, fluorine and oxygen could not be mea-
sured in the emission mode at the same probing depth. Clearly,
regions of locally increased elemental concentrations are observed,
very likely indicating the presence of the particles visible in SEM.
Interestingly, the Ga and In signal intensities are anti-correlated,
as highlighted in the overlay image in Figure 5b and in the
2-dimensional histogram in Figure 5c. A comparison of the pho-
toemission spectra of photoelectrons originating from a Ga-rich
area (region B in Figure 5b) with Ga-poor regions, as presented
in Figure S6 (a,b) in the supporting information, reveals that
the Ga agglomerates do not contain any Indium at all (In4d peak
not detected). In contrast, the regions without Ga-rich particles
contain mainly In with only a minor amount of Ga. We suggest
that the Ga-enriched areas are either GaF, Rb-Ga-F, or GaOx,
maybe with contributions of Se. Rubidium can be found every-
where on the surface, suggesting that beside the particles observed
in SEM, a (possibly very thin) Rb-compound with strongly reduced
Ga content formed, see also Figure S6(c,d) in the supporting infor-
mation. We propose that these are regions where a thin Rb-In-Se
surface layer is exposed, which is supported by complementing
analysis later in the manuscript.
