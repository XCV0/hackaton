on a pure ternary CuGaSe2 (CGS) absorber, a HA-PDT did not lead
to a significant increase in performance [15, 30, 31]. It was pro-
posed that the absence of a HA-In-Se (no indium in CuGaSe2)sur-
face phase [15] or the formation of more detrimental interface
defects [31] prevent any beneficial effect. However, a small VOC
gain was observed when RbF was added during the final stage
of absorber formation and not as a PDT [32]. Zahedi-Azad et
al. showed that a HA-PDT can lead to an improved VOC and effi-
ciency for Cu(In,Ga)Se2 (CIGS) solar cells with a [Ga]/([Ga]+[In])
ratio (GGI) up to 0.8 [33, 34]. They claim that while recombina-
tion at the CIGS/CdS interface is still limiting, it can be signifi-
cantly mitigated by the HA-PDT. However, for too large amounts
of (HA)F a transport barrier at the front contact was claimed to
lead to FF losses [33]. More recently, we tested a RbF-PDT for
(Ag,Cu) (In,Ga)Se2 (ACIGS) solar cells with a GGI ≈ 0.7 and
a [Ag]/([Ag]+[Cu]) ratio (AAC) of 0.5–0.7 and found a statisti-
cally significant VOC increase, leading to a top efficiency of
16.3% [35]. Photoelectron-spectroscopy measurements indicate
that CIGS surfaces with a high GGI are less modified by a
HA-PDT and no Rb-In-Se surface layer was detected after
RbF-PDT on CIGS with GGI = 0.9 [36]. All aforementioned
works are summarized in Table 1.
Overall, it can be concluded that, according to the previous inves-
tigations, the absorber should contain a sufficient (minimum)
amount of indium to utilize any beneficial effect of a HA-
PDT. In addition, similar to low-gap absorbers [37–40], the
(HA)F dose can also become too large for wide-gap CIGS films,
leading to FF deterioration [30, 33, 35].
In the present work, we investigate the effect of a RbF-PDT for Cu-
free Ag(In,Ga)Se2 (AIGS, i.e., AAC = 1) solar cells with a GGI of 0.5,
leading to EG ≈ 1.5 eV. As compared to previous works on wide-gap
ACIGS solar cells [35, 41–50], the Cu was removed altogether to
reach the same bandgap, but with a lower Ga content [51]. The
intention is that the lower GGI improves the absorber bulk proper-
ties (i.e., electron lifetime), as observed for Ag-free CIGS solar
cells [52–63]. Since a positive conduction-band offset (CBO) of
about + 0.35 eV is predicted at the CdS/AIGS interface for the used
absorber composition [42], interface recombination is expected to
play only a minor role, but a slightly detrimental effect on FF
may occur [64]. Up to this point, the total exclusion of Cu to process
wide-gap AIGS solar cells was barely tested before and, to the best of
our knowledge, only for GGI ≥ 0.8 and without HA-PDT [65–69].
To use a wide-gap A(C)IGS device as a top cell in a tandem struc-
ture, the back-contact material needs to be transparent. In the
past, different transparent conductive oxides were tested and a
concise summary of most works can be found in [48, 49]. A good
compromise between a sufficiently low sheet resistance (Rsheet)
and a high near-infrared (NIR) transparency in the final device
was achieved by using an In2O3:W (IOW) back contact [49, 50].
Thus, in this work we compare the effect of a RbF-PDT for AIGS
solar cells with standard, opaque Mo to devices with transparent
IOW back contacts, both subjected to the exact same absorber
deposition runs. In addition, a reduction of the standard CdS
thickness was evaluated for each sample, too.
