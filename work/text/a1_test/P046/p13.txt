Electronics) on the same samples, using monochromatic Al Kα
(1.487 keV) X-ray radiation and charge compensation. In this
way, also the fluorine signal could be measured.
Integral absorber compositions were determined with an XRF
spectrometer Epsilon 5 (Panalytical). Glow-discharge optical
emission spectroscopy was conducted in a Spectruma Analytik
GDA 750HR system to measure the alkali and GGI depth-profiles
of the complete solar cell stacks with Mo back contacts. While no
quantification was possible for Na and Rb, the GGI was quanti-
fied by cross-calibration to the XRF-deduced compositions.
The sheet resistance of the electrodes was measured in a linear
4-point probe configuration.
4.3 | Electro-optical characterization of solar cells
The EQE (only best cell each) and JV (all cells per sample) meas-
urements of completed solar cells were conducted in home-built
setups. To avoid any reflection from the stage surfaces, a highly
absorbing, black sponge was placed underneath the solar cells
with IOW. The JV characteristics were measured in forward
V-sweep at a temperature of 25°C under illumination by a class
AAA solar simulator from G2V (Pico), using light emitting diode
(LED) arrays. The light intensity was adjusted to match the JSC
value at 1 sun irradiance of a calibrated Si reference solar cell.
Capacitance–voltage profiling was conducted from V = −0.4
to + 0.3 V at 60 kHz and an amplitude of 25 mV, using an
Agilent 4284A precision LCR meter and a Keithley 2401 source
meter. A dielectric constant of εr = 10 was assumed for the AIGS
material in this study.
