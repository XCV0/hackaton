To also quantify overall concentration changes of the absorber
elements as well as of fluorine and rubidium after the water rinse,
conventional X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was per-
formed on the samples shown in Figure 4 and 5. Figure 6 illus-
trates the corresponding XPS spectra for the AIGS sample with
20 nm RbF-PDT (Mo back contact) before and after water rinse.
Generally, the changes in surface chemistry by the water rinse
resemble what we observed before for Cu-containing, wide-gap
ACIGS absorbers [35]. The fluorine and sodium signals vanish,
the Rb intensity is strongly reduced and a peak at the binding
energy (Eb)of≈21 eV disappears. All other signals, stemming from
the chalcopyrite AIGS absorber, are increased after the water rinse.
This indicates that before the water rinse the measurement is
dominated by the presence of water-soluble Rb- and F- (and to
a minor extent Na-) containing compounds. In addition, the van-
ishing of the peak at Eb ≈ 21 eV, which can be assigned to a X-Ga-F
compound (with X being Rb and/or O, C, H, Na or nothing), sug-
gests that at least some of the water-soluble particles also contain a
substantial concentration of Ga. This is in line with the detection
of the Ga-enriched and In-free particles in the XPEEM maps
before the water rinse (see Figure 5b) and should result in a
Ga depletion at the very surface of the absorber in the final device,
that is, after CdS deposition. On the first sight, it may appear less
coherent that the XPS measurements do not reproduce the Rb-Se
correlation observed in the XPEEM maps, which suggest the pres-
ence of water-soluble Rb-Se (-X) compounds. In fact, the Se3d
XPS-signal is significantly increasing after the water rinse. A pos-
sible explanation may be that the non-rinsed surface is mainly cov-
ered by Se-free (or Se-poor) particles like GaF and RbF (or
corresponding mixed compounds) and less so by Rb-Se (-X) par-
ticles. This would indeed lead to the observed increase of the Se (as
well as In and Ag) XPS-signal after the water rinse, mainly stem-
ming from the Rb-In-Se surface phase and the underlying (or
locally exposed) AIGS absorber. Generally, the differences in
