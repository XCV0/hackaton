the severe FF losses alone, considering the results from earlier
publications.
Advanced material characterization reveals that the RbF-PDT-
induced absorber modifications are very similar for AIGS and
CIGS absorbers: The formation of a Rb-In-Se surface phase
(likely RbInSe2) is detected, as well as a weak Cd in-diffusion into
the same. It is suggested that the large conduction band offset at
the potential RbInSe2/AIGS interface causes an additional loss in
fill factor. Otherwise, Rb is mainly located in group-I depleted
and In-enriched grain boundaries and at the back interface.
However, most grain boundaries are also found to be Ag-depleted
and In-enriched without RbF-PDT.
We further propose that the desired VOC boost is absent for AIGS
solar cells, since the Rb incorporation does not lead to a doping
increase for close-stoichiometric Cu-free absorbers, which
remain fully depleted, independent of a PDT step. The possible
explanation may be the higher alkali solubility in the chalcopy-
rite lattice. A reduced interface recombination by the RbF-PDT
was not expected anyhow, due to a favorable band alignment of
the untreated AIGS absorber with the CdS buffer layer. Overall,
this work shows that a RbF-PDT is not suitable for wide-gap
AIGS solar cells, especially when a transparent back contact
is used.
Lastly, the strategy to improve the minority carrier lifetime,
by entirely replacing Cu by Ag to reduce the Ga content
(GGI = 0.5 in this work) for a given bandgap, failed. As compared
to Cu-containing ACIGS devices with the same EG, the AIGS
solar cells from this study show similar VOC and JSC, but lower
FF values. This finding indicates that the high Ga content itself is
not the main reason for the large VOC deficit of wide-gap ACIGS
solar cells. However, while no beneficial effect is observed at
front illumination, the collection at rear illumination may be
slightly improved as compared to ACIGS devices with same
bandgap, reaching up to 72% JSC bifaciality for the best cell with
an IOW back contact (η = 11.6% at front illumination).
In summary, removing Cu from ACIGS absorbers altogether does
not seem to be a viable route to enhance the efficiency of corre-
sponding wide-gap solar cells for use as a top cell in tandem devi-
ces. Additionally applying a RbF-PDT makes the situation even
worse.
4 | Experimental Section
4.1 | Solar cell processing
A stack of SLG/(Mo or IOW)/NaF/AIGS/CdS/ZnO:Al was
used for the solar cell samples in this work. The opaque Mo
back contact (320 nm, Rsheet ≈ 0.7 Ω sq−1) was direct-current
sputtered, while IOW (300-380 nm, Rsheet ≈ 10 Ω sq−1)was
radio-frequency sputtered from an In2O3-WO3 compound target
(nominally 1 wt.% WO3)inanAr(40sccm)andO2 (0.4 sccm)
gas mixture. Sodium in-diffusion from the SLG was allowed
(no alkali diffusion-barrier), but additionally 7 nm- (for IOW)
or 15 nm-thick (for Mo) NaF precursor layers were coated on
top of the back electrodes via thermal evaporation. Next, a
three-stage (Ag-poor → Ag-rich → Ag-poor) co-evaporation
process was applied to grow ≈1.1 μmthickAIGSfilmsata
maximum substrate temperature of T = 550°C (≈3min at
T > 500°C). Higher Ga and lower In rates in the initial growth
stages led to the formation of a bandgap grading toward the
back contact (see also Figure S10 in the supporting informa-
tion). For samples subjected to a RbF-PDT, the metal sources
were then switched off and RbF was deposited at T ≈ 350°C
for different times at a rate of ≈1 nm/min in Se-containing
atmosphere. In total, 4 AIGS depositions with different RbF
doses (0, 5, 10, and 20 nm) were done (see integral compositions
in Table 2), each containing SLG/Mo and SLG/IOW samples.
Subsequently, the samples were cut in two halves, which were
then coated with a CdS buffer layer of either the standard
(≈65 nm) or a reduced (≈35 nm) thickness in a CBD process
at 60°C for 495 s or 330 s, respectively. Slight deviations in
CdS thickness (±5 nm) between individual depositions cannot
be ruled out, potentially leading to minor variations in the
EQE spectra at short wavelengths. Finally, a ZnO:Al (270 nm,
Rsheet ≈ 50 Ω/sq) window layer was sputtered on top. In the final
step, the samples with Mo were sectioned into 14 individual
solar cells (area = 0.05 cm2) by mechanical scribing, while for
samples with IOW 16 individual solar cells (area = 0.1 cm2)
were created via local, selective removal of the buffer and win-
dow layers (etching in HCl), allowed by a photolithography
masking process.
4.2 | Material characterization
A Zeiss Merlin SEM was used to illustrate the absorber surfaces
subjected to a RbF-PDT before and after rinsing it in deionized
water for 140 s. STEM and EDX analyses were performed on a
FEI Titan Themis 200 XFEG instrument equipped with a SuperX
detector G2 and operated at 200 kV. The TEM lamellae were pre-
pared via a Ga-based focused ion beam in a Crossbeam 550 Zeiss
system, following a standard lift-out technique. A final milling
step at maximum 2 kV ion accelerating voltage was performed
on both sides of the lamellae, and no further electron exposure
was done during focused ion beam preparation before the TEM
analyses.
To laterally map the chemical structure of the AIGS surface sub-
jected to 20 nm RbF-PDT, X-ray photoemission electron micros-
copy and low-energy electron microscopy measurements were
performed at the MAXPEEM synchrotron beamline at MAX
IV [92] on samples before and after water rinse. The XPEEM
mapping images were acquired with photon energies between
100 and 455 eV, leading to a constant kinetic energy of excited
photoelectrons of ≈85 eV. Representative high-resolution core
level spectra of absorber elements Ga3d, In4d, Se3d, and Rb3d,
were extracted and used for quantitative analysis. The as-
deposited samples were packed in a N2 atmosphere to minimize
contamination during transport to the beamline and the meas-
urements were carried out at room temperature and under vac-
uum conditions. A brief thermal annealing step (5 min at 100°C)
was conducted prior to XPEEM measurements, to remove ad-
sorbed H2O from air exposure during sample mounting.
