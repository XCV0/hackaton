VOC and FF rather improved slightly with the thinner CdS buffer,
while JSC even increased by about 1–2 mA/cm2, due to the
reduced parasitic absorption. In a previous works, we found that
our CdS deposition process leads to closed films on low-gap CIGS
absorbers for average layer thicknesses ≥30 nm, leading to no
more VOC, but a continuous FF increase for thicker films up
to the standard thickness [81]. At this point, we cannot explain
why the thinner buffer layer is clearly advantageous for the per-
formance of the AIGS solar cells analyzed in this study. In the
future, we plan to further reduce the CdS thickness to identify
the optimum configuration.
Overall, the performance level of the AIGS cells with a Mo back
contact and without HA-PDT is similar to samples which include
Cu and have the same bandgap [41, 48, 49]. Table 3 compares the
best cells from this study (AAC = 1, GGI = 0.5) with the best Cu-
containing cell from our earlier work (AAC = 0.8, GGI = 0.6) [49].
For the standard CdS thickness, the sample with Cu shows a higher
FF, but very comparable VOC and JSC values. The reason for the
lower FF for the AIGS sample in this study may be the more
pronounced CBO at the CdS interface (≈0.35 eV vs ≈0.25 eV
[42]).Insummary,reducingtheGacontentfromGGI= 0.6 to
GGI = 0.5 for a fixed bandgap of 1.46–1.47 eV by increasing the
AAC to 1 did not result in an improved cell performance. This
questions the hypothesis that the deep GaI defect is responsible
for the large VOC deficit of wide-gap ACIGS solar cells [55–61].
Light soaking is often seen to improve the performance of (HA)F-
treated ACIGS solar cells [1, 82, 83]. However, none of the samples
in this work (with or without PDT) improved by light soaking, but
instead even showed VOC degradation after 4 weeks of storage in
ambient conditions. It is therefore advised to limit the AAC value
to ≤0.5 and accept a slightly higher GGI value for a given bandgap,
which was also shown to minimize metastability issues [70, 71].
On the contrary, the thermodynamical stability of the absorber
(i.e., also of the final cell) is predicted to decrease with increasing
Ga content and AAC values close to 0.5 [84]. Thus, an AAC ≈ 0.4
may be optimal, still allowing for a sufficiently wide space charge
region for close-stoichiometric absorbers, while being more stable
and potentially gaining from a RbF-PDT.
