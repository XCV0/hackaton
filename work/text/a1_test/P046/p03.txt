In each run, soda-lime glass (SLG) substrates coated with Mo or
IOW were added and sodium was provided by a NaF-precursor
film on top of the back contact. In the case of the transparent back
contact (TBC), the NaF addition was chosen to be lower (7 nm) as
compared to Mo (15 nm), since earlier works suggest that a NaF
thickness of ≥10 nm leads to excessive GaOx formation at the
absorber/TBC interface, resulting in FF losses [48]. To study a
potential reduction of the CdS thickness without performance
losses, as enabled by the HA-PDT for low-gap (A)CIGS solar cells,
two different CdS buffer layer thicknesses, a standard ≈65 nm
thick (‘std’)anda≈35 nm thick (‘thin’) film, are compared for each
sample.
An overview of all samples is provided in Table 2, which lists the
corresponding integral compositions (I/III = [Ag]/ ([Ga]+[In]))
and absorber layer thicknesses (dabs), as deduced from
X-ray fluorescence (XRF) measurements, as well as the
bandgap energies, as extracted from external quantum effi-
ciency (EQE) measurements (inflection point method). As is
evident from the table, the absorber properties are very similar
in terms of composition and thickness for all samples, allowing
a fair evaluation of the effect of the RbF-PDT. Intentionally,
very close-stoichiometric AIGS compositions were targeted,
since for absorbers with AAC ≥ 0.5, I/III values >0.90 lead to
a significant widening of the space charge region, resulting
in adequate carrier collection, despite the low diffusion length
in wide-gap chalcopyrite material [35, 41, 44, 70, 71]. A dabs
value of ≈1.1 μm was previously identified to be optimal for
maximizing the efficiency under rear illumination, without
sacrificing too much of the performance level at front illumi-
nation [49, 50].

It is obvious that the efficiency is monotonously decreasing with
increasing RbF dose for all samples. The most pronounced loss is
a reduction in FF, indicating the creation of a transport barrier. It
was suggested that the excessive formation of an HA-In-Se phase
at the absorber surface (like RbInSe2) may be responsible for FF
losses upon too intense HA-PDTs [8, 39, 40, 72]. Since the exclu-
sion of Cu in the used AIGS film leads to a significant downshift
of the conduction band minimum [42], an even larger CBO
would be expected at a potential RbInSe2/AIGS interface, leading
to a more detrimental transport barrier. Alternatively, it was pro-
posed that when too many Na atoms are replaced by heavier
alkalis at the back contact interface, a significant back contact
barrier establishes, explaining the FF losses [26, 73].

(not if based on SnO2 [48]), which is often explained by the for-
