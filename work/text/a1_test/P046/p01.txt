ABSTRACT
This study investigates the impact of a RbF post-deposition treatment (PDT) on the properties of Cu-free, wide-gap (EG = 1.5 eV)
Ag(In,Ga)Se2 (AIGS) solar cells with either opaque Mo or transparent In2O3:W back contacts. The formation of a Rb-In-Se surface
phase and Cd in-diffusion from the buffer into this layer are detected. In addition, Rb is mainly located in Ag-depleted and In-
enriched grain boundaries and at the back interface. The RbF-PDT leads to decreased fill factor (FF) values, without providing any
gain in open-circuit voltage (VOC). This FF deterioration is more pronounced when a In2O3:W back contact is used. It is suggested
that transport barriers form at the Rb-In-Se/AIGS front and at the AIGS/GaOx/In2O3:W back interfaces after RbF-PDT. In addi-
tion, the GaOx thickness at the rear electrode increases. The absence of a VOC boost may be explained by the missing doping
increase after the RbF-PDT. Lastly, the AIGS solar cells from this study show slightly lower efficiencies as compared to Cu-
containing devices with the same EG but higher Ga content. However, the collection at rear illumination is marginally improved,
reaching up to 72% bifaciality in short-circuit current for the best cell with an In2O3:W back contact.
1 | Introduction
Today, the incorporation of the heavy alkali (HA) elements potas-
sium, rubidium, or cesium into low-gap (Agx,Cu1−x)(Iny,Ga1−y)(Sz,
Se1−z)2-based absorbers is standard for solar cell applications, since
highest efficiencies (η > 22%) cannot be reached if only sodium
(mandatory for decent performance) is added [1–3]. In most cases,
the HA species is supplied in the form of evaporated (HA)F in a
post absorber deposition treatment (HA-PDT), with the first report
of the beneficial effect of a potassium fluoride-post-deposition
treatment (KF-PDT) dating back to 2013 [4]. It was observed that
the HA-PDT allows growing substantially thinner CdS buffer
layers without losses in open-circuit voltage (VOC)orfillfactor
(FF), resulting in higher short-circuit current densities (JSC).
Possible explanations are a facilitated Cd in-diffusion (creating
type inversion) [4, 5] and an earlier formation of a fully covering
CdS layer during chemical bath deposition (CBD) [6, 7]. Typically,
the HA-PDT also leads to higher VOC values, but the underlying
physical mechanism is still under debate [3, 8–11]. Prevalent sug-
gestions are that, after HA-PDT, the recombination rates are
reduced i) at the interface to the buffer by the formation of a very
thin, wide-gap (Alkali,Cu)-(In,Ga)-Se surface layer (possibly with-
out Ga and Cu) [12–17], ii) at grain boundaries via a reduction/
passivation of charged defects [18–20], or iii) in the grain interiors
due to a reduced concentration of deep defects [21, 22](deemed
unlikely by others [18]). Alternative explanations are an increased
doping density [23–25] (but lower doping was observed as well
[26]) and reduced potential fluctuations [27].
