is deemed unlikely, since the RbF-PDT is done at a temperature
of 350°C (duration of ≈14 min for 20 nm RbF), while GaOx was
found to form only for temperatures ≥390°C [78, 79]. Generally,
it cannot be excluded that the total alkali concentration is just too
high after HA-PDT when an IOW back contact is used, since it

back into the SLG (in stark contrast to Mo). To clarify some
of these questions, the Rb distribution and interface modifica-
tions after RbF-PDT are illustrated and discussed later in
Section 2.2.
Only in the case of 20 nm RbF and for an IOW back contact does
the PDT-induced photocurrent blocking also result in a decreased
JSC, expressed by a constant reduction of the EQE level (see
Figure 1d). For all other samples, the EQE is not significantly
affected by the RbF-PDT or the choice of the back contact. The
reflectance (R) spectra for the samples with 10 nm RbF and Mo
are added in Figure 1c,d and S1(c,d) for full devices with thin
and standard CdS layers, respectively. Very good carrier collection
is observed for all samples, facilitated by the electrical field in the
fully depleted absorber films (see capacitance–voltage (CV) meas-
urements in Figure S2 in supporting information). Remaining
losses can be mainly attributed to parasitic absorption in the
ZnO:Al front electrode and, to a lesser extent, to transmission
losses by incomplete absorption in the AIGS film as well as absorp-
tion in ordered vacancy compounds (OVCs) at the absorber sur-
face [80]. When comparing the EQE spectra of samples with thin
and standard CdS (Figure 1c,d vs Figure S1(c,d)) it may appear as if
the collection in the NIR region is reduced in the latter case.
However, the differences in EQE in the NIR region are mainly
arising from differences in reflection losses (1-R)andR-corrected
EQE spectra, as presented in Figure S3 in supporting information
for the sample with Mo and 10 nm RbF, only deviate in the spec-
tral region where CdS absorbs.
Figure 2 illustrates the trends in JV parameters with increasing
RbF dose for the best cells of each sample (values in Table 1). It is
obvious that VOC is not improved by the RbF-PDT for any of the
devices. In the case of a Mo back contact, it stays at the reference
level up to 10 nm RbF and is then reduced for the highest dose.
When IOW is used, VOC deteriorates already for the smallest RbF
dose and then monotonously decreases further. We speculate
that the loss in VOC and the creation of the transport barrier share
the same origin. The trends in FF are similar to the ones in VOC,
while the situation for JSC,qe was already discussed earlier.
Overall, this leads to a monotonous decline in efficiency with
increasing RbF dose for all samples. The efficiency of the best
cells with an IOW back contact decline from ≈12% to ≈2% when
applying the most intense RbF-PDT. Clearly, a RbF-PDT is not
suitable for AIGS solar cells, in contrast to Cu-containing ACIGS
devices with a similar bandgap [35]. This indicates that the pres-
ence of Cu in the absorber plays a crucial role for the effective-
ness of an HA-PDT. However, the results also suggest that an
HA-PDT may generally be detrimental when a TBC is used in
combination with a wide-gap absorber, which needs to be con-
firmed in future works. In contrast, for low-gap CIGS solar cells
grown at temperatures ≤ 400°C (i.e., forming very little to no
GaOx) with an In2O3:Sn TBC, the application of a RbF-PDT
was shown to provide very good performance [78].
