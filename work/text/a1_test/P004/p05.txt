Various optimization steps were carried out to improve the initial eﬃciency. To re-
duce the contact resistance between Ag and a-Si:H, the solar cell layer system was supple-
mented with an aluminum-doped zinc oxide (Al:ZnO) layer. The 200 nm thin Al:ZnO was 
deposited by atomic layer deposition (ALD) on top of the silver layer. Furthermore, the 
substrate temperature during the a-Si:H deposition was increased from 180 °C to 225 °C, 
which reduces the defect density in the absorber. Additionally, the thickness of the intrin-
sic a-Si:H layer was increased from 300 nm to 500 nm to enhance the optical absorption. 
Also, the thickness of the n + doped layer was increased from 10 nm to 20 nm, as this is 
expected to reduce the density of micro-holes in this layer. With regard to the thickness of 
the ITO layer, a compromise had to be found between the low optical absorption and high 
electrical conductivity. A layer thickness of 200 nm was determined to be optimal and 
homogenous [34]. 
The solar cell layer system optimized in this way was optically characterized in the 
UV-VIS-NIR spectral region, as shown in Figure 5a. The measured transmission (T, red) 
and reﬂection (R, blue) values are given in the same way as the calculated (T + R + A = 1) 
absorption values (A, black). For the maximum of the sunlight spectrum at about 550 nm 
wavelength, more than 90% is absorbed in the solar cell layer system. 
The mentioned improvements lead to solar cells with J–V characteristics shown in 
both dashed lines in Figure 5 b. The open-circuit voltages are 860–863 mV and the short-
circuit current densities are 4.9–6.0 mA/cm2. These solar cells had eﬃciencies of 1.9–2.3% 
with ﬁll factors of 44.5–45.2%.
