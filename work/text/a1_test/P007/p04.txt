The photostability of the as-  deposited high bandgap CsPbI 2Br 
perovskite films was assessed through hyperspectral photolu -
minescence (PL) measurements under continuous 1-  Sun equiv-
alent illumination using an excitation wavelength of 405 nm 
for unencapsulated samples in air. The PL spectra exhibited no 
secondary peak over a continuous illumination period probed 
over 180 min, as depicted in Figure  2a. The PL spectra remain 
largely unchanged after 60 min of light soaking. For brevity, the 
PL spectra after 60 and 180 min of light soaking are also shown 
in Figure  S4a. Additionally, spatially resolved 2D hyperspec -
tral- PL images before and after 3 h of light soaking are presented 
in Figure  2b. Figure  S4b further provides a statistical analysis 
of the PL peak positions across the entire measured wavelength 
range. The uniform distribution of PL peak positions before and 
after light soaking, along with the absence of any peak shifts 
or secondary peaks, confirms the absence of halide segregation 
in the high bandgap CsPbI 2Br films. This observation attests to 
the exceptional photostability of these as-  deposited perovskite 
films without the need for additive engineering. Additionally, 
the PL intensity maps before and after light soaking, as shown 
in Figure  2c, indicate uniform emission across the entire ob -
served area.
Beyond photostability, thermal stability of the evaporation de -
posited CsPbI2Br perovskite films was assessed by maintaining 
the samples at 85°C inside an N 2 environment and conducting 
XRD measurements at regular intervals. It was observed that 
after 2 days, the PbI 2 peak at 2θ ≈ 12.7° starts to appear for the 
inorganic perovskite film, which was absent in the fresh sam -
ple as shown in Figure  2d. Over a period of 10 days, the XRD 
analysis of these evaporated perovskite films showed no other 
significant changes, confirming the good thermal stability of the 
evaporated CsPbI2Br perovskite films.
