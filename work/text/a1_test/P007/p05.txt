2.2   |   CsPbI2Br Perovskite- Based Semitransparent 
Single- Junction Solar Cells
To investigate the photovoltaic performance of these evaporated 
CsPbI2Br perovskite films as the top-  cell absorber in a mono -
lithic perovskite/perovskite/silicon triple- junction solar cell, we 
first fabricated semitransparent solar cells. To keep the design 
of single- junction cells similar to the final triple-  junction cells, 
we fabricated our cells on ohmic Si substrates having flat front 
and rear side with 1-  cm2 active area. The CsPbI 2Br perovskite 
absorber thickness was kept very thin, ~250 nm, and the cell 
was designed in a p-  i- n configuration, similar to the top per -
ovskite subcell of a perovskite/perovskite/silicon triple- junction 
device (Figure  S5a). This reduced thickness, combined with 
a high bandgap, is necessary to achieve better current match -
ing between the perovskite top cell and the middle cell [ 43]. 
Evaporated Spiro- TTB and C 60 layers were used as the charge 
transport layers (CTLs) for hole and electron extraction, respec -
tively. Previous studies [44–46] have shown that the perovskite/
C60 interface is a primary source of voltage loss in the high 
bandgap perovskite solar cells, primarily due to high nonradi -
ative recombination losses and poor energy band alignment be -
tween the perovskite absorber and the C 60 layer. To passivate 
the perovskite/C 60 interface, 1 mg/mL F- PEAI (fluoropheneth -
ylammonium iodide) in isopropanol (IPA) was used as depicted 
in Figure  S5b. Apart from this passivation treatment, the rest 
of the device was entirely fabricated using the physical vapor 
deposition (PVD; for perovskite absorber, CTLs and ITO lay -
ers) and the atomic layer deposition (ALD; for SnO x layer) tech -
niques, eliminating the use of any solvents. The implementation 
of F- PEAI passivation treatment at the perovskite/ETL interface 
resulted in an ~96-  mV rise in open-  circuit voltage ( VOC) and 
a 10% increase (absolute) in fill factor ( FF) giving an overall 
increase of around 2% (absolute) in the PCE  of the semitrans -
parent single- junction solar cells as compared to unpassivated 
(control) devices (see Figure  3a). Detailed j- V parameters and 
the F- PEAI concentration optimization are given in Figure  S6. 
The improved cell performance can be attributed to the F- PEAI 
passivation of the front contact stack, which effectively reduced 
nonradiative recombination losses at the perovskite/C 60 inter -
face. This is evidenced by an increase of approximately 38 mV 
in the implied open-  circuit voltage (i VOC), measured using 
PL- based iVOC imaging. Figure  3b shows the i VOC distribution 
across the active cell area for both the control and passivated de-
vices. Time- resolved photoluminescence (TrPL) measurements 
further support this, revealing an increased carrier lifetime,
