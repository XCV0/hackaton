4.1   |   Solar Cell Fabrication
4.1.1   |   Materials
Perovskite precursors, lead iodide, and bromide powders (PbI 2 
and PbBr2) were purchased from TCI; cesium iodide and bromide 
powders (CsI and CsBr) were obtained from Sigma Aldrich and 
abcr GmbH, respectively. For evaporation, lead iodide beads (from 
Alfa Aesar) were used. Methylammonium bromide (MABr), for-
mamidinium iodide (FAI), and 4-  fluorophenethylam- monium 
(F- PEAI) powders were obtained from GreatCell Solar. PFN-  Br 
(poly[9,9- bis(3′- [N,N- dimethyl]- N- ethylammoniumpropyl- 2,7- 
fluorene)- alt- 2,7- (9,9- dioctylfluorene)] dibromide) and PTAA 
(poly[phenyl(2,4,6- trimethylphenyl)amine]) were from Sigma 
Aldrich. C 60 and Spiro- TTB (2,2′,7,7′- Tetra[N,N- di- p- tolyl]
amino- 9,9- spirobifluorene) were purchased from TCI and 
Lumtec, respectively.
4.1.2   |   Solutions Preparation
For depositing perovskite absorber for middle cell 
having bandgap of 1.56 eV and composition of 
Cs0.05(FA0.90MA0.10)0.95Pb(I0.95Br0.05)3, 1.2-  M solution was pre -
pared by dissolving appropriate stoichiometric amounts of FAI, 
MABr and CsI, PbBr 2, and PbI 2 with 1% Pb excess in solvent 
mixture of DMF:DMSO (4:1) and stirred overnight.
For preparation of PTAA (3 mg/mL) and PFN-  Br (0.5 mg/mL) 
solutions, powders were dissolved in toluene and methanol, re -
spectively, and stirred overnight.
4.1.3   |   Single Junction Solar Cells
The ohmic silicon substrates were first cleaned by spin coating 
200- μL ethanol and then treated with UV-  ozone for 15 min, be-
fore being transferred to the glovebox for cell processing.
4.1.4   |   Perovskite Middle- Cell (Eg ~ 1.56 eV)
A 120- uL PTAA solution was spin coated at 6000 rpm for 30 s 
and annealed at 100°C for 10 min on a preheated hotplate. 
It was followed by spin coating 60-  μL PFN-  Br solution at 
5000 rpm for 20 s on top to improve the wetting for perovskite 
deposition. A 150-  μL perovskite solution was then spin 
coated at 4000 rpm for 35 s; after 10 s, 300-  μL ethyl acetate 
was dropped on the spinning substrates. Samples were now 
annealed at 100°C for 30 min. Samples were then transferred 
to a different glovebox for ETL deposition. Fifteen nanome -
ters of C60 was thermally evaporated with an evaporation rate 
of 0.2 Å/s. As a buffer layer to avoid sputter damage, 20-  nm 
SnOx layer was deposited by atomic layer deposition at 80 °C. 
Twenty- five- nanometer ITO was then DC sputtered as recom-
bination layer using a shadow mask defining an active area of 
1 cm2. The metallization was done by evaporating 200-  0nm 
silver, and finally, 100- nm MgFx was evaporated as antireflec-
tion coating.
4.2   |   Perovskite Top- Cell (Eg ~ 1.88 eV)
Eight nanometers of Spiro-  TTB was deposited with a nom -
inal evaporation rate of 0.2 Å/s at base pressure lower than 
3 × 10−6 Torr and a working pressure lower than 4 × 10–6 Torr. 
Afterwards, samples were transferred to a Lesker mini-  
Spectros system evaporator for perovskite deposition. For this 
purpose, precursor materials CsBr, PbI2, and CsI were loaded 
in ceramic crucibles for coevaporation in a Lesker mini-  
Spectros system (at a base pressure lower than 2 × 10–6 Torr 
and a working pressure lower than 3 × 10−6 Torr). The rates 
as measured by quartz crystal monitors for the CsBr, PbI 2, 
and CsI sources were maintained at 0.8, 0.6, and 0.13 Å/s, re-
spectively, to obtain the CsPbI 2Br perovskite thin films with 
a bandgap of 1.88 eV and a thickness of ~250 nm. The evap -
oration temperature was ~460°C for CsBr, ~300°C for PbI 2, 
and ~420°C for CsI. Ceramic crucibles, made of alumina 
(Al2O3), were used for evaporation. For passivation with F-  
PEAI, the procedure shown in the Supporting Information  
text (Figure  S5b) was followed. The samples were annealed 
at 100°C for 15 min prior to passivation. Annealed samples 
were then spin coated with 150 μL of F- PEAI solution in IPA 
at 4000 rpm for 40 s. The F- PEAI coated samples were sub -
sequently annealed at 100°C for 5 min. The substrate holder 
was maintained at 25°C. The procedure for rest of the layers, 
including C60, SnOx, ITO, Ag, and MgF x, remained the same 
as described above.
4.2.1   |   Triple- Junction Solar Cells
The triple-  junction solar cell was silicon heterojunction 
(SHJ) solar cell with flat front and rear-  side textured as the 
bottom cell. The procedure for fabricating the SHJ bottom 
cells can be found in our previous publications [ 58, 59 ]. A 
25- nm- thick ITO layer acts as the recombination layer be -
tween the bottom cell and the perovskite middle cell. The 
middle- cell fabrication procedure is like the single-  junction 
solar cell fabrication as mentioned above. A 15-  nm- thick ITO 
layer was sputtered as the recombination layer between the 
perovskite subcells. Inorganic perovskite-  based top cell was 
then deposited on the samples according to the procedure 
mentioned above.
A shadow mask was used for all three ITO layers deposition 
defining 1- cm2 active cell area. The thicknesses of the recom -
bination layers used between subcells were optimized in our 
previous publication [17].
4.3   |   Characterization
4.3.1   |   EQE and I–V Measurements
The EQE and spectral response were measured using an 
in- house measurement setup. The measurements of triple-  
junction solar cells are performed according to [60, 61]. For the 
measurements of the top cell, red and infrared (IR) light emit-
ting diodes (LEDs) were used. For measurements of middle-  
cell blue and IR LEDs, and for the silicon bottom cell, blue and 
red LEDs were illuminated on the triple-  junction device. In
