1   |   Introduction
The shift towards clean energy sources is paramount in mitigat-
ing the impending climate crisis. Multijunction solar cells hold 
great promise in this regard. They offer higher power conver -
sion efficiency (PCE) than single- junction solar cells by stacking 
multiple light-  absorbing layers with complementary bandgaps 
to maximize absorption and minimize thermalization losses [1]. 
Among various design architectures of multijunction solar cells, 
the two- terminal approach is likely the most relevant for the up-
coming market introduction, as it requires similar integration 
into a module as standard silicon photovoltaics. Cesium-  based 
lead halide inorganic perovskites are especially interesting for 
multijunction applications not only because of their high band -
gap but also due to their enhanced thermal stability as com -
pared to the organic–inorganic perovskite counterparts [ 2–4]. 
However, these all-  inorganic perovskites tend to suffer from 
phase stability issues, transitioning from the desired cubic per -
ovskite (or α-  perovskite) phase to orthorhombic perovskite (or 
δ- perovskite) phase at room temperature [ 5, 6 ]. Substituting 
iodine atoms with bromine atoms in these perovskite lattices 
can stabilize the cubic perovskite phase and widens the band -
gap [2, 6]. CsPbI2Br is one of the most stable mixed-  halide inor-
ganic perovskite composition due to its optimal Goldschmidt's 
tolerance factor [7, 8]. Additionally, it offers a bandgap of ~1.9 eV 
[2, 7–9], which is suitable for top-  cell application in perovskite/
perovskite/silicon triple- junction solar cells.
Although solution-  processed CsPbI 2Br perovskites have been 
successfully utilized as high bandgap absorbers in all- perovskite 
tandems [4, 10, 11], they have not yet been implemented into sil-
icon based multijunction solar cells. This is primarily due to the 
necessity of high annealing temperatures of ≥ 200°C [9, 12, 13] 
in solution processing techniques to achieve the appropriate 
α- perovskite phase for these inorganic perovskites. Such high 
annealing temperatures can not only damage the underlying or-
ganic (common for charge transport layers) and perovskite layers 
but can also be detrimental for the bottom silicon heterojunction 
solar cell (SHJ), which has been the most common choice for 
multijunction applications [14]. At such high temperatures, hy -
drogen effuses out of the amorphous silicon (Si:H) layer degrad-
ing its passivation quality and hence the performance of SHJ 
cells [15]. It is worth noting that high annealing temperatures 
pose challenges for silicon-  based multijunction solar cells; they 
are less problematic in all-  perovskite tandems. This is because 
all- perovskite multijunction cells use a superstrate configura -
tion, where the high bandgap perovskite is the first subcell to be 
fabricated on a glass substrate [16]. This design allows for high- 
temperature annealing without affecting the other layers. Some 
other bottlenecks associated with the solution-  processed tech-
niques are the issues related to process compatibility like upscal-
ing for industrial processes and damage to underlying layers due 
to solvent infiltration, especially when both the top and middle 
perovskite absorbers share similar solvent systems [ 17, 18 ]. To 
address these problems, we propose depositing CsPbI 2Br per -
ovskite thin films using thermal evaporation. In this technique, 
precursor materials are evaporated in a high vacuum environ -
ment at controlled rates to produce thin films on the substrate, 
enabling conformal, large- area deposition and eliminate the use 
of toxic solvents like DMF. This technique also avoids possible 
solvent penetration—and thus damage—towards the underlying 
middle cell. It also allows precise control over thin film param -
eters such as thickness and stoichiometry. Additionally, it is a 
well- established industrial technique that offers opportunities 
for scalable manufacturing production lines. Till date, only a 
limited number of studies have explored vacuum deposition 
methods for CsPbI 2Br perovskite thin films. Chen et  al. [ 19] 
used dual source coevaporation method, using CsBr and PbI 2 
sources to deposit CsPbI 2Br perovskite films. They optimized 
the annealing duration for the evaporated films to achieve a sta-
bilized efficiency of 11.5% for the inorganic perovskite absorber 
annealed at 260°C for 60 s. Later, the same group employed 
layer- by- layer vacuum deposition technique to deposit CsPbI2Br 
perovskite films. By altering the rates and the number of lay -
ers in the film stack, they reported an even higher efficiency 
of 13% for their single junction solar cell device using a similar 
annealing temperature of 260°C for the evaporated film stack 
[20]. Liao et al. [21] also used layer- by- layer (or sequential) evap-
oration technique, where they used an annealing temperature 
of 300°C to convert their 200-  nm CsBr and 312-  nm PbI 2 layer 
stack to obtain the desired CsPbI2Br perovskite films. They also 
reported single junction solar cells in n- i- p configuration, show-
ing an overall efficiency of 9.96%. Gaonkar et al. [ 22] achieved 
an efficiency of 11.8% utilizing a similar sequential evaporation 
approach, however with alternating very thin layered stacks (4- 
nm CsBr and 6- nm PbI2). They demonstrated stability at 200°C 
for 72 h, though still requiring an annealing temperature of 
200°C to convert their layered stack into perovskite film. Other 
sequential evaporation studies, such as Lee et  al. [ 7], did also 
explore deposition sequences and stacking variations to deposit 
CsPbI2Br perovskite films, reporting an efficiency of ~11% for 
single junction cells but requiring a very high annealing tem -
perature of ~300°C to obtain the perovskite phase from the 
CsBr- PbI2 layered stacks. Ma et  al. [ 23] investigated the effect 
of rate ratios between CsBr and PbI 2 sources on the coevapo -
ration deposited CsPbI 2Br film properties and their stability. 
They found nonstoichiometric, CsBr-  rich perovskite films to be 
more stable than stoichiometric or PbI 2- rich perovskite films. 
However, the stoichiometric CsPbI 2Br perovskite film gave bet -
ter solar cells efficiencies than the nonstoichiometric absorber, 
achieving a stabilized efficiency of 5.5% for the best perform -
ing solar cell in n-  i- p configuration. Similarly, Park et  al. [ 24] 
used coevaporation, achieving ~5.7% efficiency for CsPbI 2Br 
absorber- based n- i- p solar cell devices. Recently, Liu et al. [ 25] 
focused on the effects of humidity and postdeposition annealing 
on the optical properties and stability of coevaporated CsPbI 2Br 
thin films, whereas Papadopoulou et  al. [ 26] used dynamic 
spectroscopic ellipsometry technique to study the effect of an -
nealing temperature on coevaporated CsPbI 2Br perovskite film 
properties, emphasizing the importance of accounting for film 
roughness; for example, they linked tetragonal-  to- cubic phase 
transition in the films to increased roughness. Beyond sequen -
tial and coevaporation methods, flash evaporation using single 
source has also been explored in literature as a faster alternative 
to deposit these inorganic perovskite thin films. It was first re -
ported for CsPbI 2Br thin films by Tai et al. [ 27], where a laser 
was used as the heating source and carbon nanotubes (CNTs) 
as the evaporator. The CNTs coated with CsPbI 2Br precursors 
in DMSO were scanned with a laser beam in a vacuum chamber 
to evaporate the perovskite precursor, which got deposited on 
the substrate within seconds. They also reported an efficiency 
of 9.4% for their single junction solar cell in n-  i- p configuration
