Igual- Muñoz et  al. [ 3] previously demonstrated evaporation 
of CsPbI 2Br films without annealing. However, this method 
has not yet been applied to fabricate multijunction solar cells. 
Additionally, light-  induced halide segregation remains a sig -
nificant challenge for mixed halide perovskite films with band -
gap higher than 1.65 eV [10, 33, 34 ], leading to voltage losses 
in related solar cell devices. Notably, the reports on thermally 
evaporated CsPbI2Br films have not yet addressed their photo -
stability. So far, limited efforts have been directed towards ad -
dressing these challenges in developing CsPbI2Br films; instead, 
high- bandgap organic–inorganic perovskite films supported by 
various chemical additives and treatments have been promoted 
for application as the top- cell absorber in triple- junction devices 
[18, 35–38].
In this work, we addressed these challenges by optimizing the 
thermal evaporation process to produce good quality, photo -
stable CsPbI 2Br thin films directly after evaporation, eliminat -
ing the need for postdeposition annealing. The as-  evaporated 
CsPbI2Br perovskite films not only displayed a high bandgap of 
1.88 eV but also showed good photostability, that is, no halide 
segregation under continuous illumination at 1-  Sun intensity 
in air for 3 h, without any additive engineering. We further re -
port pioneering efforts to integrate these evaporated inorganic 
CsPbI2Br perovskites as top-  cell absorbers in perovskite/per -
ovskite/silicon triple- junction solar cells, achieving a PCE of 21% 
over an active area of 1 cm2.
2   |   Results and Discussion
2.1   |   Evaporated Inorganic CsPbI2Br Perovskite 
Thin Films
To develop a low temperature deposition protocol for CsPbI 2Br 
perovskite films, we optimized the thermal evaporation pro -
cess by using three precursor sources, namely, CsI, CsBr, and 
PbI2. Evaporation rate from the three sources was fine tuned 
to achieve a perovskite film with desired bandgap directly after 
the evaporation, while maintaining the substrate holder at room 
temperature. This adaptation was aimed at promoting halide 
alloying, allowing for the formation of the desired perovskite 
film without any temperature treatment. The deposition mecha-
nism of perovskite thin films using thermal evaporation differs 
significantly from solution processing techniques. In thermal 
evaporation, materials vaporize into a range of species, from 
intact molecules to dissociated molecules and atoms, which 
then condense on the substrate and react to form a solid film 
[39]. Evaporation conditions become particularly critical when 
working with materials featuring different vapor pressures; for 
instance, iodine compounds (CsI and PbI2) possess higher vapor 
pressures than their bromine counterparts (CsBr and PbBr 2), 
causing them to vaporize more rapidly [ 39, 40 ]. Apart from 
evaporation rates, perovskite thin- film properties like grain size 
are also closely related to process parameters such as base pres -
sure, substrate temperature, and the postdeposition annealing 
treatments [41, 42]. Figure 1a shows the schematic of the three- 
source evaporation process used in this study. The process pa -
rameters for the three-  source evaporation method are given in 
Section 4. In our study, we varied the evaporation rates of the 
three sources to obtain the desired perovskite film. Table S1 lists 
the various deposition rates tested for the three- source evapora-
tion method along with the resulted perovskite film bandgaps. 
The changing bandgap as a function of the evaporation rates 
of the sources suggests a rearrangement in the film deposition 
during evaporation. Initially, we tested the conventional two- 
source coevaporation method using CsBr and PbI 2 precursors; 
however, as reported in literature, the α- perovskite phase for the 
dual- source deposition technique was only obtained after sub -
jecting the evaporated films to a high annealing temperature of 
250°C [19, 23–26], making the technique unsuitable for mono -
lithic Si-  based multijunction solar cell applications. Details of 
the two- source evaporation method are discussed in Figure S1.
Towards triple-  junction solar cell integration, we selected the 
perovskite films showing a bandgap of 1.88 eV, as estimated 
using the Tauc plot (Figure  1b,c) for this study. The desired 
bandgap was obtained using the evaporation rates of 0.8 Å/s 
for CsBr, 0.6 Å/s for PbI 2, and 0.13 Å/s for CsI (see Table  S1). 
Figure  1d (inset) shows a photograph of the evaporated per -
ovskite film on a glass substrate, maintained at room tempera -
ture during deposition, which clearly exhibits a uniform brown 
color, a characteristic of high bandgap perovskite thin films. The 
X- ray diffractogram (XRD) (Figure 1d) of the as- deposited films 
confirms the presence of the pure perovskite phase. The depos -
ited perovskite films further showed a compact film morphology 
with well- defined grains as shown in Figure 1e.
