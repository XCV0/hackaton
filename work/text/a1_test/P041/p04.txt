The charge transport dynamics were carried out by steady-
state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photolumines-
cence (TRPL) measurements. The steady-state PL spectra of
perovskite ﬁlms (glass/perovskite) with and without TPFB mod-
iﬁcation are plotted in Figure 3C. Compared to pristine ﬁlm, a
notable enhancement in PL intensity was observed after the
incorporation of TPFB. Therefore, the incorporation of TPFB
could effectively passivate defects and suppress nonradiative
recombination.[31] The TRPL spectra (Figure 3D) ﬁtted by biex-
ponential functions provided more detailed mechanisms of
charge dynamics. For the structure of glass/perovskite/HTL,
both TPFB-modiﬁed and pristine perovskite ﬁlms showed fast
decay (τ1) and slow decay (τ2) phenomena related to charge
carrier transfer and radiative recombination caused by the
trapped carriers, respectively.[32] The detailed parameters are
summarized in Table S1, Supporting Information. The decrease
in TRPL lifetime implied effective charge extraction and trans-
port after incorporation of TPFB.
To further verify the charge recombination mechanism, short-
circuit current density ( Jsc) and open-circuit voltage (Voc)were
measured as a function of light intensity (Plight). The dependence
of Voc on the I is presented in Figure 3E. To assess the
recombination process, the ideality factor (n) was calculated by
Equation (2)
n ¼ e kBT dVoc dlnðPlightÞ (2)
where kB is the Boltzmann constant and e represents elementary
charge.[33]
In general, the bimolecular recombination dominates when n
approaches unity, while the Shockley–Read–Hall (SRH) mono-
molecular recombination related to trap-assisted nonradiative
recombination dominates when n value approaches 2. Also,
the recombination process dominates PSCs device properties
at open-circuit conditions because all photogenerated carriers
become recombined.[34,35] In this study, the n values obtained
from the slopes of Voc versus I were estimated to 1.85 and
1.21 for pristine and TPFB-modiﬁed devices, respectively. The
smaller n values indicated that TPFB could effectively decline
the trap-assisted monomolecular recombination, which may
result in increased Voc. For comparison, the light intensity
(I)-dependent Jsc plotted in Figure 3F showed a good linear rela-
tionship between Jsc versus I. TPFB-modiﬁed and pristine devi-
ces displayed slopes of, respectively, 0.99 and 0.98, indicating
similar effects of bimolecular recombination on both devices.[36]
To gain a better understanding of the charge transport dynam-
ics, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) characteriza-
tions were performed, and related Nyquist plots are drawn in
Figure S10, Supporting Information. The semicircle at the
high-frequency region was attributed to the charge transfer
resistance (Rct). The lower values of Rct in Nyquist plots revealed
facilitated charge carrier extraction and transport attributed to
defect passivation, consistent with TRPL measurements.[37]
