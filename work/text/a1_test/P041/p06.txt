recombination centers leading to the deterioration of efﬁciency
and stability of perovskite devices. The decreased Pb0 intensity of
TPFB-incorporated perovskite ﬁlm implied that the hypervalent
iodine part could restrict the reduction process of Pb2þ ions due
to their oxidizing properties.[43] As the hypervalent iodine part
could serve as Lewis acid, which means that it owns the oxidation
properties. We are wondering if it is possible the hypervalent
iodine part could oxidize the I/C0 to I0. To conﬁrm this hypothesis,
we dissolved the MAI and TPFB in deionized water and aged in
ambient environment for 3 days. As shown in Figure S16,
Supporting Information, it could be found that the solution
was still colorless, which means that there is no formation of
I0. The environmental stability was further explored by storing
perovskite devices under ambient air (RH ¼ 40% /C6 5%,
T ¼ 300 K) without encapsulation for 1000 h (Figure 5D). The
TPFB-incorporated perovskite ﬁlm maintained about 85% of
its initial efﬁciency, while the pristine devices preserved only
48% of the initial value.
The passivation effects of TPFB based on two-step fabricated
CsFA-based perovskite devices were also investigated through
posttreatment process. To this end, TPFB was dissolved in CB
and then spin-coated on the surface of the as-fabricated perovskite
ﬁlm. Unsurprisingly, the simple surface treatment still delivered
exceptional passivation effects. As shown in Figure 6A,B, after
TPFB modiﬁcation, the efﬁciency of 23.38% for reverse scan
and 22.34% for forward scan was achieved, respectively, and
the stabilized PCE of 23.23% was achieved. The detailed param-
eters were shown in Table S3, Supporting Information.
