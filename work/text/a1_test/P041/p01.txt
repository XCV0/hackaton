Photocationic Initiator Induced Synergy for
High-Performance Perovskite Solar Cells
Jintao Wang, Xu Zhang, Zhenyu Wang, Shuming Chen, Rui Wang,* and Yu Duan*
1. Introduction
Organic–inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs)
are promising candidates for future photovoltaic applications owing
to their exceptional photoelectric properties, such as high light
absorption coefﬁcient, long charge carrier lifetime, and tunable
bandgap.[1,2] So far, a record power conversion efﬁciency (PCE)
of 25.7% has been achieved, a value far
beyond the ﬁrst debut of 3.8%, which is on
par with silicon-based and inorganic thin-ﬁlm
solar cells. Such stunning milestone incites
further investigation for the improvement
and commercialization of PSCs.[3–5]
Despite the high efﬁciency of PSCs, the
PSCs devices still suffer from notorious sta-
bility falling far short of actual industrializa-
tion.[6] The presence of more polarized
components (e.g., MAþ)constructasoft
perovskite crystal structures, which are
more vulnerable to decomposition under
external stresses, such as oxygen and mois-
ture.[7] The solutions to these issues have
been attempted by the state-of-the-art encap-
sulation and insulation process used to
exclude the external stresses. For instance,
we have demonstrated that the use of the
atomic layer deposition process could form
dense Al2O3 ﬁlms that may inhibit the
penetration of oxygen and moisture. This
strategy resulted in preserving 95% of the
initial efﬁciency after 300 min immersion
in water.[8] Besides, Han et al. found that insertion of chlorinated
graphene oxide layer between perovskite and hole transport layer
(HTL) extremely enhanced the stability of PSCs devices.[9]
However, the intrinsic defects caused by the uncontrollable
crystallization and ionic properties of perovskite materials may
serve as nonradiative recombination sites and ions may be prone
to migration, resulting in deteriorated device performance.[10,11]
Meanwhile, ionic defects can easily form on the surface and
grain boundaries (GBs) of perovskite ﬁlms. Thus, efﬁcient sur-
face and GBs passivation strategies are highly desirable to limit
these issues,[12,13] where Lewis acid/base additives are the most
investigated passivation strategies. In this respect, Lewis base
additive with lone-pair electrons is widely used to passivate posi-
tively charged defects. However, Lewis acid passivators based on
fullerene and their derivatives require more investigations to
expand the actual application of PSCs.[14–16]
Herein, a Lewis acid passivator based on photocationic initia-
tor 4-isopropyl- 4’-methyldiphenyliodonium tetrakis(pentaﬂuoro-
phenyl)borate (TPFB) (Figure 1A) was introduced to perovskite
by a one-step antisolvent process to enhance the performance
of PSCs devices. The hypervalent iodine part served as the
Lewis acid to interact with undercoordinated halides and Pb–I
antisite (PbI3 /C0) defects through ionic interactions to eliminate
unwanted nonradiative recombination. The pentaﬂuorophenyl
borate part tailored the crystallization process through strong

The perovskite structures are prone to decomposition due to the formation of
defect sites under various stressors. As a result, the stability of perovskite
materials has been an Achilles’ heel in actual commercialization. Herein,
photocationic initiator based on 4-isopropyl-4’-methyldiphenyliodonium tetrakis
(pentaﬂuorophenyl)borate (TPFB) is introduced into perovskite ﬁlm through the
antisolvent process to enhance the efﬁciency and stability of perovskite solar
cells. The hypervalent iodine part serves as a Lewis acid to passivate the neg-
atively charged defects, while the pentaﬂuorophenyl borate part is used to tailor
the crystallization process by forming strong hydrogen bonding. Furthermore, the
superior hydrophobicity of TPFB insulates the perovskite ﬁlms. The resulted
MAPbI3 devices show maximum power conversion efﬁciency (PCE) reaching
20.41% and ultrahigh open voltage (Voc) up to 1.15 V with the remaining 85%
efﬁciency after 1000 h shelf storage. Furthermore, by introducing TPFB through a
simple posttreatment process, the device delivers a PCE of 23.4% (steady-state
PCE of 23.2%), features useful for advanced double cation-based perovskite
device. In sum, these ﬁndings develop a universal passivation strategy and open
a novel ﬁeld of Lewis acid-based passivation agent.
