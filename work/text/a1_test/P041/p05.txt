A negligible effect of absorption and bandgap was noticed after
the incorporation of TPFB, conﬁrming that the incorporation of
TPFB would not disturb the perovskite crystal structure.[38]
To compare the photovoltaic performances based on regular
n–i–p structure, complete PSCs devices with and without
TPFB incorporation were fabricated. Here, ITO was used as
transport electrode, tin oxide as ETL, and Spiro-OMeTAD as
HTL. The maximum efﬁciencies of perovskite with and without
TPFB incorporation under AM1.5G illumination are shown in
Figure 4B, and statistical distribution diagrams of photovoltaic
parameters at various TPFB-incorporated concentrations are
gathered in Figure S12 and Table S2, Supporting Information.
Enhanced Voc and ﬁll factor (FF) were observed. The Voc value
increased from 1.13 to 1.15 V, and FF rose from 73.98% to
75.32%. These values were superior to those of pristine devices
attributed to the effective defect passivation induced by the incor-
poration of TPFB. These changes agreed well with decreased
defects state density mentioned above and further conﬁrmed
by the Mott–Schottky data (Figure 4C).[39] The enhanced built-
in potential indicated declined defects, which could promote
the charge transfer leading to enhanced Voc and FF. And the
slight increment in Jsc could be ascribed to the improved charge
carrier extraction, which was conﬁrmed by the external quantum
efﬁciency (EQE) measurements (Figure 4D). The integrated cur-
rent densities from EQE spectra were estimated to 23.12 and
22.9 mA cm/C02, matching well the Jsc values obtained from J–V
curves under simulated AM1.5G.[40] The steady-state outputs
at maximum power point under continuous illumination are
shown in Figure S13, Supporting Information. The steady
PCE of TPFB-modiﬁed and pristine devices was estimated to
19.72% and 18.75%, respectively, further conﬁrming the superi-
ority of the devices after TPFB incorporation.
The incorporation of TPFB would inhibit the inﬁltration of
moisture to retard the deterioration of perovskite ﬁlm due to
hydrophobicity. To conﬁrm this hypothesis, XRD patterns were
obtained to prove the insulation effects after the incorporation of
TPFB. As shown in Figure 5A, the sample discolored and distinct
PbI2 peak accompanied with the decrease in (110) peak intensity
was observed after exposure to the ambient environment for
200 h, indicating the decomposition of pristine perovskite ﬁlm.
As for TPFB-incorporated perovskite ﬁlm, despite the inferior
PbI2 peak intensity, the little-to-no transform of color and
negligible reduction in (110) peak intensity suggested enhanced
environmental stability after incorporation of TPFB.[41] The
enhanced hydrophobicity was further conﬁrmed by water contact
angle measurements. As shown in Figure S14, Supporting
Information, the pristine perovskite ﬁlm demonstrated a contact
angle of 35.6°, which signiﬁcantly enhanced to 77.2° after TPFB
incorporation. The UV–vis absorption in Figure 5B also con-
ﬁrmed the retarded phase decomposition. Typical PbI2 absorp-
tion peaks could be distinguished at around 500 nm for the
pristine ﬁlm, while TPFB-incorporated ﬁlm only showed minor
absorption attenuation.[42]
