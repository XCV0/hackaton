3.3. Solar cell characteristics
The J – V characteristics of the solar cells measured under standard 
test conditions are given in Fig. 7a and b. The corresponding EQE plots 
measured under short circuit condition for the cells are given in Fig. 7c 
and d. In each case, the characteristics given are for the best solar cells 
among a set of four cells, shown in the experimental section Fig. 2v.
The maximum power density in mW cm – 2 obtained for the solar 
cells is numerically the maximum conversion efficiency (%) as well. 
While the absorber films in CD solar cells are of 160 nm in thickness, in 
VTE cells, these are of 300 nm. In spite of such differences, the cell 
parameters are similar, as summarized in the inset tables. A conversion 
efficiency of 4.6–5.7 % is available in the CD and VTE cells. The EQE 
plots in Fig. 7c for the CD-cell and for the VTE cell in Fig. 7d show that 
the maximum EQE is up to 0.84 for the C6H9 cell and 0.76 for the VT-3 
cell. However, the EQE falls below 0.5 at wavelengths longer than 800 
nm in both these cases. Correction of this deficiency requires the 
improvement of a p+-layer, which is assumed to form at the Sb-S-Se/C- 
electrode interface [5,13,14].
Since the EQE represents optical absorption in the absorber material 
of the solar cell and hence to α for a particular photon energy (hν (eV) =
1240/λ (nm)), the plot of (EQE × hν)2/3 versus hν in the inset for the 
near infrared toward the visible region of the spectrum would show a 
behavior similar to that in Fig. 6e and f. A linear regression fit for these 
plots for the photon energy range 1.5–2.0 eV shows values for Eg (direct- 
forbidden transitions) for the CD cells B6G9 and C6H9 of 1.48 and 1.42 
eV, close to 1.5 and 1.41 eV, Fig. 6e, obtained from the optical T and R 
data. For VT-2 and VT-3 cells, the corresponding values of Eg of 1.45 eV 
and 1.39 eV for VT-2 and VT-3 absorbers deviate more from 1.35 to 1.25 
eV obtained from the optical data (Fig. 6f).
