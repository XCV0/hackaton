CdS substrates from solutions containing potassium antimony tartrate, 
and varying quantities of thioacetamide (sulfide source) and sodium

this value increased to 5.2 %. In solar cells produced by vacuum thermal

Eg solar cells of Sb2(SxSe1-x)3 may help making innovations in these cells

2. Experimental
2.1. Development of the solar cell device structure
In both CD and VTE methods, the basic device structure remained the 
same, as described in Fig. 2i. Clear sheet glass of 2.2 mm in thickness 
with a transparent electrically conductive coating (TEC 7, fluorine- 
doped SnO2 or FTO) with a sheet resistance of 7 Ω was supplied in 30 
cm × 30 cm size sheets from Greatcell Solar Materials (Australia). These 
were cut to 7.5 cm × 2.5 cm in size, cleaned with cotton swabs dipped in 
a neutral soap solution, rinsed and dried.
2.1.1. Chemical reagents and thin film deposition
Laboratory reagents of 98–99 % assay supplied by Sigma – Aldrich or 
Fermont (Mexico) were used for preparing the solar cells described in 
Fig. 2. The colloidal graphite paint and the silver paint were purchased 
from SPI (USA). Solutions of different substances were prepared in de- 
ionized water at the concentrations (mol dm – 3 = M) mentioned for 
the chemical deposition of the different thin films. Sodium selenosulfate, 
Na2SeSO3, of 0.4 M in concentration with concurrent presence of 0.4 M 
of Na2SO3 was prepared by refluxing Na2SO3 and elemental Se in 100 
mL water, described below.
2.1.2. CdS thin film deposition
A CdS thin film of 80–100 nm in thickness, appearing in reflected 
light as a golden-purple layer on the glass-side of the FTO was made by 
chemical deposition at 80 ◦C during 55–70 min, as described in Fig. 2ii.
