Because of compositional variation along the thickness of the films, 
the (hkl) peaks are broadened. Hence, the application of Scherrer’s 
formula [21] available with the XRD software for the estimation of the 
average crystalline diameter (D) in the absorber film may lead to its 
underestimation. The full width at half maximum of diffraction peaks 
from the (120) and (321) planes of the materials were considered for the 
evaluation of D. For the top 80 nm region of the absorber layer, GIXRD 
pattern recorded at δ = 0.5◦ was used; and for the material up to a depth 
of 240 nm, the pattern recorded at δ = 1.5◦ was used. There was no clear 
difference between the values obtained in these two cases. The average 
value for D were: B6G9, 18.5 nm; C6H9, 20.5 nm; VT-1, 22 nm; VT-2, 
19.5 nm; and VT-3, 23 nm. There is no marked difference between the 
values of D in the film deposited at 80 ◦C in the case of chemically 
deposited (CD) solar cell structures and those deposited at a substrate 
temperature of 425–440 ◦C in the solar cells produced by VTE. The value 
of D for all these films may be is stated as approximately 20 nm. In 
summary, the composition of the films expected from the Se 2 –/(S2 – +
Se 2 –) ratio in the deposition bath for B6G9 and C6H9, and the S/(S +
Se) ratio in the evaporation source for the solar cells in the FTO/Cd-
S/absorber1/absorber2 stack and those experimentally determined as 
an average from the GIXRD data in Figs. 4 and 5 are.
