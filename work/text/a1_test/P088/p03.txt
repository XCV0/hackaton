2.1.3. Sb2(Sx1Se1-x1)3, Eg1/Sb2(Sx2Se1-x2)3, Eg2 thin films and cell 
structure development
The chemical deposition of the CdS film on FTO was followed by 
chemical deposition at 80 ◦C of a thin film (80–90 nm) of Sb-S-Se, 
Fig. 2iii, from solutions of potassium antimony tartrate, sodium sele-
nosulfate, thioacetamide and thiosulfate, as reported earlier [11]. The 
relative composition of selenosulfate was increased in the second 
Sb-S-Se deposition, so that an absorber layer of varying chemical 
composition and hence of varying bandgap was produced: FTO/CdS 
(80–100 nm)/Sb2(Sx1Se1-x1)3 80–90 nm, Eg1/Sb2(Sx2Se1-x2)3 80–90 nm, 
Eg2. For chemically deposited absorbers, x1 > x2 and Eg1 > Eg2.
In the vacuum thermal evaporation (VTE) methodology, the solar 
cell structures, FTO/CdS (80–100 nm)/Sb2(Sx1Se1-x1)3 100–150 nm, Eg1/ 
Sb2(Sx2Se1-x2)3 100–150 nm, Eg2 were produced by sequential evapo-
ration of powder mixtures of Sb2S3 and Sb2Se3 from two molybdenum 
crucibles heated to 850 ◦C, Fig. 2iv, with four FTO/CdS substrates 
mounted on a rotating substrate holder placed 40 cm above the cruci-
bles. Quartz – halogen lamps mounted behind the substrate holder plate 
kept the substrate at nearly 425 ◦C during the deposition. The powder 
mixture, typically of 500 mg, was spread inside the crucibles with a 
quantity of SbCl3 (60 mg) and a few drops of propylene glycol and dried 
[5]. In VTE, both x1 > x2 and Eg1 > Eg2 and x1 < x2 and Eg1 < Eg2 cases 
were experimented, with the latter option showing the best result re-
ported here, with η of 5.75 %.
