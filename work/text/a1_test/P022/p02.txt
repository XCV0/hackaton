Here, we codesign MgxZn1–xO/CdSeyTe1/C0y solar cell
performance by ﬁnding the optimum alloy compositions in
MgxZn1/C0xO contact and CdSeyTe1/C0y absorber layers. The
bandgap tunability range for MgxZn1/C0xO without forming
secondary phases and Se accumulation at the CdSeyTe1/C0y/
MgxZn1/C0xO interface are shown. The device performance data
facilitated identifying the optimal composition range for
MgxZn1/C0xO and CdSeyTe1/C0y at Mg/(Mg þ Zn) /C2518% and

solar cell device with VOC ¼ 836 mV, ﬁll factor ¼ 69%,
JSC ¼ 30.6 mA cm/C02 and efﬁciency up to 17.7%. The device
performance trends are explained based on the changing
contact/absorber bandgaps and increased charge carrier life-
times at MgxZn1/C0xO/CdSeyTe1/C0y measured by time-resolved
photoluminescence (TRPL).
2. Methods
To codesign MgxZn1/C0xO /CdSeyTe1/C0y thin-ﬁlm solar cells, we
fabricated PV device libraries that had orthogonal composition
gradients in CdSeyTe1/C0y absorbers and MgxZn1/C0xO contacts
using the combinatorial approach. For MgxZn1/C0xO material
characterization and growth optimization, thin-ﬁlm samples
with compositional gradients were deposited by combinatorial
radio frequency magnetron sputtering from ZnO and Mg tar-
gets[12] on 50 /C2 50 mm Eagle XG glass substrates. For
CdSeyTe1/C0y device fabrication, CdSe and CdTe were deposited
by sequential thermal evaporation without breaking vacuum,
on commercially available 3 /C2 3” TEC12D glass with bilayer
SnO2 and 120 nm of MgxZn1/C0xO described earlier, with
CdSeyTe1/C0y gradient orthogonal to the MgxZn1/C0xO gradient.
The resulting samples were subject to CdCl2 and CuCl2 treat-
ments, followed by thermal evaporation of Au backcontacts.
This resulted in combinatorial libraries with 128 MgxZn1/C0xO/
CdSeyTe1/C0y devices of unique MgxZn1/C0xO and CdSeyTe1/C0y com-
position combinations.
To characterize the resulting MgxZn1/C0xO/CdSeyTe1/C0y device
libraries, spatially resolved characterization methods were used.
For the MgxZn1/C0xO material characterization, each 50 /C2 50 mm
sample library was characterized at 4 /C2 11 grid of points with
spatially resolved measurements of composition using X-Ray
ﬂuorescence (XRF), structure using X-Ray diffraction (XRD),
and bandgap using optical absorption spectroscopy. The
MgxZn1/C0xO/CdSeyTe1/C0y device libraries were characterized by
current/C0voltage, external quantum efﬁciency, time-resolved pho-
toluminescence, and cross-section scanning electron microscopy
(SEM). Experimental combinatorial data were processed using
