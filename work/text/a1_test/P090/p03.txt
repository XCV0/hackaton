denser and larger crystal grains than that of CIGS without K.
These results demonstrated that K doping can increase the grain
size of the CIGS absorber ﬁlm, consistent with the litera-
ture.[22,29] “Kþ” was thought to act as a Se reservoir during
CIGS growth similar to Na doping,[22] in which Na2Sex is formed
and segregated at the grain boundaries and acts as a Se reservoir
during CIGS growth.[20]
To ﬁgure out the role that K plays in the grain growth of the
CIGS ﬁlm, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) characteriza-
tion was implemented. As shown in Figure 3, compared with
CIGS without K, the peaks for Cu 2p3/2,In3d5/2, and Se 3d
in CIGS with K all shift toward higher energy. In Figure 3b,
In-1 split peak from In 3d5/2 at around 444.7 eV for CIGS without
K or CIGS with K corresponds to that of the chalcopyrite,[30,31]
while the split In-2 peak at 445.2 eV for CIGS without K and
445.0 eV for CIGS with K may be attributed to In oxides in
ref.[32] or hydroxides.[33] As shown in Figure 3c, the raw spec-
trum of Ga 2p3/2 is very complex and a main peak can be split
at around 1117.7 eV, corresponding to that in chalcopyrite, while
the small peaks scattered on both sides may be attributed to Ga
oxides[32] or hydroxides.[33] In Figure 3d–f, Voigt peak ﬁtting on
the Se 3 d signal shows the presence of two pairs of peaks for Se
3 d contributions (Se-1 at around 54.2 and 55.0 eV and Se-2 at
around 55.1 and 55.7 eV) for CIGS without K and CIGS with
K; those signals have been suggested to belong to the CIGS chal-
copyrite chemical environment.[22,31,34,35] For CIGS with K, a
pair of new small peaks (Se-3) appear at the lower binding
energy of around 53.6 and 54.2 eV, which are ascribed to
K–In–Se species.[34] In the literature, K doping is carried out
by KF postdeposition treatment (KF-PDT), and only the
K–In–Se phase is formed on the surface.[34] However, in this
work, KCl is doped in the precursor solution, so the K–In–Se
phase may also exist in bulk. However, as shown in Figure 3g
and h, almost identical peaks of Na and K for CIGS without
K and CIGS with K were recorded, which are assigned to the
Na and K elements diffusing from the soda lime glass, while
the peak of K belonging to K–In–Se phase is invisible, because
K-In-Se was etched out by ammonium sulﬁde solution.
To further reveal the effect of K doping on the CIGS ﬁlm, the
change of Ga element content was studied. As shown in Table 2,
the feed in the ratio of Ga/(InþGa) was 0.40, and this value,
respectively, changed to 0.31 and 0.35 for CIGS without K
and CIGS with K, corresponding to 23 and 13% Ga loss. This
result demonstrates that K doping can inhibit the Ga loss.
According to the literature, doping K to CIGS by KF-PDT
strongly depletes Ga at CIGS ﬁlm surface.[36,37] Here, K doping
is realized by adding KCl into the precursor solution, which is
found to prevent Ga loss during the fabrication of the
CIGS ﬁlms.
