Effect of K Doping on the Performance of Aqueous
Solution-Processed Cu(In,Ga)Se2 Solar Cell
Shasha Hao, Shaotang Yu, Xinge Liu, Bingyan Li, Shuaiqi Han, Hao Xin, Weibo Yan,*
and Wei Huang*
1. Introduction
The fabrication of chalcopyrite Cu(In,Ga)Se2 (CIGS) absorber by
the solution process is considered to be a promising method for
low-cost and large-area production of CIGS solar cells because it
does not require high-cost vacuum facility and has compatibility
with commercial printing or blade-coating techniques.
Hydrazine-based ink was the ﬁrst solution that was reported
to prepare CIGS absorber and solar cells by Mitzi et al.,[1] and
the device efﬁciency based on this method was improved to
18.1% (17.3% certiﬁed) by Zhang et al.[2] However, hydrazine
is not a desirable solvent due to its highly
explosive and toxic features. Therefore,
less-explosive and low-toxic alternatives
for hydrazine, such as ethanol,[3] 2-methox-
yethanol,[4] tetramethylguanidine,[5] triethy-
lenetetramine,[6] N-methyl-pyrrolidone,[7]
dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO),[8] N,N-
dimethylformamide (DMF),[9,10] mixed
solvent of ethylenediamine and 1,2-
ethanedithiol,[11] mixed solvent of water
(H2O), and polyethylene glycol,[12,13] have
been developed successively. Among them,
CIGS solar cell with the highest efﬁciency
of 18.7% has been achieved from the
DMSO-based precursor solution.[14]
Nevertheless, considering complete safety,
nontoxicity, and environmental friendli-
ness, H2O is the best solvent for precursor
solution.
H2O as a solvent of the precursor solu-
tion was ﬁrst reported by Wong and co-
workers [15] and CuInSe2 (CIS) solar cell
with an efﬁciency of 5.9% was obtained. Subsequently, by adding
gallium into the solution, CIGS solar cells were fabricated and
the efﬁciency was improved to 10.5% by the same group.[16]
Using Cu, In, Ga nitrates, and thiourea as the precursor,
Septina et al. obtained 10.7% efﬁcient CIGS solar cells from
aqueous solution after sodium doping.[17] Using CuCl2, InCl3,
Ga(NO3)3·H2O, and thiourea as the precursor, Kim et al.
obtained 10.89% efﬁcient CIGSSe solar cell from aqueous solu-
tion after S alloying.[18] Meanwhile, our group also reported H2O-
based precursor solution for fabrication of CIS solar cells. Using
two pure complexes CuTU3Cl and InTU3Cl3 as Cu and In pre-
cursors to prevent disproportionation of CuCl and hydrolysis of
InCl3, CIS solar cell with an encouraging efﬁciency of 12.3% was
obtained from the aqueous solution.[19]
To optimize the bandgap of the chalcopyrite absorber and
improve device performance, Ga alloying is necessary.
Meanwhile, it has been reported that alkali (Na, K, Rb) doping
is crucial for improving CIGS absorber quality and device
performance.[20–22] To the best of our knowledge, the effects
of K doping on the performance of aqueous-processed CIGS
solar cells have not been investigated yet. In this work, we report
the fabrication of CIGS solar cells from aqueous solutions using
CuCl, InCl3, GaCl3, and thiourea as precursors and systemati-
cally investigate the effect of K doping on device performance.

for CIGS device without K doping. After K doping, the PCE

Copper indium gallium selenide Cu(In,Ga)Se2 (CIGS) solar cells fabricated by
vacuum thermal evaporation, magnetron sputtering, or organic solvent solution-
process either lead to high manufacturing cost and ﬁlm nonuniformity or cause
explosion, toxication and pollution. To address these issues, herein, water as a
solvent to prepare the CIGS precursor solution is used and 7.04% efﬁcient CIGS
solar cells without MgF2 antireﬂection layer is obtained. Furthermore, for the ﬁrst
time, K doping on CIGS ﬁlms via adding “Kþ” into the aqueous precursor
solution is conducted. K–In–Se species is formed in the process of selenization
and acts as a Se reservoir during CIGS grain growth, resulting in improved quality
of the CIGS ﬁlm with much larger grains. K doping can also inhibit the loss of Ga
element. The high-bandgap K–In–Se phase and higher Ga content combine to
result in the increase in the CIGS bandgap. As a result, the K-doped CIGS solar
cell achieves a 33.1% enhancement in efﬁciency to 9.37%, with the signiﬁcant
increase in the open-circuit voltage from 520.5 to 597.1 mV and ﬁll factor from
50.77 to 55.36%. These results provide an important preliminary foundation for
the development of aqueous precursor solution-based CIGS solar cells.
