(EQE) spectra of the two solar cells are given in Figure 4b. The
integrated current densities from the EQE are 24.44 and
26.30 mA cm/C02 for the CIGS without K and CIGS with K, which
are in good agreement with the values measured by J–V. The
CIGS device with K doping has stronger EQE response in the
whole wavelength range from 425 to 1115 nm. This phenome-
non indicates that CIGS device with K doping shows a wider
depletion width and higher quality of the absorber bulk, which
can be attributed to the relatively larger and denser grains of
CIGS with K (Figure 2). To investigate the current loss of the
CIGS with K, the EQE, the surface reﬂectivity, and the internal
quantum efﬁciency (IQE) are shown in Figure 4c, from which we
can see that a signiﬁcant current loss comes from surface reﬂec-
tion. A current density of 29.54 mA cm/C02 was calculated from the
IQE, which corresponds to an efﬁciency of 9.76%.
The bandgap (Eg), Urbach energy (EU), bandgap ﬂuctuation
(σg), and electrostatic potential ﬂuctuation (γopt) of the two absorb-
ers are extracted from the EQE data by replotting the EQE at a long
wavelength range as a function of energy according to the litera-
ture,[38] as shown in Figure S3,Supporting Information. Eg of the
CIGS without K is 1.163 eV and increased to 1.167 eV after K dop-
ing. Bandgap widening has been observed in the vacuum-based
CIGS absorber upon KF-PDT treatment.[37] This showed that a
high-bandgap K–In–Se phase forms on the surface of CIGS
absorbers subjected to KF-PDT treatment, and increases the sur-
face bandgap of KF-PDT CIGS.[37,39] In this work, bandgap wid-
ening was also observed in precursor aqueous-solution based
CIGS absorber upon adding KCl, which is also probably attributed
to the formation of the K–In–Se phase. However, we thought that
almost the K–In–Se phase was etched away by the treatment of
ammonium sulﬁde solution before deposition of CdS; therefore,
the increase in the bandgap of CIGS was not obvious. Moreover, K
doping slightly inhibits Ga loss, which also contributes to the
increase in the bandgap. The σg values of the two absorbers are
35.00 meV for CIGS without K and 33.69 meV for CIGS with
K, respectively. The slightly lower σg of CIGS with K reveals its
better absorber uniformity, consistent with the improved mor-
phology from SEM images. The Voc,def of the two absorber ﬁlms
are almost the same, while EU is reduced from 16.05 to 13.34 meV
after K doping, further proving that the quality of the absorber is
improved after K doping.[40]
3. Conclusion
In summary, the precursor solutions for CIGS ﬁlms were pre-
pared by the strategy of dissolving the composite of CuCl,
InCl3·4H2O, GaCl3, and thiourea in the environmentally benign
solvent of H2O, the precursor ﬁlms from which were selenized to
successfully obtain the CIGS ﬁlms. K doping effects on the pre-
cursor and selenized CIGS ﬁlms were ﬁrst systematically inves-
tigated. XRD, Raman, and SEM characterization indicated that K
doping has no obvious effect on the structure and morphology of
the CIGS precursor ﬁlms, while it promotes grain growth into a
larger size and forms a denser crystalline ﬁlm. XPS characteri-
zation explained that K–In–Se species generated during the sele-
nization process can provide Se in the grain growth, resulting in
the formation high-quality CIGS ﬁlms. The relative content of
surface elements of CIGS from XPS demonstrated that K doping
can inhibit the loss of Ga element. The high-bandgap K–In–Se
phase and higher Ga content combine to result in the increase in
CIGS bandgap after K doping. As a result, the K-doped CIGS
solar cell achieved 33.1% enhancement of efﬁciency to 9.37%
with signiﬁcant increase in Voc from 520.5 to 597.1 mV and
FF from 50.77 to 55.36%.
4. Experimental Section
Precursor Solution Preparation: First, 3.535 g thiourea (TU, 99%,
Aladdin, recrystallized) was added into 18 mL ultrapure water and stirred
at 65 /C14C until a clear solution was formed. Then, 30 μL of 38% hydrochloric
acid was added into the solution to inhibit the hydrolysis of the metal ions
(Cu, In or Ga), and subsequently 0.535 g CuCl (99.99%, Aladdin), 1.267 g
InCl3·4H2O (99.99%, Aladdin), and 0.507 g GaCl3 (99.99%, Aladdin) were
added at one time. The mixture was further stirred at 65 oC until all solutes
were completely dissolved and the CIGS precursor solution (without
K) was obtained. For K doping, 0.0041, 0.0065, or 0.0079 g KCl
(99.99%, Aladdin) was added into the above solution to attain three pre-
cursor solutions with K/Cu molar ratios of 0.010, 0.015, and 0.020,
respectively.
Absorber Fabrication: The precursor solution was ﬁltered with a 0.45 μm
PES ﬁlter and spin coated on molybdenum-coated soda lime glass sub-
strate at 2000 rpm for 30 s. The wet ﬁlm was immediately annealed at
300 /C14C for 60 s. The coating and annealing circle was repeated 25 times
to form the CIGS precursor ﬁlm. The fabrication was carried out in ambi-
ent air. The CIGS precursor ﬁlm was then placed in a graphite box con-
taining 0.39 g selenium and selenized in a tube furnace under an argon
atmosphere of 0.16 MPa at 555 /C14C for 25 min.
Solar Cell Fabrication: The selenized ﬁlms were immediately immersed
in 20% (NH4)2S aqueous solution (AR, Aladdin) for 15 min to etch possi-
ble impurities. Then, 50 nm CdS buffer layer, 50 nm i-ZnO, and 250 nm
ITO window layer were successively deposited via chemical bath deposi-
tion (CBD)[7,9] and sputtering. The top-contact Ni/Al grids were fabricated
on top of the window layer through a mask by thermal evaporation. The
device area was deﬁned by mechanical scribing with an active area about
0.10 cm2.
Characterization: XRD spectra (2θ scan) were collected by a Siemens
D5005 X-ray powder diffraction system using Cu Kα (λ ¼ 1.5406 Å)
X-ray as the source at a scan rate of 6/C14 min/C01. The Raman spectra were
acquired on Renishaw inVia microscope using a 532 nm laser diode as the
excitation source. The ﬁlm morphology and elemental composition analy-
sis were measured on Hitachi S4800 SEM. XPS studies were performed
using a KRATOS Axis Supra with a monochromatized Al anode X-ray
source. Atomic contents were extracted from peak areas taking into
account sensitivity factors. The current density–voltage ( J–V ) curves of
the solar cells were measured at room temperature under
100 mW cm/C02 AM1.5 simulated sunlight using a Keithley 2400 source
meter. The EQE was measured on Enlitech QE-R3018 using calibrated
Si and Ge diodes (Enli technology Co. Ltd.) as reference.
