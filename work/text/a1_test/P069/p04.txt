of a conjugated charged system necessary for the formation of
dipole systems.
Equal chemical nature of the cationic sites, as well as counter
anions in PEI and PAMAM G1 and G2 (i.e., protonated amino/
methanolate systems), allows for qualitative interpretation of the
PV results. In this sense, the total number of amino sites per
monomer/macromolecule will be used as the main difference
between dipole layers. For instance, branched PEI is generally
drawn to have four amino sites per monomer. Dendrimers in
contrast form hyper-branched treelike structures with increasingly
higher surface amino sites per generation. For example, PAMAM
G1 has eight amino sites per macromolecule, while for PAMAM G2
the number of amino sites is doubled to 16 per macromolecule (see
Figure 4). PV characterization has shown that as the number of
amino sites per monomer or molecule increases, a signiﬁcant
increase in Voc, ﬁll factor (FF), and in general a signiﬁcantly higher
efﬁciency is obtained compared to the reference sample. This can
be linked to the Helmholtz equation[26]:
Δ ¼ 1 ε · N · p (3)
The Helmholtz potential of the ﬁlm is directly proportional to the
number of dipoles (N) and dipole moment (p)andinversely
proportional to the dielectric constant (ε). As the dipole is formed
by the amino/ethanolate-conjugated system, the change in dipole
potential is in essence driven by the number of dipoles of the ﬁlm.
This number will also depend on packing factor of monomers/
molecules. Nevertheless, dipole potential is increased by a factor
2 as the dendrimer generation is increased.
