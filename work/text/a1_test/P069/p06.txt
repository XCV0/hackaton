momentum molecules to approach the Voc limits of the light
absorber. Furthermore, these alternative CTL structures mini-
mize parasitic absorption, avoiding the need of harmful and
costly dopants to provide quasi-Fermi level splitting. This
increased Voc has been postulated to stem from the increased
dipolar moment that tunes the energetics at the ETL/absorber
interface and thus improves on contact selectivity in contrast with
the use of the standard unfunctionalized oxide ETL. Further
studies will try to pinpoint the origin of this improvement to con-
ﬁrm the mechanism that enables the Voc increase. Furthermore,
the use of ultrathin a-Si:H absorber layers has allowed wave-
length selectivity in the UV–blue range, providing promising
results in terms of efﬁciency. In summary, this approach
presents an interesting route for the improvement of devices
reducing Voc deﬁcit with the introduction of an interfacial layer,
offering alternative applications and an efﬁcient power source for
low-power devices, with the potential to expand the possibilities
in the ﬁeld of PVs.
The high Voc was particularly noteworthy for devices
with FTO/AZO/G2-PAMAM/ZnO/a-Si:H(30 nm)/V2O5/AZO
architecture, which demonstrated the highest value, close to
Voc = 640 mV, a PCE of 1.5%, and AVT of 40%. To the best
of our knowledge, this result yields a record PCE when compared
to state-of-the-art devices that take this approach toward
UV-selective TPV using oxide-based CTLs. This compares with
a reference device without any dipolar molecules showing
PCE = 0.8% with Voc = 410 mV only. We have also observed that
the Voc increases proportionally with the generation of the
PAMAM dendrimer (i.e., proportional to the magnitude of dipole
moment).[27] These encouraging results bring the devices closer
to their fundamental maximum Voc, around 900 mV, as already
achieved in opaque PV for p–i–n a-Si:H solar cells while avoiding
the use of doped a-Si:H CTLs.
These results demonstrate the potential of molecular dipoles
to signiﬁcantly improve the performance of TPV solar cells[28]
and pave the way for the development of cost-effective and
aesthetically pleasing TPV devices for distributed energy
applications.
4. Experimental Section
Sample Preparation: Transparent solar cells (fabricated in this work
present the structure SLG/FTO/AZO/(dipole)/ZnO/a-Si:H/V2O5/AZO.
Commercial SLG/FTO substrates (Merck; Rsh = 10 Ω sq/C01) were used
as substrate and transparent back-contact. Prior to deposition, substrates
were rinsed in distilled water, dried in Ar ﬂux and afterward cleaned by
subsequent 10 min ultrasonic baths in isopropanol and ultrapure H2O
at room temperature, followed by a drying step in Ar ﬂux and concluding
with a 5 min O3 treatment. A 75 nm AZO ﬁlm was grown in an ALD system
(Savannah S200, Cambridge Nanotech) at 135 °C, using precursors dieth-
ylzinc (DEZ 95%, STREM), ultrapure H2O, and trimethylaluminum (TMA
99%, STREM) and using N2 as purging gas. A total of 21 supercycles at a
19:1 DEZ:TMA ratio were performed, with 19 H2O/N2/DEZ/N2 (20 ms/
3 s/15 ms/3 s) cycles and 1 H2O/N2/TMA/N2 (50 ms/1 s/20 ms/5 s)
cycle. After the AZO deposition, the dipolar molecules studied were depos-
ited on top (except for the reference device, without any dipolar ﬁlm). For
the PEI ﬁlm, fresh solutions of PEI (Merck; 20%) dissolved in methanol
(0.01 wt%) were spin coated at 5000 rpm for 50 s and immediately after
were transferred to a hot plate at 90 °C to remove any solvent. This method
yielded a 2 nm ﬁlm. For the PAMAM ﬁlms, fresh solutions of PAMAM
(Merck; 20 wt%) were prepared dissolving PAMAM in methanol
(0.01 wt%). This was done identically for both G1- and G2-PAMAM.
These solutions were spin coated at 5000 rpm for 50 s and immediately
transferred to a hot plate at 90 °C to remove any solvent. The following
step consisted in depositing an interfacial ZnO ﬁlm of 8 nm, which helped
making a better contact with the absorber/ETL and also served as a cap-
ping or protection layer so that the dipolar ﬁlms were not degraded in the
heat and plasma that the ﬁlms would see in the plasma enhanced chemical
vapor deposition (PE-CVD) of a-Si:H. Afterward, intrinsic a-Si:H ultrathin
ﬁlms of 30 nm were deposited by PE-CVD (Elettrorava) with a radio fre-
quency power supply of 13.56 MHz at 6 W. Substrate temperature was
measured at 220–250 °C during deposition, with a mixed 36/12 sccm
SiH4/CH4 atmosphere and with deposition time of 4 min, respectively.
To ﬁnish the solar cells, the HTL and front contact were deposited in a
single sequential ALD process without breaking vacuum. V2O5 was grown
at 165 °C, using tetrakis(ethylmethylamino)-vanadium (IV) (C12H32N4V)
(TEMAV 99%, STREM) and ultrapure H2O using again N2 as carrier
and purging gas. Immediately after 125 supercycles H2O/N2/TEMAV/
N2 (2 s/3 s/3 s/3 s), without breaking vacuum and keeping T = 165 °C,
AZO was deposited at a 19:1 ratio for 75 supercycles. Once the sample
was fully grown, the active area cells were isolated using a photolitho-
graphic process followed by a wet chemical etching process that effectively
isolated the cells from each other. First, photoresist at room temperature
was spread on the surface of the samples and was statically spin coating
for 30 s at 30.000 rpm. Immediately after, it was taken to a furnace at
105 °C for 20 min to evaporate the residual solvent. Then, UV photolithog-
raphy was performed with a positive mask with square patterns deﬁning a
cell active area of 0.20 cm2. Afterward, a two-step etching process was per-
formed. The ﬁrst stage used HCl at 10% to remove the AZO/V2O5 layer.
The ﬁnal etching used an isotropic etching solution of HF þ HNO3 to
attack and remove the rest of the stack (a-Si:H/ZnO/dipole/AZO) down
to the FTO, which remained unaffected. As a result, the active area pro-
tected with the resin would remain and the subcells would be effectively
isolated from each other allowing for J–V characterization without the need
of a mask.
Characterization: Spectrophotometry measurements were carried out
with a UV–vis–NIR spectrophotometer (Perkin Elmer Lambda L950) with
an integrating sphere, in single-beam conﬁguration, in transmittance and
reﬂectance mode with spectral range from 300 to 800 nm to measure the
completed device total transmittance and reﬂectance. This data was also
used to calculate the device APT, CRI, and CIE color coordinates and color
map. J–V measurements under illumination were carried out using an AAA
class solar simulator with AM1.5G illumination calibrated using an
National Renewable Energy Laboratory-certiﬁed Si reference solar cell
(Abet Technologies Model 15 150). Electrical measurements were carried
out with a source-measure unit (Keithley SMU 2400) in four-wire sense
mode, remotely controlled by the software Tracer (ReRa solutions) using
an IEEE 488 GPIB instrument control device (National Instruments GPIB-
USB-HS). Voc–T measurements were carried out in the same manner.
Temperature was controlled using a Linkam system and measurements
were carried out from 300 K down to 100 K in temperature intervals of
25 K. Spectral response measurements were acquired using a spectral
response system (Bentham PVE300) calibrated with Si and Ge
photodiodes. For optoelectronic measurements the samples were placed
in a dark opaque matt to avoid any back-reﬂections that could alter the
characterization of the transparent solar cells.
