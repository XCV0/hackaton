We begin by analyzing the optical properties of the completed
devices and later continuing with the different optoelectronic
characterizations performed.
2.1. Optical Characterization and Current Density–Voltage and
Voc–T Characteristics of TPV Devices
In this section, the complete device structure is characterized
by measuring their reﬂectance and transmittance by optical spec-
trophotometry. The complete devices were fabricated with the
same conditions using different dipole layers in the architecture
including PEI and dendrimer PAMAM Generation 1 (G1) and
Generation 2 (G2) leaving the rest of the structure unchanged.
The measurement allows the determination of the transmittance
spectra of complete TPV devices, which will serve as input for
calculation of average photopic transmittance (APT) and color
rendering index (CRI).
The optical measurements of reﬂectance and transmittance
for the completed devices were conducted in the wavelength
range of 300–800 nm as depicted in Figure 2. These spectra were
employed to determine the values of APT and CRI, as per the
equations provided in the methodology outlined by Traverse
et al.[20] All these parameters are summarized in Table 1.
From the transmittance measurements, it has been possible
to calculate the CIE color coordinates. The coordinates as well
as the CIE color maps are provided in Figure S7, Supporting
Information. As a standard in TPV characterization, from optical
measurements and external quantum efﬁciency (EQE) measure-
ments, we provide the photon balance check graphs in Figure S8,
Supporting Information. These measurements conﬁrm no over-
estimation of generated photocurrent.

An essential factor in enhancing the performance of transpar-
ent devices is bifaciality (see Figure S1–S4 and Table S1,
Supporting Information). This means that solar cells can gener-
ate close to the same efﬁciency regardless of the incident direc-
tion of light, even harnessing the energy reﬂected from surfaces.
This allows us to consider superstrate conﬁgurations, which
could be advantageous when depositing them on various
substrates and streamlining processes. This becomes especially
signiﬁcant when considering industrial scalability for the next
generations of TPV technology. Stability measurements were
performed and conﬁrmed excellent stability for the devices
(Figure S5, Supporting Information).
