The integration of dipoles[12] has been explored in organic
electronic devices, such as organic solar cells,[13,14] organic
light-emitting diodes and ferroelectric materials,[15] to enhance
carrier injection, and improve charge-carrier mobility.[16] In solar
cells and other electronic devices, careful incorporation of dipoles
through material selection, deposition techniques, and device
engineering optimizes their performance.[17] Different research
works have evidenced that they serve as passivation layers on
solar cell surfaces, reducing charge carrier recombination and
improving efﬁciency.[18,19] Furthermore, they facilitate charge
separation in solar cells, leading to more efﬁcient charge extrac-
tion and power generation. Ongoing research continues to
explore innovative ways to integrate dipoles, pushing the bound-
aries of efﬁciency and performance further in these devices. As
for inorganic solar cells, there has been less research on the
matter, although it seems a very promising approach in order
for photovoltaic (PV) devices to meet their theoretical limits
and maximize PCE especially by maximizing their open-circuit
voltage (Voc).
In this work, different molecular dipoles have been imple-
mented into device architectures, speciﬁcally at the electron-
transport layer (ETL)/absorber interfaces of a-Si:H/oxide TPV
devices. These devices have been characterized and compared
against a reference structure without the interfacial dipoles.
The reference device architecture consists of soda lime glass
(SLG)/ﬂuorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/aluminum-doped zinc
oxide (AZO)/ZnO/a-Si:H/V2O5/AZO and the studied devices
incorporate molecular dipoles embedded as an interfacial nano-
metric ﬁlm between the AZO and ZnO ﬁlms. TPV characteriza-
tion of these devices has been carried out. Speciﬁcally, the
temperature-dependent Voc characteristics have been studied
and suggest that the Voc improvement can be correlated to the
dipolar moment of the interfacial layer at the electron-selective
contact. Additionally, scanning transmission electron microscopy
and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (STEM–EDX) measure-
ments have been carried out. Optical characterization has con-
ﬁrmed the high transparency of devices and that the
incorporation of interfacial dipoles has negligible effect on the
optical characteristics of the devices. This research is potentially
relevant for the advancement of thin-ﬁlm TPV technologies as well
as for conventional PV technologies, and other technologies in
general that rely on advanced CTL/active layer/CTL architectures.
2. Results and Discussion
Planar ultrathin TPV devices are fabricated with the following
architecture: SLG/FTO/ETL/a-Si:H/V2O5/AZO. The most inno-
vative focus of this study consists in the introduction of molecu-
lar dipoles at the ETL. A reference device is prepared using an
ETL composed of AZO/ZnO and in parallel samples are fabri-
cated intercalating molecular dipoles AZO/dipole/ZnO. Three
different molecules are studied: a branched conjugated polymer,
polyethyleneimine (PEI), and two different generations (G1, G2)
of the dendrimeric macromolecule poly(amidoamine) (PAMAM)
(Figure 1). For the different samples, AZO, ZnO, and V2O5 ﬁlms
have been deposited in parallel in a single process to be able to
make accurate comparisons focusing on the effect of the dipolar
molecule incorporation. In this case, a 30 nm a-Si:H ﬁlm
is deposited while a hole-transport layer (HTL)/TCO stack of
V2O5/AZO is deposited on top to complete the cells
(Figure 1). It is relevant to stress that these devices are made
without any catalogued critical raw material and at relatively
low temperatures, being 220 °C the maximum process tempera-
ture used for the deposition of a-Si:H. Additionally, the different
