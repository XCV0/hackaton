The steady-state PL experiments are conducted to verify the
enhancement of hole extraction ability. Fig. 4d shows the PL spec-
tra of pristine perovskite ﬁlm and different graphene/perovskite
ﬁlms deposited on glass. Compared with pristine perovskite ﬁlm,
the perovskite ﬁlm with NG and Ni-NG layer not only quenches
PL by 67% and 90% respectively at approximately 769 nm, but also
shows a slight blue-shift induced by the decrease of charge trap
sites [29,47]. The stronger PL quenching with a blue-shift indicates
that the Ni-NG can improve hole extraction from perovskite to car-
bon electrode efﬁciently due to the reduction of trap-induced
charge recombination.
To evaluate the photovoltaic performance of composite elec-
trode based on Ni-NG (Ni-NG/CE), pristine carbon electrode (CE)
and composite electrode based on NG (NG/CE) are fabricated under
identical conditions as references. The current density–voltage (J-
V) curves of the PSCs under illumination of AM 1.5 (100 mW
cm/C02) are presented in Fig. 5a, and the corresponding parameters
are summarized in Table 2. The CE-based PSCs obtain an average
PCE of 8.71%, with a Voc of 0.87 V, a Jsc of 18.99 mA cm/C02 and a
FF of 52.81%. For NG/CE-based PSCs, an enhanced PCE of 9.32% is
achieved. Furthermore, when Ni-NG is introduced into composite
electrode, the PSCs show a large improvement on the photovoltaic
performance. Obviously, Ni-NG/CE can bring about a comprehen-
sive promotion in the performance mainly due to the increased
Jsc and FF. It appears that Ni doping provides abundant chemical
active sites in graphene, which improves the conductivity of gra-
phene and enhances the hole extraction efﬁciency at the interface
between perovskite and composite electrode. As Fig. S4 shows, the
Jsc density of PSCs based on Ni-NG/CE is slightly enhanced from
21.06 mA cm/C02 to 21.29 mA cm/C02 when scan rate is increased from
50 mV/s to 200 mV/s. However, the Jsc density of PSCs based on CE
is signiﬁcantly improved from 17.86 mA cm/C02 to 19.09 mA cm/C02
under the same varied scan rate. The dependence on the scan rate
indicates a low charge collection efﬁciency in the PSCs based on CE
at slow scan rate, which indicates that Ni-NG is beneﬁcial to charge
extraction and transport. The results are also in good agreement
with steady-state PL spectra in Fig. 4d. Moreover, Ni doping con-
centration plays a key role in the photovoltaic performance of PSCs
based on Ni-NG/CE. It can be found that the maximum of PCE is
obtained in PSCs based on 4-Ni-NG/CE, which reach an average
PCE of 10.90% and a champion PCE of 11.74%, exhibiting a Voc of
0.89 V, a Jsc of 20.79 mA cm/C02 and a FF of 59.18%. When the Ni dop-
ing concentration is changed from 1 /C2 10-4 to 4 /C2 10-4 mol/g, the
average PCE of devices is enhanced from 9.55% to 10.90%. The
improvement is mainly due to the optimal hole extraction ability.
In addition, the nitrogen and oxygen content in Ni-NG (4 /C2 10-4
mol/g) is slightly high, which could enhance interfacial chemical
interaction between perovskite and composite electrode to
improve the hole extraction at the interface [48,49]. The average
PCE drop to 10.35% when the Ni doping concentration further
comes to 8 /C2 10-4 mol/g, which may be related to the agglomera-
tion of Ni atoms. The excessive Ni atoms easily form the clusters of
Ni metal, which hinders the uniform dispersion of Ni single-atom
active sites and reduces the hole mobility. To conﬁrm the device
performance from J-V measurement, the steady-state photocurrent
and PCE output of PSCs based on Ni-NG/CE is shown in Fig. 5b.A
stabilized PCE of 10.3% with a corresponding photocurrent density
of 17.2 mA cm/C02 is obtained, which aligns with the value from J-V
data. The fast photocurrent response speed and stabilized output
performance indicate the device is stable under continuous light
conditions. The statistical photovoltaic parameters of PSCs based
