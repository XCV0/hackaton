Ni-NG/CE. Standard PSCs with unmodiﬁed carbon paste electrodes
were prepared as the reference. Because the quality of the per-
ovskite layer is susceptible to environmental temperature, humid-
ity and other factors during the preparation process, the efﬁciency
of standard PSCs in each experiment is slightly different. As shown
in Fig. S5 and Table S1, with Ni-NG dispersion concentration
increasing from 1 mg/mL to 5 mg/mL, the average PCE of devices
drop from 11.15% to 8.87%. This result demonstrates that the Ni-
NG agglomerates formed in high concentration dispersion are
harmful for improving the photovoltaic performance of PSCs. Gra-
phene layer stacking can affect the contact between perovskite and
Ni-NG/CE, thus reducing the hole extraction efﬁciency and causing
the accumulation of charges at the interface. Apparently, based on
the optimal Ni doping concentration of 4 /C2 10-4 mol/g, the PSCs are
further optimized by adjusting the Ni-NG dispersion concentra-
tion, reaching a champion PCE of 12.39%. As shown in Table S2,
the devices based on Ni-NG/CE show a ﬁne performanc in
carbon-based PSCs [52].
EIS measurements are used to investigate interfacial charge
transport properties and recombination behaviors of PSCs based
on reference electrode and composite electrode. Fig. 6a shows
the Nyquist plots in the frequency range of 10 MHz to 0.1 Hz
at an applied bias of 0.95 V under dark condition, and the
enlarged high-frequency region is presented in Fig. 6b. Only a sin-
gle distinct semicircle is observed, which may be attributed to the
nearly zero interface contact capacitance [50]. The traditional
equivalent circuit model composed of resistance, chare transfer
resistance and charge recombination resistance gives the worse
ﬁtting degree to experimental data, so the equivalent circuit sim-
pliﬁed to ﬁt the test results is depicted in the inset of Fig. 5a. The
series resistance (Rs) is usually related to the charge transport
resistance in the device, and recombination resistance (Rrec) can
reﬂect charge recombination ability [51]. EIS measurements at
applied bias from 0.75 V to 0.95 V provide more information
about Rs and Rrec of PSCs by ﬁtting corresponding Nyquist plots.
As shown in Fig. 6c and d, the PSCs based on composite electrode
exhibit a relatively lower Rs and a higher Rrec than reference elec-
trode, which indicates Ni doping can improve charge transport
and reduce charge recombination, thereby realizing an enhance-
ment in Jsc and FF. Notably, when the Ni doping concentration
increases from 1 /C2 10-4 to 8 /C2 10-4 mol/g, Rs of PSCs continuously
decrease. However, PSCs based on composite electrode with Ni
doping concentration of 4 /C2 10-4 mol/g reach the highest Rrec,
which suggests the presence of Ni single-atom active sites can
suppress the charge recombination due to improvement in the
hole extraction.
