1. Introduction
Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) as promising candidate for com-
mercial photovoltaic applications have attracted intense attention
due to their strong light absorption, high carrier mobility, and long
carrier diffusion length [1–3]. Recently, the certiﬁed power conver-
sion efﬁciency (PCE) of PSCs has been increased over 25% by devel-
oping new functional materials, improving device preparation and
optimizing compositional/interfacial engineering [4–9]. However,
the commonly used hole transport material (HTM) (e.g., Spiro-
OMeTAD) and counter electrode (e.g., Au) of PSCs are expensive,
and the migration of lithium ions in the Spiro-OMeTAD also limits
the long-term stability of the devices. All these disadvantages
greatly hinder the commercial development of PSCs in the future
[10–13]. It is urgent to develop a superior HTM with low cost
and long stability. The carbon materials are thought to be the most
promising candidate as counter electrode materials owing to their
low cost, rich content, excellent electronic property and stable per-
formance, which are beneﬁcial to the preparation of devices with
high stability and commercial application [14,15]. Therefore, a
HTM-free carbon based PSCs were developed, which demonstrates
a good hole-transporting ability and stable performance [16,17].
In theory, when the light shines on the perovskite absorber, the
photo-generated holes are directly extracted from perovskite to
carbon electrode in HTM-free carbon based and the surface defects
of perovskite can induce serious recombination, resulting in the
reduction of hole extraction efﬁciency and degradation of photo-
voltaic performance[18]. To solve this problem, great efforts have
been made to enhance the hole extraction capability for carbon
materials [18–24]. Some novel carbon materials such as carbon
nanotubes (CNTs) and graphene have been widely used as carbon
electrodes [25–29]. CNT-based PSCs fabricated by polymethyl
methacrylate (PMMA) over-coating have obtained a decent power
conversion efﬁciency of 11.8% [30]. In addition, chemically doping
heteroatoms into the carbon structure is regarded as an effective
way to enhance the electrical conductivity of carbon materials,
reinforce the interface contact and improve energy level matching
between carbon electrode and perovskite [31,32]. For example,
Zheng applied boron-doped carbon nanotubes (B-MWCNTs) as
the carbon electrode, which improved the PCE of devices from
10.70% to 14.60% [33]. Zhu prepared PSCs with conﬁguration of
FTO/bl-TiO2/mp-TiO2/MAPbI3/nitrogen-doped graphene (N-GF),
obtaining a high PCE of 10.32%. The incorporation of N into gra-
phene lattice reduced the work function of graphene from
5.22 eV to 4.92 eV, which effectively increased the energy level dif-
ference with perovskite and resulted in a large excess energy for
the charge transfer [34]. Besides, metal atom doping can greatly
improve the electrical conductivity of carbon electrode. Wei intro-
duced Na-embedded carbon nanotubes (Na@C) into carbon elec-
trode, which possess high conductivity and large speciﬁc surface
area, reaching a PCE of 8.89% [35].
Recently, some researchers have developed a new kind of
single-metal-atom doped carbon material by designing coordina-
tion sites on the surface of the carbon material, which can maxi-
mize the utilization efﬁciency of metal atom, possess ultra-high
density of active sites and improve the charge transport efﬁciency
[36,37]. Metal-nitrogen-doped carbon material have exhibited
remarkable catalytic performances in the ﬁeld of electrocatalysis,
which provided us another promising idea for the application of
this carbon material in PSCs. Herein, a novel composite electrode
by coupling commercial carbon electrode with Ni-N-doped gra-
phene (Ni-NG) is constructed, in which Ni single atom is uniformly
distributed as active site. N atoms replace partial carbon atoms in
graphene lattice due to their similar size, and Ni atoms are incor-
porated by forming the coordination bonds with N atoms. In this
architecture, Ni-NG with a large speciﬁc surface area can realize
a ﬁrm contact with the perovskite to facilitate the hole extraction.
Meanwhile, the presence of single Ni atom results in an efﬁcient
reduction in work function by changing the electronic structure
of graphene, which is conﬁrmed to enhance the hole extraction
from the perovskite and thus suppress the electron-hole recombi-
nation. As a result, by optimizing Ni doping concentration (4 /C2 10-4
mol/g) and dispersion concentration of Ni-NG (1 mg/mL), PSCs
with composite Ni-NG based electrode achieve a champion PCE
of 12.39%, higher than their counterpart with pristine carbon/com-
posite electrode based NG. In this paper, it’s ﬁrst time using single-
atom Ni doped graphene as carbon electrode of PSCs, which shows
great potential to develop effective and stable carbon electrode for
practical application. Our work also provides new insights into the
design of carbon electrode architecture for HTM-free carbon-based
PSCs.
2. Experimental section
2.1. Synthesis of Ni-NG
Ni-NG was prepared by liquid-phase impregnation [38]. First,
graphene oxide (GO) was synthesized by Hummer method. 2 g gra-
phite powder and 12 g potassium permanganate was carefully
added into the mixture of 120 mL concentrated sulfuric acid and
13.3 mL phosphoric acid. After the mixture slowly stirring for
12 h, hydrogen peroxide solution was added into the mixture until
there was no precipitation at the bottom of the beaker, then the
ﬁnal solution was centrifuged repeatedly and freeze-dried to
obtain graphene oxide powder. After that, 200 mg graphene (GO)
was dispersed in 400 mL deionized water. Subsequently the GO
solution was stirred for 1 h and ultrasonically dispersed for 6 h
until obtaining a homogeneous dispersion without precipitation.
Then, additional Ni species was introduced in graphene through
incipient impregnation of Ni(NO3)2 solution. Different volumes of
Ni(NO3)2 (12.5 mM) solution was slowly added to GO dispersion
under sonication to form the precursor solution with different Ni
doping concentration. The precursor solution was lyophilized to
give a GO foam containing Ni ions. Finally, the GO foam was
annealed at 900 /C176C for 2 h at a heating rate of 4.0 /C176C min/C01 under
Ar (100 sccm) and NH3 (100 sccm) to obtain the Ni-NG material.
The reductive annealing procedure can simultaneously achieve
the atomically dispersed Ni doping, avoiding large aggregated Ni
particles due to abundant nitrogen dopants serving as binding sites
[39,40]. For comparison, the Ni-free N-doped graphene, denoted as
NG, was prepared by the reductive annealing without adding Ni
(NO3)2 solution.
2.2. Preparation of composite electrode
Ni-NG and NG were ﬁrst dispersed in 2 mL chlorobenzene, fol-
lowed by stirring vigorously and sonication to form a uniform dis-
persion. Then 40 lL dispersion was spin coated onto the substrate.
Finally, a commercial carbon paste was doctor bladed on the top of
the Ni-NG and NG layer to construct the composite carbon elec-
trode (Ni-NG/CE and NG/CE). After that, dried at 100 /C176C for 1 h.
Pristine carbon electrode (CE) was prepared by the doctor blade
method without the graphene layer as reference.
