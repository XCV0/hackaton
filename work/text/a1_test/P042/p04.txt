To  investigate  the  relationship  between  VO generation  and
degradation of the PVK films, XPS measurements were conducted
on  the  bottom  surface  of  the  PVK  films  without  and  with  MOF
modification (Figs. 2(d)–2(f)). By comparing the O 1s spectra of the
control  and  the  MOF-modified  perovskite  films  after  aging,  it  is
found that a new peak at 530.7 eV for the control sample appears.
This peak is attributed to the migration of oxygen from SnO2 to the
buried  interface  of  the  perovskite  during  the  aging  process.  As  the
heating  process  continues,  VO formation  in  SnO2 is  further
promoted.  However,  in  the  MOF-modified  samples,  the
dissociation of oxygen from the SnO2 lattice is hindered due to the
interactions between the MOF and SnO2, as well as the electrostatic
effects  of  the  MOF.  This  leads  to  the  inhibition  of  VO formation
and  prevents  the  migration  of  oxygen  to  the  perovskite  film
interface.
The  inhibition  of  I  and  O  migration  by  the  MOF  can  be
attributed to the interactions between the functional groups on the
MOF and the perovskite layer, as evidenced by the FTIR and NMR
results.  In  consistent  with  these  findings,  XPS  of  the  I  3d  orbitals
(Fig.  S7  in  the  ESM)  shows  typical  double  peaks  at  630.5  and
619.9  eV  for  the  control  perovskite  film,  while  the  MOF-modified
sample  displays  peaks  at  630.2  and  618.7  eV  with  slight  deviation.
