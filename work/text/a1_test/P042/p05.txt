layer, thus affecting the iodine bonding environment. Similarly, the
XPS spectra of the Pb 4f orbitals (Fig. S8 in the ESM) show a shift in
the double peaks from 142.0 and 137.1 eV in the control sample to
142.2  and  137.4  eV  in  the  MOF-modified  sample,  indicative  of  an
interaction  between  the  MOF  and  Pb2+ ions.  These  results  further
demonstrate  that  the  MOF  plays  a  crucial  role  in  stabilizing  the
perovskite  film  by  modulating  the  metal–halide  bonding
environment.
To  investigate  whether  the  MOF  itself  functions  as  a  cohesive
whole  to  impede  oxygen  migration,  we  prepared  perovskite  films
by  adding  organic  molecules  with  similar  functional  groups  (2,5-
diaminoterephthalic  acid,  2,5-dimercaptoterephthalic  acid,  and  4-
aminothiophenol).  These  films  were  characterized  by  EPR
spectroscopy. As shown in Figs. S9–S11 in the ESM, the addition of
these  organic  molecules  resulted  in  a  notable  increase  in  oxygen
vacancy  concentration  compared  to  the  MOF-modified  films,
highlighting  the  unique  stability  and  efficacy  of  the  MOF.  The
organic  groups  on  the  MOF  exhibit  excellent  thermal  stability  and
maintain  a  fixed  position  at  the  perovskite  interface,  preventing
diffusion  into  the  perovskite  or  decomposition  under  the
conditions  where  small  organic  molecules  might  degrade.  This
stabilizing  effect  of  the  MOF  contrasts  with  the  organic  molecules,
which  tend  to  diffuse  and  decompose,  leading  to  increased  defect
formation and VO. The migration of oxygen atoms from SnO2 into
the perovskite layer during aging is known to cause the occupation
of  iodine  vacancies  (VI)  and  the  subsequent  migration  of  iodine
ions. The introduction of MOF effectively inhibits the migration of
O  elements,  preventing  them  from  occupying  the  VI,  which
ultimately  enhances  the  long-term  stability  of  PSCs.  The  proposed
action mechanism of the MOF in PSCs is summarized in Fig. 2(g).
To  further  explore  the  role  of  the  MOF  in  the  crystallization  of
perovskite,  we  conducted in-situ ultraviolet–visible  (UV–vis)
absorption  spectroscopy.  As  illustrated  in Figs.  3(a) and 3(b),  the
crystallization  time  of  the  control  perovskite  film  (5.7  s)  is  much
shorter  than  that  of  the  MOF-modified  perovskite  film  (8  s).  This
indicates that the presence of MOF delays the crystallization process
of  the  perovskite  film,  likely  due  to  the  interaction  between  the
MOF  and  the  perovskite  precursor,  which  affects  the  nucleation
and  growth  rate.  SEM  images  of  the  buried  interface  of  the
perovskite films (Figs. 3(c) and 3(d)) reveal that the grain size of the
MOF-modified  perovskite  film  is  slightly  larger  than  that  of  the
control, consistent with the delayed crystallization process observed
by  UV–vis  absorption  (the  grain  size  statistics  can  be  found  from
Figs.  S12  and  S13  in  the  ESM).  The  presence  of  white  spots  in  the
SEM  images  is  attributed  to  excess  lead  iodide,  which  may  be
formed  during  the  crystallization  process  [52].  The  cross-section
SEM images of perovskite films with MOF modification have been
provided in Fig. S14 in the ESM. As seen, the perovskite layer has a
thickness of about 600 nm, suitable for sufficient light absorption.
The  UV–vis  absorption  spectra  of  the  MOF-modified  and
pristine  perovskite  films  (Fig.  S15  in  the  ESM)  indicate  that  the
MOF-modified  perovskite  film  exhibits  a  steeper  absorption  edge,
suggesting that the MOF treatment helps suppress the formation of
defects  within  the  perovskite  layer.  Additionally,  transient
absorption  (TA)  spectra  were  obtained  to  further  investigate  the
effect  of  MOF  on  the  optoelectronic  properties  of  the  perovskite
films.  As  shown  in Figs.  3(e) and 3(f),  a  peak  at  approximately
760  nm  appears  across  a  broad  time  window  (Δt =  1  ps–7  ns),

with  defect  states.  The  ground  state  bleaching  peak  of  the  MOF-
