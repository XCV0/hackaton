treatment  effectively  suppresses  trap-assisted  recombination,
thereby  enhancing  the  photovoltaic  performance  of  the  device.  In
addition,  dark  current  density–voltage  (J–V)  measurements  were
performed  to  detect  the  leakage  current  of  PSCs  (Fig.  4(i)).  The
leakage current of the MOF-modified device was lower than that of
the  control  device,  indicating  reduced  defect  states  and  improved
interface contact. Thus, carrier recombination is suppressed, which
ultimately leads to a higher PCE.
The incorporation of MOF into the perovskite film interface not
only  enhances  the  photovoltaic  performance  but  also  significantly
improves the thermal stability of the perovskite film. XRD analyses
of  perovskite  films  aged  at  85  °C  for  different  durations  were
conducted  to  monitor  the  decomposition  of  the  perovskite
structure.  As  shown  in Figs.  5(a) and 5(b),  the  peak  intensity
corresponding to PbI2 increased with the aging time, indicating the
degradation  of  FAPbI3.  The  integrated  area  ratio  of  the  strongest
peaks  for  PbI2 and  perovskite  was  calculated  to  quantify  the
degradation  of  the  perovskite  films  (Fig.  5(c)).  For  the  MOF-
modified  perovskite  film,  the  initial  PbI2/PVK  ratio  was  0.08%,
compared  to  1.2%  for  the  control.  After  aging  for  10  days,  the
PbI2/PVK  ratio  increased  to  7.43%  for  the  MOF-modified
perovskite film and 11.18% for the control film, demonstrating that
the MOF modification greatly inhibits the degradation process and
enhances  the  thermal  stability  of  the  perovskite  films.  Further
investigations  were  conducted  by  aging  the  control  and  MOF-
modified  perovskite  films  at  85  °C  coupled  with  high  relative
humidity  (RH  =  ~  85%).  As  shown  in Fig.  5(d),  the  control
perovskite  film  turned  yellow,  indicating  the  complete
decomposition  of  perovskite  structure,  while  the  MOF-modified
perovskite  film  retained  its  mostly  black  color,  suggesting  the
preservation of its integrity under harsh conditions. This highlights
the  role  of  MOFs  in  inhibiting  the  degradation  of  perovskite,
contributing to the long-term stability of PSCs. Long-term thermal
stability tests were performed by aging the PSCs in a nitrogen glove
box  at  85  °C  (Fig.  5(e)).  The  MOF-modified  PSCs  (10  pieces)
showed  an  average  peak  PCE  after  300  h  of  thermal  aging,  after
which  the  efficiency  remained  nearly  stable.  After  2000  h  of  aging,
the  average  PCE  was  still  above  100%  of  the  initial  value.  In
contrast,  the  control  devices  reached  their  maximum  efficiency  at
120 h and then exhibited a significant decline, with the average PCE
dropping  to  40%  of  the  initial  value  after  1500  h.  Furthermore,
PSCs  based  on  organic  small  molecules  with  similar  functional
groups exhibited a drastic decline in efficiency, falling below 60% of
the initial PCE (Fig. S22 in the ESM). These results underscore the
substantial improvement in thermal stability of the MOF-modified
PSCs,  making  them  promising  candidates  for  long-term  practical
application.
