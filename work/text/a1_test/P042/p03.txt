circled  white  dots)  have  a  size  in  the  tens  of  nanometers,  and  they
are  uniformly  dispersed  at  the  surface  of  SnO2.  The  slightly
enhanced signals of S, N, O, and Zr elements in the EDS images of
MOF-modified  sample  (Fig.  S5  in  the  ESM)  prove  the  presence  of
MOF.  At  the  same  time,  the  uniform  distribution  of  each  element
in  EDS  images  confirms  the  uniform  dispersion  of  MOFs  without
obvious agglomeration.
Fourier  transform  infrared  (FTIR)  spectra  (Fig.  1(d))  further
reveal  the  characteristic  stretching  vibration  peaks  of  the –NH2
group  at  3350  cm−1 for  the  MOF  and  3333  cm−1 for  the
MOF/formamidinium  iodide  (FAI)  sample,  signifying  interaction
between  the –NH2 groups  of  UiO-66-NH2-SH  and  the  FAI  in  the
perovskite layer. This peak shift suggests the formation of hydrogen
bonds  between  the –NH2 group  of  UiO-66-NH2-SH  and  FAI,
confirming a chemical interaction. In addition, 1H nuclear magnetic
resonance  (NMR)  spectroscopy  (Fig.  1(e))  shows  a  noticeable  shift
of  resonance  signals  in  MOF/FAI  sample  toward  higher  fields,
which  further  supports  the  presence  of  hydrogen  bonding
interactions  (N–H···I)  between  the –NH2 group  and  FAI.  X-ray
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of SnO2 without and with MOF
treatment was tested to evaluate surface interactions. The O 1s XPS
spectra  of  MOF-modified  SnO2 (Fig.  1(f) and  Fig.  S6  in  the  ESM)
show the emergence of a new peak at 530 eV, which is absent in the
pristine  SnO2 sample,  indicating  the  formation  of  S=O  bonds
between  MOF  and  SnO2.  Besides,  a  remarkable  reduction  in  VO
concentration  was  observed  in  the  MOF-modified  SnO2 sample,
indicating  improved  crystallinity  and  fewer  defects  at  the  MOF-
modified  SnO2 surface.  These  results  indicate  that  the  MOF  can
interact  effectively  with  both  the  SnO2 and  perovskite  layers,
passivating  interface  defects  and  inhibiting  the  formation  of  VO at
the SnO2 ETL surface.
To  evaluate  the  effects  of  MOF  treatment  on  interface
enhancement and the thermal stability of PSCs, two distinct PVK-
based  films  were  prepared:  (i)  indium  tin  oxide  (ITO)/SnO2/PVK
and  (ii)  ITO/SnO2/MOF/PVK.  The  electron  spin  resonance  (EPR)
spectra  shown  in Figs.  2(a)–2(c) illustrate  the  comparison  of  VO
concentrations  between  the  two  samples.  Notably,  the  MOF-
modified sample exhibits a slight reduction in VO concentration for
the  fresh  samples.  Upon  aging,  the  VO concentration  in  the
ITO/SnO2/PVK sample is approximately twice as high as that in the
ITO/SnO2/MOF/PVK sample, indicating that the presence of MOF
significantly inhibits the formation of VO during the aging process.
