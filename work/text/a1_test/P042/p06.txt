lead  to  enhanced  optoelectronic  properties  and  reduced
recombination. Furthermore, a slight displacement of the bleaching
peak is observed for both films, which is related to photogenerated
carrier  trapping  at  defect  states.  The  displacement  of  the  control
film is 23.69 meV, while that of the MOF-modified perovskite films
is 18.22 meV [53]. The reduced displacement in the MOF-modified
perovskite  films  indicates  fewer  defect  states  near  the  edge  of  the
energy  band,  which  can  alleviate  the  deficiency  of  open-circuit
voltage  (VOC)  commonly  observed  in  PSCs  with  higher  defect
densities.  These  findings  further  highlight  the  role  of  the  MOF  in
promoting  the  crystallization  of  perovskite  films  and  improving
their optoelectronic performance.
To  further  investigate  the  effect  of  MOF  modification  on  the
charge  transport  properties  of  PSCs,  devices  with  the  structures  of
ITO/SnO2/PVK/[6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric  acid  methyl  ester
(PCBM)/Ag and ITO/SnO2/MOF/PVK/PCBM/Ag were fabricated,
and space charge limited current (SCLC) tests were performed. The
trap-filling  limiting  voltage  (VTFL)  of  the  modified  and  unmodified
perovskite  films  was  measured  to  be  0.11  and  0.42  V,  respectively,
revealing a significant reduction in the defect density in the MOF-
modified films. Based on these measurements, the trap state density
(Ntrap)  was  calculated  to  be  2.1  ×  1015 cm−3 for  the  MOF-modified
films,  compared  to  8  ×  1015 cm−3 for  the  unmodified  films.  The
obviously  reduced  defect  density  in  the  MOF-modified  perovskite
films  is  conducive  to  improving  the  efficiency  of  PSCs,  as  fewer
defects lead to less recombination and improved carrier extraction.
The  energy  level  arrangement  of  the  device,  as  illustrated  in
Fig.  3(h),  was  further  investigated  using  UV  photoelectron
spectroscopy (UPS) and UV–vis absorption spectra (Figs. S16–S19
in  the  ESM).  It  was  found  that  the  conduction  band  minimum
(CBM)  of  MOF-modified  SnO2 is  relatively  higher  than  that  of
SnO2,  which  reduces  the  energy  barrier  and  minimizes  electron
transport losses at the buried interface. This, in turn, promotes the
efficient extraction of photogenerated carriers.
Steady-state  photoluminescence  (PL)  measurements  were
conducted  to  evaluate  carrier  dynamics  in  the  PVK/ETL  films
(Fig. 3(i)). As shown, the PL signals for the MOF-modified films are
significantly lower than those of the unmodified films, indicating a
faster carrier transport rate in the modified films. This is consistent
with  the  reduced  internal  defect  density  observed  from  earlier
characterization,  further  confirming  that  the  MOF  modification
improves carrier mobility. Time-resolved PL (TRPL) measurements
were also carried out, revealing average carrier lifetimes of 135 and
210  ns  for  the  MOF-modified  and  unmodified  perovskite  films,
respectively  (Fig.  3(j)).  The  shorter  carrier  lifetime  in  the  modified
films is indicative of reduced non-radiative recombination, which is
facilitated  by  the  improved  crystallinity  and  reduced  defect
concentration resulting from the MOF modification. This enhances
the  charge  carrier  extraction  and  transport  processes,  further
boosting device performance.
To assess the impact of MOF on the exciton dynamics in PSCs,
transient photovoltage (TPV) and transient photocurrent (TPC) of
PSCs were measured (Figs. 3(k) and 3(l)). The TPV lifetime of the
MOF-modified  PSCs  was  measured  to  be  1231.29  μs,  significantly
longer than the 297.04 µs lifetime of the unmodified PSC. The four-
fold  increase  in  TPV  lifetime  suggests  that  MOF  can  effectively
inhibit exciton recombination caused by defects, thereby enhancing
the  stability  and  performance  of  the  devices.  Conversely,  the  TPC
lifetime of the MOF-modified PSC (0.98 µs) is shorter than that of
the  unmodified  device  (1.60  μs),  which  reflects  a  faster  carrier
transport  in  the  MOF-modified  device.  These  results  indicate  that
the  MOF  modification  not  only  suppresses  exciton  recombination
but  also  accelerates  carrier  transport,  which  is  beneficial  for
improving the optoelectronic performance of PSCs.
As  shown  in Fig.  4(a),  the  PCE  of  the  control  device  under
reverse  scanning  is  22.60%,  with  a VOC of  1.16  V,  a  short-circuit
current  density  (JSC)  of  25.68  mA·cm−2,  and  a  fill  factor  (FF)  of
75.80%.  In  contrast,  the  optimized  MOF-modified  device  exhibits
an  enhanced  PCE  of  25.22%,  with  a VOC of  1.18  V, JSC of
25.54  mA·cm−2,  and  FF  of  83.58%  under  reverse  scanning.  The
hysteresis factor (HI) has been calculated. HI decreases from 9.47%
for  the  control  device  to  4.76%  for  the  MOF-modified  device.  The
results  show  that  MOF  modification  can  significantly  improve  the
charge  transfer  at  the  perovskite  interface.  Furthermore,  PSCs
modified  with  organic  molecules,  such  as  2,5-diaminoterephthalic
acid, 2,5-dimercaptoterephthalic acid, and 4-aminothiophenol, were
also prepared. As shown in Figs. S20 and S21 in the ESM, the PSCs
with  2,5-diaminoterephthalic  acid  and  2,5-dimercaptoterephthalic
acid  achieved  a  champion  PCE  of  22.83%,  while  those  modified
with 4-aminothiophenol achieved a champion PCE of 22.37%, both
comparable  to  the  control.  This  indicates  that  the  MOF  treatment
functions  as  a  stable  and  cohesive  unit,  contributing  to  improved
device  performance.  To  verify  the  reliability  of  the  data,  the
statistical distributions of photovoltaic parameters of several devices
are  shown  in Figs.  4(b)–4(e).  For  the  control  devices,  the  average
PCE  is  22.27%,  with  an  average VOC of  1.16  V,  FF  of  77.07%,  and
JSC of  24.72  mA·cm−2.  For  the  MOF-modified  devices,  the  average
PCE  is  23.49%,  with  an  average VOC of  1.16  V,  FF  of  79.59%,  and
JSC of 25.54 mA·cm−2.
External  quantum  efficiency  (EQE)  measurements  were  also
conducted (Fig. 4(f)). As seen, EQE has a significant improvement
in  the  short  wavelength  band  (<  500  nm),  indicating  the
improvement  of  charge  extraction  at  the  buried  interface.  At  the
same  time,  the  synchronous  improvement  of  EQE  in  the  long
wavelength  band  (500–800  nm)  indicates  the  improvement  of
perovskite  quality  and  the  reduction  of  defect-related
recombination.  The  integrated  current  density  was  calculated,
yielding  a  value  of  24.56  mA·cm−2 for  the  MOF-modified  device.
The  discrepancy  between  the  EQE  and JSC values  is  less  than  5%,
indicating the consistency and reliability of the performance data.

may  be  caused  by  improved  energy  level  alignment  and  an
