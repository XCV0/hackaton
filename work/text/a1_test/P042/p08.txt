formation  of  VO in  SnO2,  and  preventing  the  transformation  of
perovskite  from  its  α  phase  to  the  β  phase.  These  effects  lead  to
substantial  enhancements  in  both  the  thermal  and  environmental
stability  of  the  perovskite  films  and  PSCs.  Additionally,  the
incorporation  of  the  UiO-66-NH2-SH  not  only  passivates  the
interface  defects  between  SnO2 and  perovskite  but  also  promotes
the  crystallization  of  the  perovskite  layer,  leading  to  larger
perovskite  grains  and  more  efficient  charge  transport.  As  a  result,
the  champion  device  incorporating  the  MOF  achieved  an
impressive  PCE  of  25.22%,  significantly  outperforming  the  control
devices.  More  importantly,  the  MOF-modified  devices  exhibited
exceptional  long-term  stability,  retaining  100%  of  their  initial
efficiency  after  2000  h  of  storage  at  85  °C.  In  stark  contrast,  the
control  devices  experienced  a  dramatic  decline  in  performance.
This  remarkable  stability  under  harsh  conditions  underscores  the
pivotal  role  of  the  MOF  in  overcoming  the  stability  challenges
currently faced by PSCs. These findings offer a promising pathway
for  the  development  of  reliable,  high-efficiency  PSCs,  positioning
them for practical applications in the photovoltaic industry.
