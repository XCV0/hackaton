by a complex dynamic among the light-absorber, buffer,
HRMO, and TCO layer at the front contact. Poor material
choice for the HRMO will affect cell stability, result in subopti-
mal band alignment, provide inadequate shunt protection, or
directly generate highly defective interfaces and subsequent
barriers for the diode current or photocurrent.[12a,14/C015]
Device anomalies such as cross-over, roll-over, and kinks in
the dark and light current-voltage ( JV ) curves may arise,
signaling ineffective carrier extraction.[13d,16]
HRMO interfacial layers have only begun to be explored and
implemented within antimony chalcogenide solar cell architec-
tures, wherein only a few HRMO materials have been tested exper-
imentally. SnO2 is the most studied of such interfacial layers in
Sb2Se3 solar cells. In addition to imparting shunt protection, a
SnO2 layer has proven to contribute to a more optimal (front
contact) band alignment and assist in improving the orientation
of Sb2Se3 grains.[8d,10a,17] However, SnO2 layers have resistivities
that are typically too low (0.01–1 Ω cm) to act as an ideal shunt
screening element.[13d] Other metal oxides with a higher resistivity
(>104 Ω cm) and an improved lattice match, such as TiO2 or ZnO,
would be better suited as HRMO interlayers.[13d,18] Studies on
TiO2, ZnO, and Zn(O,S) as interfacial layers have been conducted
in both Sb2Se3 and Sb2(Sex,S1/C0x)3 solar cells. Speciﬁcally, Zn(O,S)/
CdS combination at the front contact has been employed to main-
tain a high shunt resistance in devices with a thinner CdS buffer to
minimize its parasitic absorption.[19] ZnO and TiO2 have been used
to enhance band alignment and device stability in Sb2Se3 and
Sb2(Sex,S1/C0x)3 solar cells with CdS buffer.[20]
Despite the progress provided by these works, there remains a
lack of detailed studies on HRMO materials within antimony chal-
cogenide solar cells other than SnO2. Existing studies into ZnO
and TiO2 HRMOs have been somewhat cursory, focusing mainly
on overall efﬁciency gains. To date, there have been no in-depth
studies focused on the origins of these gains. Most importantly, it
is still unclear what effect these HRMO have on both defect
generation/suppression and the resultant quality of the interfaces
in these solar cells. This shortfall in knowledge within the ﬁeld of
antimony chalcogenide photovoltaics represents an opportunity to
develop and contribute critical knowledge around the role of key
HRMO (window) layers within this system. These investigations
are essential to guide the further development of high-quality front
contacts in these devices and provide rational decisions on the
most optimal window layer to employ in Sb2Se3 solar cells.
Here, we investigate how the deposition of ZnO and TiO2
layers affect the quality of the front contact in Sb2Se3 solar cells
containing a /C2560 nm CdS buffer. The given HRMO was deposited
via reliable and inexpensive solution-processed methods.[18a,20]
The effect of each HRMO on device efﬁciency, Sb2Se3 grain ori-
entation, parasitic resistances, and front contact interface quality is
comprehensively investigated and contrasted. For these studies,
optimized devices were fabricated via vapor transport deposition
(VTD) and analyzed by various techniques, including X-ray diffrac-
tion (XRD), X-Ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), scanning
electron microscope (SEM), impedance spectroscopy, Solar Cell
Capacitance Simulator (SCAPS) simulations, as well as general
current–voltage analyses under various operational conditions.
2. Results and Discussion
The schematic in Figure 1a displays the device architecture used
in this study consisting of ﬂuorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/
(HRMO)/CdS/Sb2Se3/Au. For details on device preparation
and the deposition conditions of the various layers please see
the Experimental Section. Brieﬂy, a sol–gel method was used
to deposit either ZnO or TiO2 HRMO layers on glass/FTO sub-
strates. Subsequently, devices with these layers, as well as control
substrates (glass/FTO), were used for the deposition of a CdS
buffer via chemical bath deposition (CBD, Figure 1b).
Detailed characterization for these layers is reported in the
Supporting Information (Figure S1, S2, Supporting
Information). Next, the Sb2Se3 absorber layer was fabricated
via an optimized vapor transport deposition (Figure S3,
Supporting Information). Finally, gold back contacts were depos-
ited by thermal evaporation to complete the devices.
Impedance spectroscopy is an excellent means to gather initial
insight into the electrical properties and differences in the capac-
itive and resistive behavior of thin-ﬁlm materials within devices.
Figure 1c shows Nyquist plots taken from optimized devices with
either a ZnO or TiO2 HRMO layer compared to CdS-only control
devices (CdS deposited directly on FTO). Devices with an HRMO
layer deposition showed parallel resistances of /C25104 Ω cm2
(TiO2 ¼ 0.54 /C2 104 Ω cm2, ZnO ¼ 2.6 /C2 104 Ω cm2), which is
over two orders of magnitude higher than the control device
(CdS only (no HRMO)¼ 3.1 /C2 102 Ω cm2). This conﬁrms that
the use of ZnO or TiO2 drastically improves the shunt resistance
of our Sb2Se3 solar cells. Notably, the extracted series resistance
remained unaffected with TiO2 ¼ 1.9 Ω cm2, ZnO ¼ 2.0 Ω cm2,
and CdS only (no HRMO) ¼ 2.2 Ω cm2. Consequently, the use
of ZnO or TiO2 improves the device PCE (Figure 1d),
mostly through an enhanced short-circuit current (average JSC
CdS only (no HRMO) ¼ 16.5 mA cm/C02,TiO2 ¼ 18.0 mA cm/C02,
ZnO ¼ 19.6 mA cm/C02). Equally important to overall PCE improve-
ments is the signiﬁcant reduction in the variability of the solar cell
device parameters observed when employing an HRMO
interlayer, including a vastly improved and less variable FF.
Superstrate Sb2Se3 devices clearly receive beneﬁts from the
application of a metal oxide interlayer between the FTO electrode
and CdS. However, unlike in other systems, in the case of Sb2Se3
depositing these layers has further implications on ﬁlm texture.

the c-axis ([002] direction) is shown in Figure 1e. The crystal
structure of Sb2Se3 comprises a covalently bonded 1D
(Sb4Se6)n ribbons running along the c-axis of the crystal that
are weakly held together by van der Waals forces. As highlighted
in Figure 1e charge transport is not favored in [hk0] directions
(such as [120]–orthogonal to the c-axis) due to inefﬁcient carrier
transport between adjacent ribbons. It is well established that an
inverse relationship exists between the degree of (120) orienta-
tion and cell efﬁciency.[4a,7a,21] In light of its 1D crystal structure,

XRD, I(120)/I(221), provides a means to assess the expected current
losses within a thin-ﬁlm device. Although it may be somewhat
