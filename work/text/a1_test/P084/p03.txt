To evaluate device stability with different interlayer architec-
tures, the shunt resistance and efﬁciency of Sb2Se3 devices were
measured over time for devices stored in a dry cabinet (<4% rel-
ative humidity). In Figure 2a, the dark JV curves taken over time
from our best performing solar cells show that a shoulder devel-
ops in the reverse bias region of CdS-only devices, which occurs
due to the reduction in shunt resistance upon aging. No such
changes are observed in the reverse bias region of dark JV curves
with the use of ZnO or TiO2. Figure 2b shows the extracted Rsh
values for these devices. The Rsh of newly prepared devices was
signiﬁcantly more irregular in the case of CdS-only devices. In
addition to their initial variability, the average Rsh of all CdS-only
devices decreased signiﬁcantly (more than two orders of magni-
tude over 100 days of aging) stabilizing at /C25102 Ω cm2. In con-
trast, the average Rsh remained remarkably stable over time
with the use of ZnO and decreased only slightly when TiO2
was used. Furthermore, as shown in Figure 2c, control devices
dropped to /C2580% of their initial efﬁciency after 137 days while
devices with HRMO interlayers remained highly stable, retaining
more than 95% of their initial PCE over the same period.
