Fast nucleation and growth tend to occur at concave locations, resulting
in the formation of small, ﬁne grains. On the other hand, the two pro-
cesses proceed relatively slowly at the convex locations, leading to the
formation of larger, coarser grains. The presence of cracks and pin holes
in MHP ﬁlm creates direct contact between the hole transport layer and
electron transport layer, introducing additional shunt pathways that
exacerbate current leakage and charge recombination, resulting in sig-
niﬁcant energy loss. Additionally, these cracks have a signiﬁcantly
detrimental impact on the mechanical performance of UF-PSCs by
diminishing the bending strength of MHP ﬁlm. DMF prewashing has been
proven to be an effective strategy to improve the wettability of PTAA to
some extent. As shown in Fig. 4c and d, the top-view SEM images of MHP
ﬁlms deposited on DMF prewashed PTAA (DMF PW) exhibit better
coverage with fewer voids and cracks in comparison to the control ﬁlm.
However, the ﬁlms still display crystalline inhomogeneities as the DMF
prewashing does not entirely counteract the challenges of large rough-
ness and insufﬁcient wettability of PTAA. In contrast, the MHP ﬁlm
deposited on the Al2O3 SM substrate displays full coverage over a large
area (Fig. 4e), which is crucial for the preparation of high-performance
devices. To obtain a comprehensive understanding of the crystallinity
enhancement of MHP ﬁlms by Al2O3 SM, we conducted X-ray diffraction
(XRD) and UV–Vis characterizations. The detailed results can be found in
the supporting information (Fig. S7 and Fig. S8). In Fig. S7, the XRD
patterns indicate that, compared to the perovskite ﬁlms on the control
and DMF PW substrate, the perovskite ﬁlm on Al2O3 demonstrate higher
crystallinity, which is also validated by the UV–Vis spectra in Fig. S8.
We conducted further investigations to explore the defect character-
istics of various MHP ﬁlms. The steady-state and time-resolved photo-
luminescence (TRPL) spectra of MHP ﬁlms deposited on different
substrates are presented in Fig. 5a and b. In this comparison, we aim to
identify the inherent trap states in MHP ﬁlms. To achieve this, we
deposited the MHP ﬁlms on different substrates that lack a charge
extraction layer (PEDOT:PSS or PTAA). Compared to the bare PET sub-
strate, both Al2O3 coating and DMF prewashing can enhance wettability
and improve crystallinity, resulting in higher PL intensity. Particularly,
the PL intensity of the Al2O3-based MHP ﬁlm is substantially higher than
that of the control MHP ﬁlm, indicating a noticeable decrease in trap
density and reduced nonradiative energy loss. TRPL spectra were
analyzed to assess the carrier lifetime, which is inﬂuenced by defect
density and distribution in the MHP ﬁlms. Fig. 5b demonstrates that the
carrier lifetime τ of the MHP ﬁlm deposited on the DMF prewashed PET
(τ¼0.85 μs) is approximately twice that of the MHP ﬁlm deposited on
PET (τ¼0.39 μs). Meanwhile, the carrier lifetime (τ¼1.36 μs) of the MHP
ﬁlms deposited on the Al2O3-coated PET is approximately three times
that of the MHP ﬁlm deposited on PET (the ﬁtted carrier lifetime is
summarized in Table S1). The enhancement in carrier lifetime suggests
fewer defects in MHP ﬁlms, which can be attributed to the improvement
in MHP crystallinity resulting from the improvement in wettability.
