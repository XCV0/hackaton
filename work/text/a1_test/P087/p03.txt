their contact angles using, as shown in Fig. 3a. The hydrophobic nature of
PET substrate results in a relatively poor wettability to the mixed solvent
of DMF and DMSO, with a contact angle of 39.9/C14, which can be improved

PEDOT:PSS as HTM: its acidity is unsuitable for MHP ﬁlm deposition, and

accumulation. PTAA is a widely used HTM that is superior to PEDOT:PSS
in terms of hole extraction, but its hydrophobic nature weakens the
wettability of PTAA/PEDOT:PSS/PET compared to PEDOT:PSS/PET.
Covering PTAA with Al2O3 NPs is a good strategy to address this issue,
which can signiﬁcantly reduce the contact angle from 31.1/C14 to 14.7/C14.
In addition to surface wettability, surface roughness is another crucial
factor that requires careful consideration. Unlike the rigid glass substrate,
commercially available ultra-thin ﬂexible PET substrates have a signiﬁ-
cantly large surface roughness. However, this can be reduced through the
sequential deposition of other layers such as PEDOT:PSS, PTAA, and
Al2O3 NPs by spin-coating (Fig. S4). The effect is particularly noticeable
when depositing an Al2O3 NPs layer, which can remarkably reduce the
surface roughness Sa from 723 nm to 348 nm. To ﬁgure out the distri-
bution of Al2O3 NPs on PTAA, the 3D-SLCM was employed to evaluate
the roughness of the PTAA/PEDOT:PSS/PET and generated a 3D
morphological proﬁle, as depicted in Fig. 3d and Fig. S5. Subsequently,
we simulated the distribution of Al2O3 NPs on PTAA by means of sim-
center STAR-CCM þ simulation, as illustrated in Fig. 3e. The Al2O3 NPs
can ﬂatten the rough surface of PTAA/PEDOT:PSS/PET by ﬁlling the
concave region, in turn resulting in a relatively smooth substrate, as
conﬁrmed by the SEM image in Fig. 3c. Meanwhile, we prepared four
samples: PET, PEDOT:PSS/PET, PTAA/PEDOT:PSS/PET, Al2O3/PTAA/
PEDOT:PSS/PET. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) images, illustrated in
Fig. S6, were then captured. The results obtained indicate a lack of cor-
respondence between the observed roughness and morphological
changes and the outcomes of our distribution simulation. The analysis of
the reasons is as follows: Unlike the morphology of conventional devices
(on rigid or normal ﬂexible substrate), the substrates of ultra-ﬂexible
substrate (~10 μm) often display a broad range of undulations. The
AFM characterization method is typically limited to observing micro-
scopic roughness and morphology due to its viewport range constraint.
The relatively more macroscopic undulations cannot be measured by
AFM characterization, resulting in disparities between the roughness
results output by AFM characterization and the simulation results. In our
simulation model, the substrate dimensions exceed 700 μm in length and
width, while the 3D-SLCM offers a viewport range of up to a millimeter.
Therefore, we consider the 3D-SLCM results to provide more compelling
evidence compared to the AFM results. Each image in Fig. S5 has a
viewport range of 1 mm /C2 1 mm.
As aforementioned, the surface properties of these substrates differ
signiﬁcantly. To further analyze the effect of these differences on MHP
formation, we conducted ﬁnite element simulations of spin-coating MHP
precursor solutions on various substrates. The surface energy of each
substrate was obtained through contact angle measurements, while other
substrate parameters regarding surface roughness were determined using
3D-SLCM. The simulation results, which indicate the spreading speed of
the MHP precursor solution on the control and Al2O3 SM substrates, are
clearly presented in Fig. 3f and g. In our simulation, we deﬁne the length
of the viewport occupied by the original droplet as the normalized length
“1". At t¼1.5 s, 1.5 s after the start of spin-coating, the MHP precursor
solution on the control substrate spreads to L¼2.33. However, this then
surprisingly shrinks back to L¼1.83 at t¼2 s (as shown in Fig. 3f). The
difﬁculty in the MHP precursor solution spreading on PTAA is due to the
relatively larger interface energy between PTAA and MHP precursor
solution. It therefore becomes clear that achieving complete coverage of
the MHP precursor solution on an untreated PTAA surface is impossible.
In comparison to the untreated PTAA surface of the control substrate, the
Al2O3 SM substrate has lower interface energy with the MHP precursor
solution. As a result, the spreading speed of the MHP precursor solution
on the Al2O3 NPs-coated PTAA surface is much faster than that on un-
treated PTAA. For instance, by t¼1.5 s, the MHP precursor solution
spreads to L¼4.56 and continues spreading to nearly cover the entire
viewport by t¼2.0 s. It can be concluded that both the reduction in sur-
face roughness and the enhancement of surface wettability contributed to
the facilitated spreading of the MHP precursor solution.
