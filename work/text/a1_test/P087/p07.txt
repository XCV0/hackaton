Fig. 6a shows the device structure and cross-sectional SEM image of a
device fabricated on a glass substrate (for demonstrative purposes only)
because preparing a cross-sectional UF-PSCs sample for SEM character-

thickness of approximately 650 nm, while the PEDOT:PSS layer is only
70 nm thanks to the enhanced conductivity. The Al2O3 thickness is
estimated to be approximately 40 nm. The photovoltaic performance of
our UF-PSCs was evaluated by photocurrent-voltage (J-V) measurement
under AM1.5G, one sun illumination (100 mW cm/C02). To increase the
persuasiveness of our ﬁndings, we meticulously fabricated 30 devices for
each group utilizing distinct substrates. The statistical PCE values of these
devices are presented in Fig. 6b and Table S2. Furthermore, we have
included the J-V curves of the most superior device from each group,
showcasing both forward and backward scanning directions, in Fig. 6c.
When fabricating UF-PSCs on the control substrate, a signiﬁcant number
of devices, exceeding 50%, experience complete short circuits, leaving
only 10–20% of devices with mediocre performance. The highest-
performing device on the control substrate is only able to achieve a
modest PCE of 11.96%. Limited improvement can be observed in the case
of DMF PW-based UF-PSCs, but it is noteworthy that 40% of the devices
still suffer from short circuit issues. While the DMF prewashing treatment
has been utilized to enhance the PCE of rigid p-i-n PSCs, our ﬁndings
suggest that this method falls short in addressing the wetting issues that
arise in the realm of UF-PSCs. Two primary factors contribute to the low
yields and poor repeatability observed in both the control and DMF PW-
based devices. There are two primary factors that contribute to this
challenge. The ﬁrst factor is the presence of numerous pinholes and
cracks, leading to incomplete coverage of the MHP ﬁlms on PTAA. The
second factor is a high degree of surface roughness, which causes the
device electrodes to connect and results in short circuits. Thanks to the
full coverage of MHP ﬁlm, the Al2O3 SM-based devices exhibit excep-
tional reproducibility and do not experience any short circuit problems,
achieving 100% yields and high repeatability. Impressively, the Al2O3
SM-based devices achieve an average PCE of approximately 15%.
Moreover, the most outstanding UF-PSCs in our current investigation
exhibit a JSC of 24.59 mA cm/C02,aVOC of 1.046 V, and a ﬁll factor (FF) of
63.49%, ultimately achieving a remarkable PCE of 16.33%. This PCE is
higher than that of either the DMF PW-based (12.20%) or the control
(11.96%) devices. It is worth noting that the JSC value mentioned above
is consistent with the integrated photocurrent obtained from the external
quantum efﬁciency (EQE) measurement, as shown in Fig. S9.
The hysteresis phenomenon observed in both backward and forward
J-V scanning directions for PSCs has been extensively reported in pre-
vious literature. This phenomenon is associated with multiple factors,
including ion migration, defects, interfacial charge recombination, and
other related mechanisms. Fig. 6c and Table S3 demonstrate that the
degree of hysteresis varies signiﬁcantly among the three champion UF-
PSCs. In comparison to the control and DMF PW-based devices, the
hysteresis of the Al2O3 SM-based devices is negligible. The presence of
cracks and pinholes as well as high defect density in the active layer
(MHP) are major reasons responsible for the signiﬁcant hysteresis
observed in the case of the control or DMF PW-based device. By SCLC
characterization, we calculated that the defect density of states of Al2O3
SM devices is much lower compared to control devices, which is one of
the reasons for the reduced hysteresis. Furthermore, we tested the tran-
sient photovoltage (TPV) decay curves of the optimized ﬁlms and control
devices. As shown in Fig. S10, the Al2O3 SM-based device exhibits a
carrier recombination lifetime that is much higher than that of the con-
trol device. This is in good agreement with the enhanced integrity of the
MHP/PTAA interface and explains the low degree of hysteresis.
For Al2O3 SM devices that exhibit both a low FF, we provide the
following explanation: the FF of a device is primarily determined by the
series resistance, bypass resistance, and characteristics of the PN junc-
tion. An increase in series resistance, a decrease in bypass resistance, and
the presence of defects and impurities within the PN junction, and other
unfavorable conditions, contribute to a reduction in the FF. To achieve
ultra-ﬂexible bending, we utilize PEDOT:PSS as the electrode material,
which offers greater resistance to bending compared to ITO. However, it
should be noted that the conductivity of PEDOT:PSS is inferior to that of
ITO, resulting in a reduced FF for the device. To isolate the impact of
substrate conductivity on the reduced FF, we prepared devices with an
identical structure on ITO substrates. The J-V curves of the ITO device
and ultra-ﬂexible device are presented in Fig. S11. The results demon-
strate that ITO devices can attain a FF of 82% when the reduction in FF
caused by substrate conductivity is excluded.
To evaluate the mechanical durability of our UF-PSCs, we conducted
bending tolerance tests using a bending radius of 1.0 mm and 0.5 mm,
respectively. Following the scheduled bending cycles, we conducted J-V
tests to collect the PV parameters of the UF-PSCs. Fig. 6d and e depict the
normalized PCE of the UF-PSCs as a function of bending cycles. Our re-
sults demonstrate that the PCE of DMF PW-based UF-PSCs decreases to
47.6% (radius¼1 mm) and 58.9% (radius¼0.5 mm) of the initial PCE
after only 500 bending circles, while the control UF-PSCs can maintain
60.2% (radius¼1 mm) and 78.3% (radius¼0.5 mm) of the initial PCE
under the same conditions. This result suggests that the DMF PW-based
UF-PSCs exhibit inferior mechanical properties compared to the control
devices. This could be attributed to non-uniform stress release during
bending. Uneven stress release leads to a quicker fracturing of the ﬁlm,
resulting in a faster decline in PCE. In contrast, the Al2O3 SM-based de-
vices demonstrate impressive bending stability, maintaining 98.6%
(radius¼1 mm) and 79.0% (radius¼0.5 mm) of the initial PCE after
10,000 bends. We postulate that the enhanced contact between the MHP
and PTAA interfaces facilitated by the addition of Al2O3 NPs reinforces
the interfacial mechanical coupling, thereby shielding the ﬁlm from
peeling off the substrate during bending.
3. Conclusion
In summary, we conducted a comprehensive study on the preparation
of UF-PSCs, with a focus on utilizing Al2O3 as a surface modiﬁer to
address a common problem encountered in the ﬁeld of UF-PSCs. Our
investigation encompassed the mechanical properties, morphology, and
semiconductor optoelectronics of MHPs deposited on substrates with
different roughness and wettability. We highlighted the positive effects
of the Al2O3 NPs on improving the crystalline quality of MHP, the
reproductivity, and the PCE of UF-PSCs. By employing this approach, we
achieved a high PCE of 16.33% in UF-PSCs with excellent bending
tolerance. While further improvements in PCE and scalability of large-
area devices are necessary for practical applications, our work provides
valuable insights into the development of ﬂexible photovoltaics. We
believe that our ﬁndings will signiﬁcantly contribute to the advancement
of UF-PSCs research and inspire further exploration in this exciting ﬁeld.
