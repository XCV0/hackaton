regardless of the substrate employed is rigid or ﬂexible [23,24]. The
conductive polymer poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sul-
fonate (PEDOT:PSS, PH 1000) is a popular choice as an electrode for
organic solar cells and PSCs due to its superior ﬁlm processability and
mechanical durability compared to the rigid and brittle ITO electrode. As
PEDOT:PSS (unless otherwise speciﬁed, PEDOT:PSS will refer to
PEDOT:PSS PH 1000) is a p-type organic semiconductor, UF-PSCs typi-
cally are in a p-i-n architecture of Ag/BCP/PCBM/MHP/PEDOT:PSS/PET
[9,15]. Modulation of the conductivity of PEDOT:PSS layer is essential in
the fabrication of high-efﬁciency devices. For example, Vaagensmith
et al. reported the use of additive and plasma treatment to decrease the
sheet resistance of PEDOT:PSS, which then served as a good transparent
electrode for ITO-free PSCs [25]. Hu and coworkers also demonstrated
the use of an ionic additive to improve conductivity, transmittance, and
mechanical endurance [26]. For UF-PSCs, it is not sufﬁcient to use
PEDOT:PSS alone to selectively extract photoinduced holes or block
photoinduced electrons. Therefore, to achieve high performance in
UF-PSCs, it is crucial to use appropriate hole transport materials (HTMs)
such as nickel oxide (NiOx) and poly-bis(4-phenyl) (2,4,6-trimethyl-
phenyl) amine (PTAA) [27–29]. PTAA is one of the most widely used
HTMs in inverted PSCs owing to a lower parasitic absorption and a better
energy band alignment with MHP [7,30,31]. Whereas, PTAA's high
surface energy and subsequent poor wettability may cause discontinuity
or pinholes in the MHP layer [32,33]. Conventional methods to improve
the wettability of MHP ﬁlms on hydrophobic substrates, such as pre-
washing with dimethylformamide (DMF), may be not sufﬁcient as it
usually results in incomplete ﬁlm coverage. To overcome these limita-
tions and achieve high-performance UF-PSCs, alternative methods must
be explored to ensure complete coverage and strong interfacial coupling
of MHP ﬁlms on PTAA for ultra-ﬂexible substrates.
Here, we report the utilization of Al2O3 nanoparticles (NPs) as an
inorganic surface modiﬁer to enhance the wettability and reduce the
roughness of PTAA simultaneously. This strategy enables good afﬁnity
between the substrate and MHP precursor solution, in turn attaining
uniform and dense MHP ﬁlms with full coverage and fewer defects,
which proves essential in fabricating UF-PSCs. Combined with computer
simulations, we systematically investigated the effect of Al2O3 NPs on
spin-coating and ﬁlm formation. This strategy not only signiﬁcantly in-
creases PCE from ~12% to over 16%, but also promotes reproducibility
by effectively addressing the short circuit issue commonly encountered in
the ﬁeld of UF-PSCs. Moreover, our UF-PSCs demonstrates good me-
chanical stability, maintaining 98.6% and 79.0% of their initial PCEs
after 10,000 bending cycles with radii of 1.0 and 0.5 mm, respectively.
2. Results and discussion
As illustrated in Fig. 1, we present the preparation process of UF-PSCs.
Firstly, Ultra-ﬂexible polyethylene terephthalate (UF-PET) was lami-
nated onto a smooth glass surface using Polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) as
an adhesive layer. PEDOT:PSS was then spin-coated onto the PET. To
facilitate the extraction of photogenerated holes, a PTAA layer was spin-
coated onto PEDOT:PSS prior to the deposition of a MHP light-absorbing
layer. Precise control over the deposition of MHP ﬁlms and strong
interfacial coupling with PTAA on ﬂexible substrates is crucial for the
preparation of high performance UF-PSCs. [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid
methyl ester (PCBM) and bathocuproine (BCP) layers were sequentially
deposited by a spin-coating process. The integrated UF-PSCs device was
obtained after the deposition of the Ag electrode, which was ﬁnally
peeled off from the glass substrate. It is important to note that the surface
wettability and roughness of the substrate prior to MHP deposition are
crucial factors in the fabrication of high-efﬁciency UF-PSCs.
As a result of the strong hydrophobic nature of the PTAA surface,
achieving a uniformly coated MHP ﬁlm through direct spin-coating of the
MHP precursor onto the PTAA surface presents a challenge, as depicted
in Fig. 2a. To overcome the challenge of obtaining a uniformly coated
MHP ﬁlm on the hydrophobic PTAA surface, a DMF prewashing strategy
has been frequently employed. However, even after DMF prewashing, the
ﬁlm coverage remains inadequate, as shown in Fig. 2b. The limited af-
ﬁnity of DMF on PTAA restricts the enhancement of wettability, and the
prewashing process with DMF can compromise the glove box atmo-
sphere, negatively impacting subsequent deposition and crystallization
of MHP. To address this issue, a low-temperature processed layer of
Al2O3 NPs was deposited on PTAA. The abundant surface hydroxyl
groups endow Al2O3 NPs with excellent amphiphilic properties, allowing
them to bind not only to PTAA but also to the MHP solution. Conse-
quently, the modiﬁcation with Al2O3 signiﬁcantly enhances the wetta-
bility of the PTAA surface, enabling complete coverage of the substrate
by the MHP solution. This leads to the formation of a high-quality MHP
ﬁlm and ultimately a superior device, as illustrated in Fig. 2c. For sub-
sequent discussion, the PTAA without any treatment, with DMF pre-
washing, and with a covering of Al2O3 NPs are referred to as “control”,
“DMF PW”, and “Al2O3 SM”, respectively. We utilized Al2O3 NPs
dispersed in isopropyl alcohol (IPA) instead of an aqueous dispersion
system, since IPA is an organic solvent, which spreads well on hydro-
phobic PET surfaces. In Fig. S3, we provide photos demonstrating the
spreading of the Al2O3 IPA dispersion on the hydrophobic PET.
