that the Na accumulation at abundant grain boundaries

Li addition could lead to less Na incorporation in the

Moreover, the reduced Na content with Li addition
can correspond to the increased Cu content (increased Δ
Cu/Sn and Δ Cu/Ge), through which the out‐diffusion of
Na from SLG tended to be suppressed. This higher Cu
content suggests that the Li addition could also cause
more Sn and Ge loss during the sulfurization process. The
intensified Sn and Ge loss was also revealed by the
increased weight loss rate at around 470°C where the
bottom CZTGS decomposes drastically, as shown in
thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) curves in Figure 3C,D.
We consider that the increased Sn and Ge loss did not
originate from the proportionally reduced Cu/
(Znþ Snþ Ge) ratio with the Li addition as the grain size
of the absorber did not significantly change with different
Cu/(Znþ Snþ Ge) in CZTGS layer (Figure S3). Instead,
the loss of Sn and Ge was probably related to the loss of Li
(reduced Li/Cu in Table 1) during the sulfurization pro-
cess. The loss of Li could be evaporation of Li‐S com-
pound from sulfurized LiCl as the Li‐S compound has
lower calculated Gibbs formation free energy than LiCl,
creating force of gaseous flow and porous film structure
for accelerating the Sn and Ge loss.29–31
We consider that the consumption of Li may also play
a role during the sulfurization process because the
resulting increased Cu/(Znþ Snþ Ge) molar ratio (Cu‐
rich) in the absorber can still lead to the enlarged grain
size instead of the regularly reduced size. In this regard,
the second assumption was verified by monitoring the
loss of Li (or Li/Cu molar ratio) in the Li‐CZTGS nano-
particle film at different sulfurization temperatures for
10 min using ICP‐MS (Table S5). This method was
employed because the added Li source usually suffers
from major loss during the high‐temperature annealing
processes, as LiCl and the by‐product Li‐S compound
have relatively high vapor pressure and tend to escape
from the film during the sulfurization process.24,30 To
avoid the redissolution and loss of LiCl during multilay-
ered spin‐coating,23,27 we monitored the Li loss using
one‐layer samples (Li content was 10 times higher than
MDL). As shown in Figure 3B, the Li/Cu ratio of the one‐
layer sample (300°C, 0.097) was half the Li content in the
precursor solution (0.176), indicating that there was still
more than half of Li in the precursor solution remaining
in the one‐layer Li‐CZTGS nanoparticle film. The partial
Li loss during the coating and annealing process was
probably caused by the partial evaporation of the high
vapor pressure Li‐S compound after the thermal reaction
between LiCl and thiourea, as the evaporation of LiCl can
be negligible at such a low temperature.23 The formation
of a residual Li‐S compound after the pre‐annealing
process was expected as the obtained Li‐S compound
could not be totally alloyed into CZTS because the total
lattice energy of kesterite CZTS structure increases with
Li alloying.32,33 Noticeably, a sharp drop of the Li/Cu
ratio appears at around 300–350°C, suggesting the major
Li loss at this temperature range relates to the formation
of a high vapor pressure Li‐S compound. This range has
also been proved by comparing the differential ther-
mogravimetric curves between CZTGS and Li‐CZTGS
nanoparticle films, where a small peak of the weight
loss rate could be detected in the Li‐CZTGS sample at
around 328°C (Figure 3C,D). Since the decomposition
temperature of the CZTS nanoparticle layer is much
higher than this temperature, we can consider that the
small weight loss peak can correspond to the Li loss as
the Li‐S compound. Interestingly, the loss of Li in the
absorber slowly decreases as the sulfurization tempera-
ture goes higher than 350°C. This can account for the
detectable signal of Li by SIMS and ICP measurement
after the regular sulfurization process at 530°C
(Figure 3A,B). The residual small amount of Li allows the
formation of Li‐S flux, which can promote the LGG
process of CZTS at over 350°C before escaping from the
absorber. In this sense, we can confirm that the added
LiCl can enhance the grain growth of CZTS by providing
a little amount of Li‐S flux at sulfurization process of over
350°C. Nevertheless, adding excess amount of LiCl may
lead to nonuniform distribution of Li source in the pre-
cursor film.23 This distribution can trigger the early
escape of the Li‐S compound in the absorber (reduced Li
content shown in Table S4), leading to locally smaller
grain size as shown in Figure 1E.
