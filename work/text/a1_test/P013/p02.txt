recognized large open‐circuit voltage (VOC) deficit, that
is, Eg/q‐VOC, and the relatively low carrier collection ef-
ficiency compared to Cu(In, Ga)Se2 (CIGS) and CdTe
solar cells.7–9 Specifically, the low carrier collection effi-
ciency is caused by the short minority carrier diffusion
length due to the heavy recombination at abundant near‐
horizontal grain boundaries in a typical CZTS absorber.
Our recent electron beam induced current (EBIC) and
cathodoluminescence measurements have revealed that
the universal small grain size of CZTS in absorber layer
tends to trigger the loss of short‐circuit current density
(JSC) and fill factor (FF), limiting the device perfor-
mance.10 In this sense, investigating strategies to enlarge
the grain size and reduce recombination in detrimental
grain boundaries in CZTS is one of the promising routes
to further stretch the efficiency of CZTS solar cells.
The liquid‐phase‐assisted grain growth (LGG) process,
where the liquid phase is introduced for providing a
capillary force to merge small grains through Ostwald
ripening, is an important process for enlarging the grain
size of CZTS.11 This process has been applied for growing
large CZTSSe grains at low temperature, using metal
chalcogenides Sn(S,Se)2‐x, GeSe2‐Se as the liquid pha-
ses.12–15 However, it is relatively difficult to apply the LGG
process in pure sulfide CZTS, as the melting point of CZTS‐
related metal sulfides (CuxS, ZnS, Cu2SnS3, and SnSx) is
usually beyond the grain growth temperature region of
CZTS.16 Recently, our group reported a low‐temperature
LGG process for CZTS using Na‐rich Cu‐Sn‐S liquid
phase generated from controlled decomposition of a bot-
tom CZTGS nanoparticle layer.17 Although this method
can significantly enhance the overall carrier collection
efficiency in the absorber layer, there is still space for
further improving the JSC as there are still considerable
residual small grains lying between the large grains.
Introducing alkali metal into the precursor absorber
of CZTS is one of the effective approaches to further
promote the LGG process and enlarge the grain size of
CZTS as it can provide low‐melting‐point alkali‐
chalcogenide liquid phases.18 For instance, large
amount of the Na‐S liquid phase resulting from NaF
addition can assist the formation of large CZTS grains
spanning the absorber layer, though it tends to narrow
the depletion width and limits the minority carrier
collection efficiency of CZTS at the backside as a result of
increased p‐type doping.18–21 Incorporation of Li can also
enlarge the grain size because Li can promote the
following: (a) the introduction of Na‐Se via extra Na
supply from soda‐lime glass (SLG), which is then
exchanged by Li during high‐temperature (>400°C)
annealing22 and (b) the formation of intermediate Li‐Se
flux at > 350°C for promoting the LGG process.23–25
Meanwhile, Li doping via post‐deposition treatment
shows the capability of mitigating the deep defects in the
CZTS Se absorber by forming shallow LiZn acceptors de-
fects.26 Nevertheless, both in situ and pre‐deposition
doping strategies reported so far usually trigger massive
Li losses, making it difficult to clarify the actual chemical
state evolution of Li in the absorber during the grain
growth process. The loss of Li was mainly classified as Li‐
Na exchange between Li‐sources and Na in SLG,22
redissolving of Li into the solution during the spin‐
coating process,23,27 and the out evaporation of Li‐Se or
Li‐S at high temperature.18,28,29 Although these Li loss
mechanisms can partially explain the function of Li in
grain enlargement, further investigations should be
addressed on the details of temperature‐dependent loss of
Li and consumption of Li‐S or Li‐Se during the grain
growth process.
In this work, we have developed an Li‐enhanced
grain‐growth process to further enlarge the grain size of
our recently reported LGG‐processed CZTS. This method
was realized by introducing LiCl into the Ge‐alloyed
Cu2Zn(Sn,Ge)S4 (CZTGS) bottom nanoparticle layer.
The LiCl addition did not change the formation of liquid
phase beneath the CZTS layer in our previous CZTGS
modification strategy. Instead, this LiCl addition can
indirectly introduce the additional Li‐S liquid phase via
the controlled decomposition of the Li‐doped CZTGS
bottom layer, promoting the LGG process and the
merging of small CZTS grains. As a result, CZTS films
with a larger grain size of around 3 μm were achieved
with an improved JSC by 2.6 mA/cm2 and 1.4 mA/cm2
compared to the reference and regular CZTGS‐processed
CZTS solar cells, respectively. The temperature‐
dependent loss of Li was monitored by evaluating the
change of composition in the Li‐doped CZTGS bottom
layer during the sulfurization process. This work unveils
the evolution of Li species for the Li‐doped CZTS
absorber and obtains an extraordinarily large CZTS grain
size for efficient carrier transport, which can stimulate
the development of kesterite solar cells and other
emerging chalcogenide solar cells.

2.1 | Effect of Li addition in grain

The addition of LiCl in the CZTGS is aimed to further
enlarge the grain size of CZTS. Here, the addition of LiCl
is based on different Li/(Cu þ Li) molar ratios while
retaining the same (Cuþ Li)/(Snþ Ge) ratio. The LiCl
