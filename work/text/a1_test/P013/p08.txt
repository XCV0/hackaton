using 640 nm laser (Figure 4C). However, the minority
carrier lifetime near the junction area (measured by
470 nm laser) is slightly decreased with Li addition
(Figure S4). This reduced lifetime can be explained by the
increased carrier acceptor density NCV near the junction
from 6.16� 1016 cm−3 to 1.02� 1017 cm−3, as shown in
the capacitance–voltage (C–V), and drive level capaci-
tance profiling (DLCP) measurement (Figure 4D). The
calculated interface defect density for Li‐CZTGS samples
(4.44� 1011 cm−2) is close to the value for the CZTGS
sample (3.37� 1011 cm−2), suggesting that the increased
acceptor concentration with Li did not significantly in-
crease the interface acceptor defect density. Instead, the
increased carrier concentration with Li can improve the
junction quality (reduced ideality factor A) and thus
reduce the recombination loss near the junction region
and theVOC deficit (VOC, def) to a low value at 787 mV, as
evidenced by the slightly reduced reverse saturation
current density J0. In this case, we can associate the
improved JSC in the CZTS‐CZTGS samples with a better
charge carrier collection due to the further enlarged grain
size and increased bulk minority carrier lifetime with the
LiCl addition, along with a slightly reduced carrier loss at
junction area.
2.5 | RecorddeviceperformancewithLi
addition
After depositing an ARC on the best Li‐CZTGS device, we
further achieved a new record efficiency at 10.53% for a
Cd‐free CZTS device (total area), approaching the current
record device performance of CZTS. As shown in
Figure 5A, the Li addition enabled a significantly highJSC
of 22.60 mA/cm2, withVOC of 743.6 mV andFF of 62.65%
close to our previous record CZTGS‐modified CZTS de-
vices. Besides, a remarkably lowVOC deficit of 787 mV was
obtained when compared to other reported Cd‐free CZTS
devices. Figure 5B presents the EQE spectra of the best
performing device, with the integrated current density
measured notably high at 23.18 mA/cm2 under 1 sun
illumination at 1000 W/m2. This value is slightly higher
than the JSC obtained from the total‐area J–V measure-
ment because the EQE measurement has avoided the op-
tical loss from the metal grid shading on devices. Although
the highest EQE has reached 95% at a wavelength of
500 nm, the values between 600 and 750 nm are still lower
than 90%. This indicates that the carrier collection near the
front interface is approaching the limit, while the collec-
tion of carriers in bulk CZTS and near the rear interface
still needs to be improved. Further optimizing the carrier
collection near the rear interface is necessary forJSC boost
and efficiency breakthroughs of CZTS.
3 | CONCLUSION
We have proposed an Li‐enhanced LGG process to
significantly enlarge the grain size of pure sulfide CZTS
by adding LiCl into a CZTGS nanoparticle bottom layer
based on our reported LGG process. The introduction of
LiCl in the CZTGS layer extended the grain size to over
3 μm, resulting in aJSC boost of 1.4 mA/cm2 by reducing
the recombination of minority carriers. A champion Cd‐
free CZTS solar cell was obtained with an efficiency of
10.53% with ARC coating. This work has provided a novel
method to introduce Li into pure sulfide CZTS for fabri-
cating large‐grain CZTS and reducing bulk recombina-
tion and an indication upon the function of Li during the
sulfurization process. Insights into the Li‐enhanced LGG
process can provide indications on the manufacture and
performance optimization of high performance chalco-
genide thin film solar cells.
