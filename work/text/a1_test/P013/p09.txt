4.3 | Device fabrication
A ZnxSn1−xO (ZTO) buffer layer with a thickness of
10 nm was deposited on the as‐sulfurized absorber by the
atomic layer deposition (ALD) method using a Fiji G2
ALD system (Cambridge Nanotech) at a substrate tem-
perature of 150°C.36 DEZ and TDMASn were used as Zn
and Sn precursors, respectively. Deionized water was
employed as an oxidant for ZTO deposition and Ar as the
carrier gas. Both water and the DEZ precursor were kept
at room temperature, while the TDMASn precursor was
heated to 75°C contained in a stainless steel cylinder. The
ALD process contained a Zn/Sn:Ar:H2O:Ar cycle with
pulse lengths of 0.06/0.15:10:0.06:10 s, respectively. The
Zn‐to‐Sn subcycle ratios were confined to 3/1 with a
nominal growth rate per cycle of 0.7 Å. After that, a thin,
high‐resistivity i‐ZnO (≈50–60 nm) was deposited using
RF sputtering (AJA International, Inc., Model ATC‐2200)
at a power of 90 W (3 in. target) with a prolonged power
ramping rate to protect the heterojunction from
bombardment damage. Then an ITO (≈220 nm) layer
with a sheet resistance lower than 30 Ω/sq was deposited
using the AJA RF sputtering tool with a power of 90 W
using a 4 in. target with a pressure of 1.5 mTorr and gas
flow rate (Ar) of 15 sccm. E‐beam (Lesker PVD75)
evaporated Ni‐Al patterning with a height of ≈2 μm was
employed as top contact, and the final cell area is 0.22–
0.23 cm2 defined by mechanical scribing.
