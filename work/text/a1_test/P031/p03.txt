Cathodoluminescence (CL) in STEM: Hyperspectral mapping was 
carried out, which involves collecting a full CL spectrum at each image 
pixel. High resolution CL measurements were performed with a mono-
chromated and CS-corrected ChromaTEM modified Hermes200 STEM 
instrument from NION. CL data was collected using a M¨onch system 
from Attolight, fitted with a diffraction grating giving a wavelength 
resolution of 0.34 nm. Experiments were performed at 60 kV for all CL 
analyses. 
Scanning Transmission Electron Microscope Bright Field (STEM-BF) 
images and corresponding elemental maps were collected using a Philips 
TECNAI F20 equipped with an Oxford 80 mm2 SDD EDX detector 
operating at 200 kV. 
The same TEM sample was used in both STEM systems, allowing for 
corresponding EDS and CL measurements. The sample was thinned 
using a Xe ion beam to limit ion damage during the formation of the TEM 
lamella. The use of Xe has been shown to significantly increase the CL 
signal compared to the conventional Ga focused ion beam [6]. 
Capacitance measurements: the capacitance measurements 
including capacitance-voltage (CV), drive-level capacitance profiling 
(DLCP) and admittance spectroscopy (AS) were performed in the dark 
with a Keysight E4990 impedance analyser. AS measurements were 
carried out in an evacuated closed-cycle helium cryostat for tempera-
tures between 315 K and 115 K with 20 K steps. 
Device characterisation: the external quantum efficiency (EQE) 
spectra were acquired with chopped light using a Bentham PVE300 
system. The measurements were performed at 0 V bias with a spectral 
resolution of 5 nm. The light current density–voltage (J–V) character-
istics were measured using a Newport solar simulator under 1000 W/m2 
with AM1.5G filter, calibrated using a reference Si photodiode. The 
short-circuit current (Jsc) obtained in light J-V is consistent with EQE- 
based Jsc, which is calculated by integrating the EQE with the AM 
1.5G spectrum. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Light current density-voltage (J-V) and external quantum efficiency 
(EQE) measurements 
The choice of CdSe precursor thickness dramatically affects FTO/ 
SnO2/CdSeTe/CdTe device performance. The CdSe film thickness con-
trols the Se concentration and gradient in the CdSeTe/CdTe absorber. It 
is important that the CdSe is completely consumed to form CdSeTe at the 
front of the device. The light J-V curves in Fig. 1 show an optimal CdSe 
precursor thickness between 100 nm and 120 nm for a 3.5 μm CdTe layer 
in the CdSe/CdTe stack [7]. The selenium gradient depends on the CdCl2 
activation conditions used. When the CdSe thickness increases into the 
range of 200–400 nm, there is a probability that a pure CdSe layer will 
remain after the CdCl2 treatment. This remnant CdSe layer significantly 
reduces Jsc. In this work, two CdSe precursor layers, with thicknesses of 
120 nm and 400 nm, were chosen in the same device batch for detailed
