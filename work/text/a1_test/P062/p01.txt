Device Simulation of CsPbBr3 Solar Cells for
High-Efﬁciency Blue Light Photovoltaic Power Converters
Yuejie Tan and Shinsuke Miyajima*
1. Introduction
The development of wireless power transfer (WPT) systems has
attracted signiﬁcant attention, especially for battery charging
of electric vehicles, implantable medical devices, etc.[1,2] Home
applications such as controllers and smartphones have been
commercialized as being capable of wireless charging. However,
the main challenges of current WPT systems are the short power-
transmission distance and electromagnetic interference,[3] which
affect electromagnetic noise to electrical devices and the human
body.[4] To solve the problem of short-distance transmission,
there are two options: the WPT technique using microwaves
or the optical wireless power transfer (OWPT) technique using
light. Both techniques have the potential for long-distance power
transmission. OWPT has several advantages over microwave
WPT in some respects. These advantages include small equip-
ment size, no electromagnetic noise, and the possibility of
underwater applications. Therefore, we
focus on OWPT in this study.
As a WPT technique, OWPT has a high
potential to deliver watt-level power over a
meter-level distance without electromag-
netic interference. As shown in Figure 1a,
a monochromatic light source, laser, or
light-emitting diode (LED) is used as the
light source to convert electric power into
light, which passes through the transmis-
sion medium, and ﬁnally, the solar cell con-
verts the light into electricity, which can be
supplied to a wide range of applications,
from charging implantable medical devices
through the human skin[4] to sending
power through water.[5,6] The solar cell in
the OWPT system is referred to as a pho-
tovoltaic power converter. The efﬁciency
of the OWPT system is determined by
the product of three efﬁciencies: light source, transmission,
and photovoltaic power converter. For relatively short distances,
up to 10 m in air, the effect of light source beam absorption and
scattering by air can be almost neglected. Therefore, high trans-
mission efﬁciency is expected if the light beam can be adjusted to
the photovoltaic power converter by applying appropriate object
detection techniques using sensors.[7,8] In underwater applica-
tions, the absorption loss due to water is greater than that due
to air. However, it is still signiﬁcantly lower than that due to
microwave or radio frequency, which demonstrates the superior-
ity of this system. It can be concluded that the efﬁciency of the
OWPT system is primarily determined by the conversion efﬁ-
ciencies of the light source and photovoltaic power converter,
except for extremely long-distance power transmission, such
as over 100 m.
From the perspective of light source efﬁciency, there are not
many light source wavelength options. According to the litera-
ture, various semiconductor lasers,[9,10] including edge laser
diode (LDs), vertical cavity surface emitting lasers (VCSELs),
and LEDs, can achieve high conversion efﬁciency exceeding
50%. Speciﬁcally, near-infrared lasers perform optimally in
the 800–1000-nm wavelength range,[9,11,12] whereas blue lasers
are most efﬁcient at ≈450 nm.[13–17] For near-infrared lasers, a
VCSEL light source can achieve wall plug efﬁciency (WPE)
exceeding 63%,[18] whereas edge LDs have demonstrated
remarkable efﬁciency of up to 85% at /C050 °C and 76% at
10 °C.[11] Previous studies also indicate that blue LEDs can
achieve WPE of above 49.1%,[15] with notable cases reaching
over 70% for III-nitride LEDs emitting at 455 nm.[17] In 2010,

The performance of CsPbBr3 solar cells is investigated using device simulation
to clarify the criteria for obtaining high-efﬁciency blue light photovoltaic power
converters. The results show that the conduction band offset between the
electron transport layer (ETL) and CsPbBr3 layer signiﬁcantly impacts conversion
efﬁciency. The best device performance is obtained when the conduction band of
the ETL is 0.4 eV higher than that of the CsPbBr3 layer. Simulations also revealed
that gallium nitride is preferred to conventional titanium oxide (TiO2) as the ETL
material. The analysis indicates that the carrier diffusion length of the CsPbBr3
layer signiﬁcantly affects the short-circuit current density and ﬁll factor of devices
and that a carrier diffusion length of at least 0.5 μm is required to realize high-
efﬁciency devices. The use of a transparent conductive oxide layer with a work
function smaller than 4.0 eV or the insertion of a buffer layer with electron afﬁnity
less than 4.1 eV can effectively improve the open-circuit voltage of devices.
