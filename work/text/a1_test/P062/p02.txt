conditions, and device design. According to this situation, blue
light is considered one of the optimum light sources for high-
efﬁciency OWPT.
In the case of photovoltaic power converters, high-efﬁciency
devices have only been developed for near-infrared light.[19]
GaAs photovoltaic power converters have exhibited conversion
efﬁciency of 68.9% for 858 nm monochromatic light with a
light intensity of 11.4 W cm/C02 (about 60% for 0.1 W cm/C02).[20]
Although high-efﬁciency photovoltaic power converters for blue
light sources have not yet been developed, a promising material
choice is CsPbBr3 (a bandgap of 2.3 eV).[21] This material has a
bandgap energy comparable to the photon energy of blue light
(2.7 eV). It also exhibits greater stability against moisture and
oxygen than other halide perovskite materials.[22–24] CsPbBr3
is primarily investigated as a light-absorbing material for multi-
junction solar cells. Theoretical work has demonstrated the
potential of this material for use as the light-absorbing material
of power converters in OWPT systems.[23] A recently reported
CsPbBr3 solar cell exhibited conversion efﬁciency of 10.79%
under AM1.5G and 100 mW cm/C02 illumination (sunlight)[25]
and estimated conversion efﬁciency of ≈43.5% under a blue light
source (estimated from solar cell performance under AM1.5G
illumination and external quantum efﬁciency [EQE]). Our group
has also developed a CsPbBr3 photovoltaic power converter;
however, its conversion efﬁciency under blue light was only
≈20%.[10,26,27] The aforementioned conversion efﬁciencies are
signiﬁcantly lower than their theoretical values. Therefore, it
is important to analyze the efﬁciency- limiting factor and the
quality of the CsPbBr3 layer in the photovoltaic power converter.
In addition, the electron transport layer (ETL) (Figure 1a) is
essential to obtain high-efﬁciency devices. The role of the ETL
is to selectively transport electrons. For effective electron
transport, an ETL with suitable band alignment is critical. We
intensively investigated the inﬂuence of the ETL on device
performance in this study.
At present, device simulations of perovskite solar cells with
bandgaps (1.3–1.9 eV) suitable for normal solar cell application
have been intensively reported.[28–31] However, there are a few
reports on the device simulation of CsPbBr3 solar cells.[32] In
addition, device simulation of CsPbBr3 solar cells as photovoltaic
power converters has not been reported. Therefore, device
simulations of CsPbBr3 photovoltaic power converters were
performed to obtain criteria for high-efﬁciency CsPbBr3 photo-
voltaic power converters in this study. The effect of conduction
band offset between the ETL and the CsPbBr3 layer was investi-
gated. The results highlight the suitable ETL material choice for
devices using CsPbBr3 as the light-absorbing layer. Speciﬁcally,
the interface between the ETL and CsPbBr3 layer, the quality of
the CsPbBr3 layer, and the interface between the ETL and trans-
parent conductive oxide (TCO) layer were also investigated.
2. Simulation Details
We used a one-dimensional device simulator (AFORS-HET)[33]
in our device simulation. As shown in Figure 1a,b, the structure
of the simulated-CsPbBr3 photovoltaic power converter is glass/
TCO layer/ETL/front interface layer (FIL)/CsPbBr3 layer/rear
interface layer (RIL)/hole transport layer (HTL)/Au. Spiro
OMETAD was adopted as the HTL, and two interface layers
(FIL and RIL) were introduced to consider carrier recombination
at the interface of the two layers. The electrical and optical param-
eters of the interface layers were consistent with those of the
CsPbBr3 layer. As shown in Figure 1b, the structure of the
CsPbBr3 photovoltaic power converter adopts a perovskite solar
cell structure with an n-i-p conﬁguration.[34] The electrical param-
eters used in the simulations are summarized in Table 1. These
parameters are obtained from previous studies.[35–37] The surface
reﬂectance of the device was calculated separately using e-ARC[38]
and was implemented in the calculation using AFORS-HET.
