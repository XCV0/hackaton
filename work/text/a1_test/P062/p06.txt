due to the slightly smaller optical absorption coefﬁcient of TiO2.
However, the device with the GaN ETL exhibited ≈0.5 V higher
Voc than that with the TiO2 ETL regardless of the interface defect
density. At an interface defect density of 1016 cm/C03, the device
with the GaN ETL exhibited conversion efﬁciency of 56.0%
(Voc = 1.92 eV, FF = 0.909). In comparison, the device with
the TiO2 ETL exhibited conversion efﬁciency of only 46.2%
(Voc = 1.56 eV, FF = 0.900). When the defect density increases
to 1019 cm/C03, the conversion efﬁciencies of the GaN and TiO2
ETL devices are 40.6% and 31.8%, respectively. Therefore,
GaN is a potential material for the ETL of CsPbBr3 photovoltaic
converters. To further investigate the Jsc loss in the device with
the GaN ETL, we calculated the EQE of the device with the GaN
ETL. Figure 7 shows the dependence of the EQE of the device on
GaN ETL thickness. The EQE decreased with decreasing wave-
length due to the absorption loss in the GaN ETL. Typically, a
highly conductive doped material is used as the ETL, and such
a highly doped material is typically defective and has a small
minority carrier lifetime. Therefore, the electron–hole pair
produced in the GaN ETL immediately recombines in the
GaN ETL, which is referred to as the absorption loss. In the case
of the application of CsPbBr3 photovoltaic power converters with
a blue light source, the EQE at ≈450 nm is most important. The
EQE at 450 nm decreases with increasing layer thickness; how-
ever, the absorption loss is almost negligible when the thickness
is less than 50 nm. From the perspective of thin GaN deposition,
it is difﬁcult to achieve highly crystallized nc-GaN when the
thickness is less than 50 nm. Therefore, 50 nm thick nc-GaN
is one of the promising candidates for the ETL of CsPbBr3
photovoltaic power converters.
3.2. Effect of the Quality of CsPbBr3 Layer
To clarify the inﬂuence of the quality of the CsPbBr3 layer, we
investigated the effect of its defect density, carrier diffusion
length, and thickness on device performance. Figure 8a shows
the dependence of the device parameters on Ndef for different
carrier mobilities. Regardless of the mobility, Voc decreased lin-
early with increasing Ndef due to the enhancement of carrier
recombination in the device. The behavior of Jsc and FF was sig-
niﬁcantly inﬂuenced by carrier mobility. At low mobility, a reduc-
tion in these values was observed for small Ndef. Conversely,
these values remain almost constant up to an Ndef value of
1017 cm/C03 at a carrier mobility value of 1000 cm2 Vs/C01.

inﬂuenced by Ndef.Jsc and FF are affected by both Ndef and carrier
