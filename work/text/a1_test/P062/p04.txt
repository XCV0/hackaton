The thickness of the CsPbBr3 layer is also important because it
affects light absorption and photogenerated carrier collection. The
thicker the CsPbBr3 layer, the greater the light absorption. In con-
trast, an increase in CsPbBr3 layer thickness suppresses photogen-
erated carrier collection due to the reduction of the internal electric
ﬁeld in the CsPbBr3 layer. Furthermore, the thicker the layer,
the more the number of defects in the layer. Therefore, carrier
recombination is enhanced in a thicker CsPbBr3 layer. To deter-
mine the optimum thickness, we investigated the effect of
CsPbBr3 layer thickness on device performance.
2.3. Effect of the Work Function of TCO Layer and the Electron
Afﬁnity of Buffer Layer between TCO Layer and ETL
In the previous sections, the effect of the ΔEc between the ETL
and CsPbBr3 layer on device performance when ﬂuorine-doped
tin oxide (FTO, work function of 4.4 eV)[41] was used as the TCO
layer material was investigated. Similar to the ΔEc between the
ETL and CsPbBr3 layer, it is also important to consider the band
alignment between the TCO layer and the ETL. Therefore, we
investigated the effect of the TCO work function on device per-
formance. In these simulations, we used nc-GaN as the ETL
material and the TCO work function was varied from 3.9 to
4.4 eV. Furthermore, the TCO layer was treated as an electrode
with a speciﬁc work function rather than as a semiconductor.
These values are possible for real-world devices using zinc oxide
(ZnO)[42] as the TCO layer. We also investigated the effect of
amorphous Zn–Si–O (a-ZSO) as the buffer layer between the
TCO layer and ETL. The electron afﬁnity of a-ZSO can be con-
trolled by changing the Si content.[40] Therefore, the a-ZSO
buffer layer can improve carrier transport through the TCO layer
and ETL by adjusting the band alignment. Finally, we compared
simulation and experimental device performance to identify ways
to improve device performance.
3. Results and Discussion
3.1. Effect of Conduction Band Offset between ETL and
CsPbBr3 Layer on Device Performance
The effect of ΔEc on device performance was investigated to ﬁnd
the criterion for selecting the ETL material. Figure 3a shows the
illuminated current density–voltage ( J–V ) curves of the CsPbBr3
devices without both interface defect layers (IL; namely FIL, RIL).
The shape of the J–V curves is affected by ΔEc. The dependence
of the device parameters on ΔEc is shown in Figure 3b. The
device performance under AM1.5G (sunlight) is also shown
for comparison. Although the conversion efﬁciency differed
under the different illumination conditions (blue light or
AM1.5G) primarily because of the difference in the short-circuit
current density ( Jsc), the behavior of the device parameters was
very similar. For blue light illumination, the conversion
efﬁciency slightly decreased with increasing ΔEc from /C00.4 to
0.8 eV primarily due to the deterioration of the ﬁll factor (FF).
Conversely, the conversion efﬁciency suddenly dropped when
ΔEc was less than /C00.5 eV primarily due to the reduction in Jsc.
At small ΔEc values, an increase in the open-circuit voltage (Voc)
was also observed. The highest conversion efﬁciency (65.9%) was
obtained at ΔEc = /C00.4 eV for blue light illumination.
