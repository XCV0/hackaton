As shown in Figure 3, we did not consider the FIL. This cor-
responds to an ideal interface without defects. However, inter-
face defects exist in real-world devices. Therefore, we
introduced the FIL to investigate the effect of interface defects.
The defect density of the FIL was 1 /C2 1019 cm /C03. The results are
shown in Figure 4a,b. Compared with the results of the device
without the FIL, the difference in Voc and FF is observed when
ΔEc increases from /C00.2 to 0.8 eV. The decrease in conversion
efﬁciency in this ΔEc range was very small for the device without
the FIL. However, the conversion efﬁciency of the device with the
FIL decreased signiﬁcantly because of the deterioration of Voc
and FF. This indicates that carrier recombination at the interface
between the ETL and CsPbBr3 layer is signiﬁcantly inﬂuenced by
ΔEc. Similar results were obtained in numerical simulations of
Cu(In, Ga)Se2 solar cells.[43] The best conversion efﬁciency
(64.5%) was obtained at ΔEc = /C00.4 eV for blue light illumina-
tion. This value is very similar to that for the device without the
FIL, indicating the importance of ΔEc adjustment.
To understand the principles behind these device perfor-
mance behaviors, simulated band diagrams at the maximum
power point (MPP) are shown in Figure 5.AtΔEc = /C00.8 eV,
a spike structure is observed in the conduction band at the inter-
face between the ETL and CsPbBr3 layer. The height of the spike
is equal to the absolute value of ΔEc and acts as an electron trans-
port barrier.[44] This results in a small Jsc when ΔEc is less than
/C00.5 eV. When ΔEc is between /C00.1 and /C00.4 eV, the ETL/
CsPbBr3 layer interface is a spike structure with a small electron
transport barrier. This spike structure with a small energy barrier
effectively prevents carrier recombination in the FIL and good
electron transport. At ΔEc = 0.6 eV, a cliff structure is observed
in the conduction band at the interface. In this situation, holes in
the CsPbBr3 layer and electrons in the ETL accumulate in the
FIL, thereby enhancing carrier recombination. This is the reason
for the decreased conversion efﬁciency of the device with the FIL
with increasing ΔEc. At ΔEc = 0 eV, a cliff structure is still
observed in the conduction band. Therefore, the best device
performance was not obtained at ΔEc = 0 eV. This explains
why a slightly negative ΔEc value of ≈/C00.4 eV is the optimal
ΔEc value. In addition, if ΔEc exceeds /C00.4 eV, a lower value
is preferred. This is one of the most important criteria for
ETL material selection.
