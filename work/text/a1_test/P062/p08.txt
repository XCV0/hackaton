the effects of the TCO work function and the electron afﬁnity of
the buffer layer between the TCO layer and ETL. The results are
summarized in Figure 10. In these simulations, nc-GaN was
used as the ETL material. When we used a TCO layer with a large
work function, Voc and FF decreased. A work function less than
4.0 eV is required to obtain the best conversion efﬁciency. This
value is very different from that of FTO, which is the standard
TCO layer material in perovskite solar cells. Highly doped ZnO,
such as aluminum-doped ZnO (AZO), has a work function of
≈4.0 eV.[45] Therefore, one of the promising candidates for the
TCO layer material is AZO. Replacement of the TCO layer mate-
rial is a possible solution to realize an improved TCO layer/ETL
interface. Insertion of a buffer layer between the TCO layer and
ETL is another solution. The effect of the electron afﬁnity of the
buffer layer on device performance is illustrated in Figure 10.
In these simulations, FTO was used as the TCO layer material.
The electron afﬁnity of the buffer layer signiﬁcantly affects FF.
The best conversion efﬁciency was obtained when the electron
afﬁnity of the buffer layer was 4.1 eV. This value can be realized
using a-ZSO. Figure 11 shows the band diagrams of the devices
with FTO, AZO (a work function of 4.0 eV), and FTO and the
buffer layer (electron afﬁnity: 4.1 eV). A candidate material of this
buffer layer is a-ZSO.[40] For the device with FTO (no buffer
layer), a large Schottky barrier is formed at the interface between
the TCO layer and ETL. This prevents smooth electron transport
from the ETL to FTO, thereby decreasing FF. Furthermore, this
Schottky barrier acts as an additional photodiode and generates
photovoltage in a direction different from that of the photovolt-
age of the CsPbBr3 device. This reduces the Voc of the device.
By replacing FTO with AZO, the height of the Schottky barrier
is reduced, improving Voc and FF, as shown in Figure 11.
Figure 11 also shows the effect of the buffer layer on the band
diagram. In this case, sufﬁciently small Schottky barriers are
formed at the ETL/buffer layer and TCO layer/ETL interfaces,
resulting in good device performance.
