The effect of layer thickness on device performance is illustrated
in Figure 9. In these simulations, we also investigated the effect of
carrier mobility. Voc decreased with increasing thickness due to the
enhancement of carrier recombination. Jsc increased with increas-
ing thickness due to enhanced optical absorption. Voc and Jsc were
not affected by mobility. By varying mobility from 1 to 10 cm2 Vs/C01,
LD varied from 0.22 to 0.72 μm. In this LD range, Voc and Jsc are
almost constant, as shown in Figure 8b. Therefore, the inﬂuence of
mobility on Voc and Jsc is very small. Conversely, FF was inﬂuenced
by thickness and mobility. Larger thickness leads to smaller FF.
Higher mobility leads to larger FF, and the reduction in FF by
increasing the CsPbBr3 layer thickness is reduced for higher mobil-
ity. As shown in Figure 8b, FF is signiﬁcantly inﬂuenced by LD in
the range of 0.22–0.72 μm. Therefore, only FF is affected by mobil-
ity. Given the balance between the improvement of Jsc and the
reduction in Voc and FF, the optimal thickness of the CsPbBr3 layer
was in the range of 400–600 nm. The optimal thicknesses of the
CsPbBr3 layer are ≈400 and 600 nm with carrier mobilities of 1
and 10 cm2 Vs/C01,respectively.
