on previous research.[47–49] For the ideal device, we also assumed
that the device had no interface layers (FIL and RIL) and adopted
AZO as the TCO layer material. The simulation results of the
ideal device are indicated by “Ideal Case” in Figure 12b,c.
Further improvement of Voc and FF was observed. This opti-
mized device achieved high efﬁciency of 11.82% (Voc = 1.88 V,
Jsc = 7.22 mA cm/C02,FF= 87.08%) under AM1.5G illumination
and 74.2% under 450 nm illumination. The device parameters
are summarized in Table 3. These results indicate that the
performance of our device is primarily limited by two factors:
interface defects and the mismatch of the band alignment
between the TCO layer and ETL.
4. Conclusion
Device simulations of CsPbBr3 photovoltaic power converters
were performed to identify the criteria for obtaining high-
efﬁciency devices. The simulation results highlighted that the
conduction band offset at the ETL/CsPbBr3 layer interface signif-
icantly affected the device performance. The best conduction
band offset was /C00.4 eV, which appeared as a spike structure,
and a larger conduction band offset degraded device perfor-
mance. The simulation results also highlight that TiO2, a conven-
tional ETL material for standard perovskite solar cells, is not
suitable for CsPbBr3 photovoltaic power converters. We pro-
posed and demonstrated the potential of nanocrystalline GaN
ETLs using device simulations. The effect of the quality of the
CsPbBr3 layer was also investigated. According to the results,
the carrier diffusion length primarily determines Jsc and FF;
however, the defect density is the limiting factor of Voc. The effect
of the TCO layer/ETL interface was also investigated. The band
alignment of this interface also affects device performance. Our
simulation revealed that the use of a low TCO work function or
the insertion of a buffer layer with small electron afﬁnity can mit-
igate the problem of the TCO layer/ETL interface. Finally, we
analyzed our experimentally obtained device using our developed
simulation model. By adjusting the interface defect density and
the quality of the CsPbBr3 layer, we successfully reproduced the
J–V curves and EQE results. This indicates the reliability of our
simulation model. Using this model, we also discussed ways to
improve device performance.
