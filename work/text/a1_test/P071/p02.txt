2.ResultsandDiscussion
The butylammonium (BA)-based 2D perovskite ﬁlm,
BA2MAn−1PbnI3n+1 (<n> = 5), prepared by hot-casting was
used in this study owing to its considerably high device perfor-
mance and the well-established crystallographic parameters of
theRPPsinglecrystals.Ourpreviousstudieshavedemonstrated
that2D-PSCsbasedontheseﬁlmsachievedapowerconversion
eﬃciency (PCE) of ≈14.5%,[17,32] which is amongst the highest
performance reported for BA-based 2D-PSCs. As indicated by
the UV–vis absorption spectrum (Figure 1a), these ﬁlms are a
phase mixture of 2D RPPs with small-n (n = 2, 3, 4, …) and
large-n (n = L). The photoluminescence (PL) spectra of the
quasi-2Dﬁlmscollectedfromthetop(ﬁlm/air)sideandbottom
(ﬁlm/substrate) side are shown in Figure 1a. Both spectra fea-
ture a dominant PL peak at ≈750 nm assigned to the large-n
component, indicating the existence of exciton funneling from
small-n tolarge-n RPPsuponphotoexcitationthatquenchesthe
luminescence from small-n RPPs.[33] The phase distribution
proﬁle of the 2D perovskite ﬁlm along the vertical direction
can be investigated by transient absorption (TA) measurements
conductedusingthetop-sideandbottom-sideexcitationmodes.
As shown in Figure 1b, TA spectra collected at the initial time-
delay stage contain speciﬁc ground-state bleaching (GB) peaks
assigned to various RPP components.[34] The larger intensity
ratioofGBpeaksofsmall-n (n = 3, 4, 5) RPPs observed with
the bottom-side excitation mode and the dominant GB peaks of
large-n (n = L) RPPs for the top-side excitation mode suggest
that the bottom part of the quasi-2D perovskite ﬁlm is occupied
by small-n RPPs, mostly the n = 3 phase, while the top part of
theﬁlmisdominatedbythelarge-n RPPs.
