The effect of FPEAI on the film crystallization was character -
ized by X-ray diffraction (XRD). As seen in Figure 3a, compared 
to the Control film, the Additive film delivers a high ratio of (100) 
plane, indicating that the incorporation of FPEAI promotes the 
preferred (100) orientation. [22] As the FPEAI content increases 
further (Figure  3b), a significant increase can be observed in 
the peak intensities of the (100) and (200) reflection, especially 
the (100) peak, which further proves the preferred orientation of 
crystalline grains towards the (100) direction after adding FPEAI. 
The enhancement of preferred orientation along the (100) plane 
also indicates that the addition of FPEAI functionalizes specific 
crystal planes, thereby changing the surface energy during the 
crystal growth. [23] Surprisingly, the magnified (200) peak posi-
tion is slightly shifted toward a lower degree (Figure 3c), which 
indicates a unit cell expansion in the perovskite lattice induced 
by the incorporation of the larger alkylammonium cation of 
FPEA+ into the WBG perovskite. Increasing the FPEAI con-
centration of 5 mol% has not induced a larger peak shift but 
facilitates the formation of a diffraction peak at 5.2° , which can 
be assigned to be from FPEA2PbI4 (Figure S3, Supporting Infor-
mation). Especially after 10 mol% addition, the peak intensity 
of 5.2°  and 10.8°  is more obvious. The reason is that FPEAI 
with the large spacer is not enough to form 2D perovskite when 
added in a small amount and does not enter the 3D perovskite 
lattice while FPEAI with the large spacer exists at or near the 
grain boundary. [24] Given the different diffusivity and van der 
Waals interactions between FA + and FPEA +, the FPEA + is pro-
posed to form both on the surface and within the grain bounda-
ries via self-assembly to form 2D/3D perovskite structures with 
increasing the FPEAI concentration. [25] Steady-state photolumi-

based 2D perovskite illuminating from the perovskite side and
