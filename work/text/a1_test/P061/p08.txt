Defects at grain boundary or surface of polycrystalline 
perovskite films act as channels for water and oxygen intru-
sion and ion migration, resulting in poor stability. We further 
demonstrate the effect of this strategy on stability. Figure  5f  
displays the water contact angle of the Control and A&P perov-

angle of 79.41° , more than twice the Control film (35.80° ). The 
increased contact angle indicates that such synergistic strategy 
can effectively enhance the film stability and prevent the corro-
sion of moisture and oxygen, which is due to the improved crys-
tallization and formed 2D/3D heterostructure hydrophobic bar -
rier. To verify the enhanced moisture stability, the operational 
stability is tested under a relative humidity of 30%± 10% without 
encapsulation at room temperature. A&P devices retain 85% 
of their original PCEs after 1000 h, whilst the Control devices 
maintain 70% under the same conditions. This result indicates 
the improved moisture stability with this proposed synergetic 
strategy via modulating the crystallinity and defect passivation.
3. Conclusion
In this work, we report a synergetic strategy to simultaneously 
improve the crystallinity and passivate the defects at grain 
boundary and interface by using alkylammonium salt 4-fluoro-
phenylethylammonium iodide (FPEAI) as a dual-functional 
agent. In this synergistic strategy, the FPEAI modulates the 
crystallization of 3D WBG perovskite film and reduces trap den-
sity, and suppresses nonradiative recombination, leading to a 
remarkable PCE up to 19.07% with significantly enhanced VOC to 
1.30 V and FF to 77.8%. On a large-area device of 1 cm 2, a PCE of 
15.01% with a high VOC of 1.29 V can be achieved, indicating the 
potential for scale-up PSCs. Furthermore, the 2D perovskite at 
the grain boundary and surface endows the cells with enhanced 
hydrophobic property and long-term stability. As a result, the 
modified devices retained 85% of their initial efficiencies under 
a relative humidity of 30% for 1000 h. Our study provides new 
insight into high-efficiency and stabilized WBG PSCs.
4. Experimental Section
Raw Materials: All the materials and chemicals were used without 
further purification. SnO 2 nanoparticle was purchased from Alfa Aesar. 
Spiro-OMeTAD (99.8%) was obtained from Lumtech. 4-tert-butylpyridine 
(tBP, >96%), lead (II) iodide (PbI 2, 99.99%), lead bromide (PbBr 2, 
99.99%), cesium iodide (CsI, 99.99%), formamidinium iodide (FAI, 
≥99.5%), and 4-fluoro-phenylethylammonium iodide (FPEAI, ≥99.5%) 
were ordered from Xi’an Polymer Light Technology Corp. All the solvents 
were purchased from Sigma Aldrich.
Fabrication of WBG PSCs: Indium-doped tin oxide (ITO) glasses were 
precleaned with detergent, acetone, and ethanol for 15 min each. The 
cleaned ITO was treated with UV ozone (UVO) for 15 min to remove the 
organic residue and improve the wettability. The SnO 2 dispersion was 
(diluted at 1:3 with deionized water) deposited on the ITO substrate at a 
rate of 4000 r.p.m. for 20 s, followed by annealing at 180  °C for 20 min. 
The SnO2/ITO/glass substrates were treated with UVO for 15 min and 
then transferred to the N 2 glove box. For the Cs 0.2FA0.8Pb(I0.7Br0.3)3 
composition, 52.0 mg CsI, 137.6 mg FAI, 253.6 mg PbI 2 and 165.2 mg 
PbBr2 were dissolved in dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO)/dimethylformamide 
(DMF) mixed solvent (1:3 v/v). To synthesize the Additive perovskite, 
1.5 mol% FAI was substituted by FPEAI. The films were deposited at 
1000 r.p.m. for 10 s and 4000 r.p.m. for 30 s. 100 µ L chlorobenzene was 
dropped on the film after 30 s. Then the film was annealed at 100  °C 
for 30 min. For FPEAI treatment, the FPEAI/IPA solution was spin-
coated onto the perovskite surface at 4000 r.p.m. for 30 s. Subsequently,  
72.3 mg spiro-OMeTAD, 17.5 µ L of LiTFSI (520 mg mL−1 in acetonitrile), 
and 29 µL tBP were dissolved in 1 mL chlorobenzene, then the solution 
was spin-coated onto the perovskite film at 4000 r.p.m. for 30 s. Finally, 
100 nm Ag was thermal evaporated onto the spiro-OMeTAD layer, the 
active area of the device is determined by a shadow mask of 0.14 cm 2.
Characterizations: The crystallographic characterizations were tested by 
X-ray diffraction (XRD) using PANalytical B.V. X-ray diffractometer with 
Cu Kα  radiation under operating conditions of 40 kV and 40 mA. The 
morphology of the films was observed by SEM (FEI INSPECT F50, U.S.A.). 
AFM images were obtained by AFM (KEYSIGHT Technologies 7500). 
TEM was performed by FEI Tecnai G2 F20. XPS and UPS were taken with 
an XPS/UPS system (Thermo scientific, Al Kα  radiation). The UV–vis 
spectra were measured using a UV-3600 spectrophotometer (Shimadzu 
Corporation). PL and TRPL were performed with an FLS980 (Edinburgh 
Instruments Ltd). Current density–voltage (J –V) curves of WBG PSCs 
were performed using a Keithley 2400 Source Meter under standard AM 
1.5G illumination (100 mW cm −2, SS-F5-3A). The corresponding EQE 
spectrum was measured by a PVE300 photovoltaic system.
