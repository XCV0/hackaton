To clarify the reaction mechanism between the FPEAI and 
WBG perovskite and further confirm the existence of FPEAI, 
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was carried out on 
the Control, Additive, Passivation, and A&P perovskite films. 
Figure  3d shows the F1s peak that is observed at 687 eV in 
the perovskite films (except for the Control film). In the C1s 
spectra (Figure  3e), there are three peaks at 288.7, 286.5, and 
284.8eV, which belong to the C O, C N, and C C bonds, 
respectively. The C N and C C bond are attributed to the 
FA+ and FPEA + cations. The emerged C O bond is attributed 
to the surface degradation invaded by the adsorbed oxygen/
moisture when exposed to the ambient condition. Obviously, 
the stronger intensity of the C O bond exhibits the poor sta-
bility, and further indicates that it is prone to degradation and 
attacked by water or oxygen. The C O bond disappears in 
A&P perovskite film, revealing the improved moisture stability 
with the synergetic strategy. The chemical reaction between 
the uncoordinated Pb 2+ and FPEAI is shown in Figure  3f.
