2. Results and Discussion
The composition of WBG perovskite in this work is 
Cs0.2FA0.8Pb(I0.7Br0.3)3, deposited by a one-step method. [20] 
Its optical bandgap (E g) is ≈1.75 eV, as shown in Figure S1 in 
the Supporting Information. For the FPEAI-added sample, 1.5 
mol% FAI was substituted by FPEAI in the perovskite pre-
cursor. The FPEAI/IPA solution was spun cast onto the WBG 
perovskite film to obtain the passivated film. For simplicity, the 
films of pristine Cs0.2FA0.8Pb(I0.7Br0.3)3, FPEAI-added Cs0.2FA0.8
Pb(I0.7Br0.3)3, FPEAI interface treatment for pristine Cs 0.2FA0.8P
b(I0.7Br0.3)3, and synergetic strategy are named as Control, Addi-
tive, Passivation, and A&P, respectively. The schematic illus-
tration of the perovskite film deposition process is depicted in 
Figure 1. As can be seen, the synergetic mechanism of FPEAI is 
to modulate crystallization and in-situ form 2D/3D heterostruc-
ture perovskite, as deduced by the following studies.
Top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) measurement 
was conducted to investigate the surface topography. As shown 
in Figure 2a,b, the films have dense and pinhole-free mor -
phology. With adding FPEAI, the grain size of samples becomes 
smaller. Figure 2e shows the corresponding size distribution of 
the two samples. As can be seen, the average grain size of the 
Control film is ≈248 nm, whereas the Additive film is decreased 
to 175 nm. This observation implies that the FPEAI influences 
the crystal growth mode. After FPEAI interface treatment, the 
3D WBG perovskite film was covered by the in situ fabricated 
2D perovskite as shown in Figure 2c,d. An obvious grain-coars-
ening process can be seen and should be attributed to the ther -
modynamically spontaneous Ostwald ripening process. [21] The 
A&P film exhibits a flatter surface than the Passivation film, 
and the density of grain boundary is relatively reduced, which 
can be explained by the cross-sectional SEM (Figure  2f). The 
atomic force microscopy (AFM) (seen in Figure S2 in the Sup-
porting Information) images show a reduced root-mean-square 
surface roughness (RMS), demonstrating the smoother mor -
phology of the synergetic strategy. To confirm the presence of 
2D Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) perovskite, transmission electron 
microscopy (TEM) was used to analyze the A&P perovskite 
film. Figure  2g shows a chunk of the A&P sample scratched 
from the substrate. One of the typical regions is magnified 
and the corresponding highlighted regions I (perovskite grain) 
and II (grain boundary) are shown in Figure  2h,i. As can be 
seen, the d-spacing of region I can be measured to be 3.08 Å, 
matched with the (200) crystal plane of the 3D WBG perovskite. 
The region II shows a larger d-spacing at the grain boundary to 
be 4.1 Å, which is consistent with the (004) plane of FPEA 2PbI4 
(Table S1, Supporting Information). These results suggest that 
the observed 2D perovskites are formed at the grain boundary 
or the upper surface, which can effectively passivate the defect.
