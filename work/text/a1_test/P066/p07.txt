Since the diﬀerent alkali cations show diﬀerent eﬀect on
the electronic properties and charge recombination process in
the device. We suspect that this could be related to the dif-
ferent mechanistic behavior of the cations in the device. To
conﬁrm this hypothesis, we conducted time-of-ﬂight secondary
ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS) to investigate the distribu-
tion of alkali cation in the devices. It should be noted that, we
employed a double cation perovskite that does not contain Cs
((FAPbI3)0.95 (MAPbBr3)0.05) in the ToF-SIMS measurement in
order to identify the eﬀect of CsF treatment in the device. The
ToF-SIMS proﬁles of cations and anions distributing across the
perovskite/SnO2/ITO layers are shown inFigure 4a–c. We have
found that, for the samples with RbF and KF treatment, signiﬁ-
cant amount of Rb+ and K+ was detected not only on the SnO2
layerbutalsoinsidetheperovskitelayer.Theresultrevealsthat,
upon contact with the perovskite layer, Rb+ and K+ cations can
diﬀuse from the surface of SnO2 layer into the perovskite layer.
Meanwhile,theF− anionremainsontheSnO2 layer(FigureS14,
SupportingInformation),whichisconsistentwiththeclaimthat
F− anion can be incorporated into SnO2 layer by replacing the
–(OH)group.IthasbeenreportedthatincorporationofRb+ and
K+ into perovskite layer beneﬁted the PSC devices through re-
duced defect and improved crystallinity.[46,47] This is consistent
withtheresultsinthiswork.

alkali-metal alloy K-In.[48] The formation of alloy clusters may
