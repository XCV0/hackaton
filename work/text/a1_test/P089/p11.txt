the spinel and bixbyite structures [see the inset in the left plot of
Fig. 7]. Interestingly, the crystalline bixbyite structure and the
amorphous bixbyite structure look rather similar, i.e., the crystalline
bixbyite structure at x = 0.75 loses some crystallinity. The corre-
sponding radial distribution function tends toward one already at
distances above 5 Å and mimics the radial distribution function of
the amorphous structure. The spinel, on the other hand, retains a
pronounced crystallinity at x = 0.75. We suggest, based on this spe-
cific result, that mixed alloys are expected to form amorphous
structures around x = 0.75, in agreement with our experimental
results from XRD and HRTEM.
In a previous work, we considered incorporation of oxygen
impurities in the crystal structure of In2S3 and found that depend-
ing on the crystallographic environment of sulfur atoms, the forma-
tion enthalpy of oxygen on S sites can amount to 0.49, 0.76, and
1.0 eV.39 The low formation enthalpy of O on different S sites indi-
cates the prevalence of OS point defects in In2S3. Obtaining an
ample oxygen content of x = 0.1 in In2S3, however, is only possible
at elevated temperatures of 2000 /C0 3000 K, as can be estimated
from the Boltzmann factor with the respective formation enthalpy.
Regarding electronic activity of O impurities in In2S3,wehave
shown that O-on-S sites form extremely deep (+/0) donor levels at
around 2 eV below the CBM. Note that in order to get easily
ionized and effectively doped electrons to the system, donor levels
are expected to form near the CBM. Therefore, O impurities on S
sites are electrically inactive and do not contribute to n-type con-
ductivity of the In2S3 samples.
IV. CONCLUSIONS
Increasing the oxygen content x in sputtered In2(OxS1−x)3
buffer layers for CIGS thin-film solar cells decreases the power con-
version efficiency drastically, mainly due to decreasing VOC values.
This trend was found for CIGS cells with In2(OxS1−x)3 buffers sput-
tered from ceramic targets and reactively sputtered from an In2S3
target with different oxygen contents in the gas flow. Similar to
CIGS cells with pure In2S3 buffers, the highest efficiencies were
observed for higher Tsub = 200 or 220 °C compared to RT. Only no
or moderate oxygen contents with x < 0.25 seem to be tolerable,
whereas medium and high oxygen contents result in almost lethal
cells. The problems for the cells with the oxygen-containing buffers
can be explained by an increase of the negative band offset between
the CIGS absorber and In2(OxS1−x)3 buffer as calculated by DFT,
resulting in a pronounced VOC degradation as experimentally
observed. An additional issue in view of efficiencies could be an
even more pronounced sputter damage of the sensitive CIGS
surface with increasing oxygen.
The binary compounds In2S3 and In2O3 sputtered at
Tsub = 220 °C exhibit a crystalline structure, whereas In2(O0.25S0.75)3
shows a nanocrystalline structure and both In2(O0.5S0.5)3 and
In2(O0.75S0.25)3 are amorphous. These experimental findings are
supported by DFT calculations, and it is very unlikely that crystal-
line In2(OxS1−x)3 will form under various experimental conditions
due to the necessity of very high temperatures above 2000 K.
Adding oxygen to In2S3, an optical bandgap with around 2 eV
results in a bowing of the values below 2 eV, finally reaching values
around 2.7 eV for In2O3 in case an indirect band transition is
applied. For the evaluation under assumption of a direct band tran-
sition, higher values from 2.7 to 3.7 eV result with an almost linear
increase from In2S3 to In2O3, respectively. These findings are valid
for In2(O,S)3 thin films prepared by sputtering from ceramic
targets and reactive sputtering, and this relation with x is mostly
independent of the substrate temperatures during sputtering.
A pronounced oxygen incorporation in In2S3 is not beneficial
in terms of device performance because oxygen is electronically
inactive and poorly miscible.
