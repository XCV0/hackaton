Note that the applied model may not be suitable enough to
yield exact optical bandgap data because the total absorption is
the sum of indirect and direct transitions within the In2(O,S)3
material or, what is much more difficult, is the sum of inhomoge-
neous sequential or parallel ordered different material phases of
the films.
The optical bandgaps, derived by extrapolation in Fig. 2, for
films with different oxygen admixture are summarized in Fig. 3 as
a function of the OOS ratio (from EDXS measurements on sister
samples). Additionally, the results for films deposited at RT from
the ceramic targets and both from the reactive sputtering at RT and
Tsub = 220 °C are displayed as well. Based on the results in Fig. 2,
the indirect band-to-band transitions dominate the absorption
mainly at lower oxygen contents, whereas approaching In2O3, the
direct transitions become more important. Figure 3 suggests an
(initial) narrowing of the indirect bandgap with increasing oxygen
admixture. The strong absorption increase ascribed to a direct
bandgap at high photon energies shifts monotonically from below
3.0 to about 3.7 eV for highest oxygen concentrations. This obser-
vation is independent of the sputtering method (ceramic target vs
reactive sputtering), and it seems that Tsub has a minor impact.
Often, slightly higher optical bandgap energies were observed for
the layers sputtered at Tsub = 220 °C compared to RT, which we
found also in previous experiments with pure In2S3 layers deposited
by magnetron sputtering.16
