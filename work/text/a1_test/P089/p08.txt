interfacial charge recombination at the absorber–buffer interface.
Therefore, despite their larger bandgaps, In2O3 and oxygen-rich
In2(O,S)3 are not appropriate buffer systems to be considered in the
stacking sequence of CIGS devices due to their pronounced nega-
tive CBO (cliff ) with potential absorbers.
D. Materials’ properties of sputtered In2(O,S)3 buffer
layers
1. Structural analysis
First, the structure of the different sputtered In2(OxS1−x)3
layers was analyzed by XRD, and the measured diffractograms are
shown in Fig. 5. The data are given for relatively thick In2(OxS1−x)3
layers with different OOS ratios x sputtered on quartz glass sub-
strates. According to data for the layers sputtered from the ceramic
targets [Fig. 5(a)], only the binary compounds In2O3 and In2S3
exhibit a crystalline structure for Tsub = 220 °C. X-ray diffracto-
grams of the In2(OxS1−x)3 compounds with x equal to 0.25, 0.50,
and 0.75 do not show distinct diffraction peaks and, therefore,
appear to be x-ray amorphous. This finding seems to be indepen-
dent of the oxygen incorporation during sputtering, as for the reac-
tively sputtered layers shown in Fig. 5(b), only reflexes appear for
the oxygen-free In2S3 sample (0 vol. % O2) and for the samples
with very extensive oxygen incorporation (4 and 5 vol. % O2). The
sample with 1 vol. % O2 shows only two distinct reflexes compared
to the oxygen-free In2S3 layer and also reflects the trend that with
oxygen addition, the samples tend to be x-ray amorphous. The
samples deposited at RT (see Fig. S6 in the supplementary
material) support the above-described findings except that pure
In2S3 for the ceramic target series seems to be x-ray amorphous. It
should be noted here that two different pure In2S3 targets from dif-
ferent targets suppliers were used for the two experimental cam-
paigns (reactive sputtering vs ceramic targets) to avoid
contamination with oxygen of the oxygen-free In2S3 reference
layers for the series with the ceramic targets due to reactive sputter-
ing with oxygen. A similar trend was reported by Jayaraman et al.
for In2(OxS1−x)3 layers grown by ALD, i.e., x-ray amorphous for
In2(O,S)3 and crystalline for the binary In2S3 and In2O3 layers.29
To consolidate the XRD findings, the structure of the
In2(OxS1−x)3 layers sputtered at Tsub = 220 °C from the different ceramic
targets was investigated by HRTEM imaging at the CIGS/buffer
interface in complete solar cells. Figure 6 shows typical HRTEM
images of interfacial regions between the CIGS absorber and the
In2(OxS1−x)3 layers, namely, In2S3 [Fig. 6(a)], In2(O0.25S0.75)3
[Fig. 6(b)], In2(O0.5S0.5)3 [Fig. 6(c)], In2(O0.75S0.25)3 [Fig. 6(d)], and
