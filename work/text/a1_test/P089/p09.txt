note that a small volume fraction with a disordered structure

lattice-plane type of the subjacent CIGS grains. The HRTEM image
in Fig. 6(e) shows an orientation relationship that is typical for the
pure In2O3 on CIGS. In the imaged CIGS/In2O3 interfacial region,
the oxide layer exhibits a high degree of crystallinity with the (22/C222)
planes of cubic In2O3 (space group Ia-3, a = 10.12 Å) parallel to the
(/C221/C2212) planes of CIGS. The Fourier transform [inset in Fig. 6(e)]
confirms the monocrystalline structure of the imaged region with
cubic In2O3 in the [213] zone-axis orientation. In addition, In2O3
grown on (020) CIGS planes is also highly crystalline, and a well-
defined orientation relationship is detected with (020)In2O3||(020)
CIGS. However, In2O3 on other CIGS lattice-plane types has a
nanocrystalline structure, which indicates that the degree of crystal-
linity of In2O3 seems to depend on the orientation of the In2O3/
CIGS interface. Overall, there is a good agreement between XRD
data obtained on 300–500 nm thick In2(OxS1−x)3 layers on glass
[cf. Fig. 5] and structural data obtained on thin In2(OxS1−x)3 layers
in complete solar cells as revealed by HRTEM. Of course, HRTEM
imaging reveals more details of the real structure of the sputtered
In2(OxS1−x)3 layers under investigation, and it is known that x-ray
amorphous does not exclude a nanocrystalline structure.
