Koida et al. introduced (In1−yGay)2O3 (y = 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, and
1) as an alternative buffer material for CIGS thin-film solar cells and
found that the performance varied systematically as a function of y.
For values of y equal to 0.9 and 1 (pure Ga2O3), the VOC values of
the cells were comparable to that of a reference cell with the standard
CdS/i-ZnO buffer system.27 The above-mentioned, and our own,
working CIGS solar cells with a sputtered Ga2O3 buffer26,28 behave
differently compared to the completely dead CIGS cells with the
In2O3 buffer presented in Figs. 1(a) and 1(e).
B. Optical bandgap energy of In2(O,S)3 dependent on
oxygen content
Figure 2 shows transmittance measurements and correspond-
ing fits for the In2(O,S)3 series sputtered from ceramic targets at
Tsub = 220 °C (for transmittance raw data and corresponding
absorption, see Fig. S4 in the supplementary material). The
absorption shifts continuously toward higher photon energy with
increasing oxygen concentration in the targets. It is difficult to
decide on the appropriate energetic region where the plotted
curves exhibit linear behavior leading to the related optical
bandgap by extrapolating to the abscissa. We assume that the
absorption strength caused by the indirect transitions depicted in
Fig. 2(a) is weak [below about 30 000 cm−1;seealsoFig.S4(b)in
the supplementary material], whereas for direct transitions shown
in Fig. 2(b), it is dominant between 50 000 and 150 000 cm−1.
Hence, the linear regions are expected if (α⋅Eph)0.5 <400cm−1 and
if (α⋅Eph)2 ∼ 2×1011 cm−1 for indirect and direct transitions,
respectively.
