B. Solar cell and thin-film characterization
Current density–voltage (J–V) measurements were performed
with a WACOM (Saitama, Japan) AM1.5G solar simulator with
four-point geometry at standard testing conditions with a silicon
reference solar cell for calibration. The CIGS cells for J–V charac-
terization were in the as-grown state without any further light-
soaking or post-annealing procedures.
External quantum efficiency (EQE) was measured with an
additional light bias using a BENTHAM PVE 300 setup with Si
and Ge reference photodiodes for calibration.
Evaluation of transmittance data of around 500 nm thick sput-
tered In2(O,S)3 films on D263T borosilicate glass as measured by a
Perkin Elmer Lambda 900 spectrometer was performed using the
DIPLOT software.17 The film thickness was derived from the inter-
ference patterns in which interference amplitude and period were
fit by refractive index dispersion and film thickness. Based on the
film thickness, the absorption coefficient α is calculated. It is
common practice to plot (α⋅Eph)0.5 or (α⋅Eph)2 vs Eph with the
photon energy Eph, in order to determine the bandgap energy from
the abscissa intersect of the extrapolated linear regions based on
the assumptions of allowed indirect or direct interband transitions,
respectively.
XRD was measured with a PANalytical Empyrian in Bragg–
Brentano geometry using Cu-Kα radiation.
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was applied to char-
acterize the structural properties and microchemistry of the buffer/
absorber interface regions. For this purpose, cross-sectional TEM
samples were prepared by means of focused-ion-beam (FIB)
milling using an FEI Helios G4 dual-beam microscope. The FIB
lamellae were finally polished by a Ga+-ion beam with a low accel-
erating voltage of 1 kV to minimize material damage.
High-resolution TEM (HRTEM) imaging was performed on an
image-aberration corrected FEI Titan3 80–300 transmission elec-
tron microscope at 300 kV. Chemical analyses by means of EDXS
were performed in the scanning TEM (STEM) mode with a 200 kV
microscope of the type FEI Tecnai Osiris. For EDXS analyses, the
microscope is equipped with a FEI Super-X system comprising
four silicon drift detectors. X-ray maps were recorded in the
so-called HyperMap mode via the Bruker software Esprit version
1.9. Subsequently, these maps were quantified using the thin-film
approximation and line profiles of the element concentrations were
obtained.
C. Computational details
The Vienna ab-initio Simulation Package (VASP)18,19 was
used to perform density functional theory (DFT) calculations with
projector augmented wave pseudopotentials20 using the range-
separated Heyd–Scuseria–Ernzerhof (HSE) hybrid functional with
a mixing parameter of α ¼ 0:25 and exchange-screening length of
0.13 Å−1. A plane-wave cutoff energy of 400 eV was applied for all
calculations. Ionic positions were relaxed until residual forces were
below 10 meV Å−1. Γ-centered k-point grids of 4 × 4 × 2 and
2 × 2 × 2 were used to sample the Brillouin zones of spinel In2S3
and bixbyite In2O3 structures, respectively. Table I summarizes
experimental and calculated values for structural, electronic, and
thermodynamic properties of In2S3 and In2O3.
III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS
A. Influence of sputtering conditions of the
In2(OxS1−x)3 buffer layers on solar cell parameters
Figure 1 illustrates the influence of the [O]/([O] + [S]) (OOS)
ratio x on the CIGS solar cell parameters. In general, the same
trend is observed for CIGS solar cells with reactive or ceramic
In2(O,S)3 buffer layers either sputtered at RT, 200 °C (only ceramic
targets), or 220 °C substrate temperature: mainly a decrease of
power conversion efficiency (PCE) as depicted in Figs. 1(a)
and 1(e) with increasing oxygen content as a result of strongly
decreasing open-circuit voltage (VOC) values [Figs. 1(b) and 1(f )].
It seems that only low or moderate OOS ratios up to 0.25 are toler-
able for decent cell efficiencies, whereas OOS ratios above 0.75 end
up in almost lethal cells, especially for cells with pure In2O3. The
PCE curve for the cells with the In2(OxS1−x)3 buffers deposited at
RT lies around 4% (absolute) below the values of the 220 °C
samples. Probably, there could be a PCE maximum for moderate
OOS ratios, especially observed for cells deposited at RT (see
Fig. S1 in the supplementary material). Taking a closer look at rep-
resentative J–V curves in Fig. S2 in the supplementary material,we
do not observe a kink or rollover effect, i.e., the solar cells show a
good diode behavior without any cross-over between dark and light
J–V curves. The drop in PCE depending on increasing OOS ratios
is mainly affected by a decreasing VOC, which can be a result of a
pronounced cliff configuration as discussed later. Only the cells
with In2(O0.75S0.25)3 deposited at 200 °C from ceramic targets and
the cells with pure In2O3 deposited at RT and 200 °C (also ceramic
targets) exhibit a strong shunting effect. The FF values in Figs. 1(c)
and 1(g) follow mostly the trend of the decreasing VOC.
Comparing the effect of reactive vs non-reactive sputtering of
In2(OxS1−x)3, it can be recognized that the drop in VOC and FF
with increasing OOS is stronger for the CIGS cells with a reactively
sputtered buffer. We suppose that this finding is due to additional
stress on the sensitive CIGS surface caused by the oxidizing plasma
with increasing O2 concentration during reactive sputtering. JSC is
not affected by increasing OOS ratios, until OOS ≥ 0.75 when the
values decrease significantly for both sputtering approaches. This
observation is supported by selected EQE spectra depicted in Fig. S3
in the supplementary material. We also observed limited PCE values
below 14% for CIGS solar cells with sulfur-free sputtered Ga2O3
buffer layers.26 Niemi et al. also reported a drastic drop in PCE and
FF for CIGS solar cells with reactively sputtered In2(O,S)3 buffers
with more than 1 vol. % O2 in the sputtering gas.11
