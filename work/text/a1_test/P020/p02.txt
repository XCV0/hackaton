Abstract
Recent research on CdTeSe thin-ﬁlm solar cells has indicated that front-junction grading suppresses
nonradiative recombination and improves carrier collection, thereby improving the solar-cell
efﬁciency. However, a comprehensive study encompassing different bandgap-grading proﬁles—such
as linear, nonlinear, and piecewise homogeneous—is required to fully understand their impact on
device performance. Hence, detailed optoelectronic simulations of these solar cells were performed to
determine the effects of compositional grading and absorbing-layer thickness on power-conversion
efﬁciency. The transfer-matrix method was used to calculate the electron-hole-pair (EHP) generation
rate, and a one-dimensional drift-diffusion model was used to determine the EHP recombination
rate, open-circuit voltage, short-circuit current density, power-conversion efﬁciency, and ﬁll factor.
Optimization using the differential evolution algorithm indicates that linearly and nonlinearly graded
CdTeSe photon-absorbing layers of 3000 nm thickness can deliver an efﬁciency of 21.79% and
21.22%, respectively, when the selenium-to-tellurium ratio is not allowed to exceed 2/3. Also, the
simulations indicate that a two-layered piecewise-homogeneous CdTeSe photon-absorbing layer,
with a thickness of 3000 nm, can deliver 22.13% efﬁciency. If higher selenium content is permitted,
the maximum efﬁciency attainable rises to 24.68%.
1. Introduction
Thin-ﬁlm solar cells (TFSCs) with a cadmium-telluride (CdTe) photon-absorbing layer are the second most
common solar cells in the global marketplace after crystalline-silicon (c-Si) solar cells [1]. Although CdTe
TFSCs’ performance has improved signiﬁcantly in last two decades, their power-conversion efﬁciency (denoted
by η) is still far below the Shockley–Queisser (SQ) limit. While η reaches 82% of the SQ limit of c-Si solar cells,
η is still restricted to 68% of the SQ limit for CdTe TFSCs [2], the SQ limit for CdTe TFSCs being 32.7% [2].
The primary η-limiting factors for CdTe TFSCs are: (i) small charge-carrier lifetimes; (ii) low charge-carrier
densities [3]; and (iii) low open-circuit voltage Voc [3, 4] caused by radiative and non-radiative recombination
that occurs through band tails, defects, and lower-bandgap states causing sub-bandgap absorption [1]. Also,
interfacial recombination and the charge-carrier selectivity of the back-contact lower both Voc and η [4].
Another limiting factor for large-scale production and reduction of the levelized cost of electricity (LCoE) with
CdTe TFSCs is the scarcity of tellurium [5].

been found to increase the short-circuit current density Jsc [8], passivate CdTe grains and grain boundaries [7],
