4. Conclusion
In this work, the limits of device efﬁciency, transparency, and
LUE have been studied in TPV devices based on the integration
of a nano a-Si:H layer between optimized selective contacts. The
study performed corroborates that the thickness of the absorber
layer is crucial to the overall performance of TPV)devices. As
absorber thickness increases, transparency and color accuracy
decrease. This tradeoff is essential for applications like BIPV,
where both energy efﬁciency and esthetic considerations are
important. Thicker absorbers improve energy conversion at
the expense of transparency and potential color distortion.
There is an optimal thickness range, between 50 and 150 nm,
where a suitable balance between PCE and transparency is
achieved, yielding maximum LUE values in the range of
LUE ≈ 1.25–1.33%, corresponding to devices with PCE values
up to 2.9% and transparency (APT) in the range of 40–60%.
These constitute world-record LUE values for inorganic TPV
devices. On the other hand, devices are characterized by good
Voc values (in the range of 788–817 mV) and collection efﬁcien-
cies (in the range of 80–90%) for the whole device series, which
can be related to the optimized selective contacts implemented in
these devices. The highest carrier collection efﬁciencies of 90%
are achieved within the thinner absorbers (thickness ≈ 22 nm)
and this partially compensates for potential efﬁciency losses
related to the deterioration of μτeff observed at these very low
absorber thicknesses, likely related to the inﬂuence of charged
defects at the interfaces.
Finally, reﬂection and EQE/IQE measurements indicate the
presence of reﬂection losses mainly located in the 450 nm spec-
tral range, with the 220 nm AZO layer notably contributing to
these losses. Correction of these losses by suitable adjustment
of TCO thickness and/or implementation of suitable antireﬂec-
tive coatings could allow for a relevant increase of LUE beyond
values in the range of 1.6%.
5. Experimental Section
Sample Preparation: Transparent solar cells fabricated in this work pre-
sented the structure SLG/FTO/AZO/a-SiCx:H(n)/a-Si:H/V2O5/AZO.
Commercial SLG/FTO substrates (Merck; Rsh = 10 Ω sq/C01) were used
as substrate and transparent back contact. Prior to deposition, substrates
were rinsed in distilled water, dried in Ar ﬂux, and afterward cleaned by
subsequent 10 min ultrasonic baths in isopropanol and ultrapure H2O
at room temperature, followed by a drying step in Ar ﬂux. A 45 nm
AZO ﬁlm was grown in an ALD system (Savannah S200, Cambridge
Nanotech) at 200 °C, using precursors diethylzinc (DEZ 95%, STREM),
ultrapure H2O, and trimethylaluminium (TMA 99%, STREM) and using
N2 as purging gas. Sixteen supercycles at a 19:1 DEZ:TMA ratio were
performed, with 19 H2O/N2/DEZ/N2 (20 ms/5 s/15 ms/5 s) cycles and
1 TMA/N2 (s/20 ms/5 s) cycle. After the AZO, the following step consisted
in depositing an ETL ﬁlm of 12 nm, deposited by PE-CVD (Elettrorava) with
an RF power supply of 13.56 MHz at 6 W. Substrate temperature was mea-
sured at 148–150 °C during deposition, with a mixed 36/12/30 sccm SiH4/
CH4/SiPH3 atmosphere and with deposition time of 3 min, Afterward,
intrinsic a-Si:H ultrathin ﬁlms of 24, 38, 56, 74, 80, 129, and 288 nm were
deposited by PE-CVD (Elettrorava) with an RF power supply of 13.56 MHz
at 6 W. Substrate temperature was measured at 148–150 °C during depo-
sition, with a mixed 36/12 sccm SiH4/CH4 atmosphere and with deposi-
tion time of 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 17, and 34 min, respectively. To ﬁnish the solar
cells, the HTL and front contact were deposited in a single sequential ALD
process without breaking vacuum. V2O5 was grown at 125 °C, using tet-
rakis(ethylmethylamino)-vanadium (IV) (C12H32N4V) (TEMAV 99%,
STREM) and ultrapure H2O using again N2 as a carrier and purging
gas. Two hundred supercycles of H2O/N2/TEMAV/N2 (2 s/5 s/5 s/5 s)
were carried out. AZO was deposited at a 19:1 ratio for 75 supercycles
at T = 175 °C. Once the sample was fully grown, the active area cells were
isolated using a photolithographic process followed by a wet chemical
etching process that effectively isolated the cells from each other. First,
photoresist at room temperature was spread on the surface of the samples
and was statically spin coating for 30 s at 30.000 rpm. Immediately after, it
was taken to a furnace at 105 °C for 20 min in order to evaporate the resid-
ual solvent. Then, UV photolithography was performed with a positive
mask with square patterns deﬁning a cell active area of 0.25 cm2.
Afterward, an etching process was performed using an isotropic etching
solution of HF þ HNO3 to attack and remove the thin ﬁlms down to the
FTO, which remained unaffected. As a result, the active area protected
with the resin would remain and the subcells would be effectively isolated
from each other allowing for J–V characterization without the need for a
mask to deﬁne the active area during measurements (Figure 2).
