The devices studied in this research are based on the integra-
tion of a nanometric hydrogenated amorphous silicon layer
(a-Si:H)[9] that is sandwiched between two charge-carrier selec-
tive contacts: a hole transport layer (HTL) and an electron trans-
port layer (ETL).[10] These selective layers play a crucial role in
separating the photogenerated charge carriers. The HTL allows
the ﬂow of holes while blocking electrons and the ETL does a
complementary work, allowing the pass of electrons while
blocking holes. Various materials have been investigated for these
layers, including MoOx,[11] V2Ox,[12] and NiOx [13] as HTLs, and
silicon-doped, AZO and TiOx as ETLs. From this study, the opti-
mal device conﬁguration includes vanadium oxide (V2Ox) as the
HTL[14] and n-type phosphorous-doped hydrogenated amorphous
silicon carbide (a-SiCx(n)) as the ETL.[15] The transparent conduc-
tive oxides (TCOs) chosen to complete the device were ﬂuorine-
doped tin oxide (FTO) for the back contact and aluminum-doped
zinc oxide (AZO)[16] for the front contact[17] enabling connection
to an external circuit. The absorber material where photogener-
ated electron-hole pairs are created was intrinsic hydrogenated
amorphous silicon (a-Si:H). This study focuses on analyzing
the impact of absorber thickness (varied from 22 to 233 nm)
on the performance of the TPV device while keeping other
layers constant.[12] The absorber thickness is critical for optimiza-
tion, as increasing the thickness leads to higher photocurrent
generation,[18] that is, a major number of electron-hole pairs
photogenerated per second,[17] which is reﬂected in an increase
in the short-circuit current density ( Jsc). However, as one could
expect it is also observed that a higher thickness results in lower
average photopic transmittance (APT) and color rendering
index (CRI).[19]
It is relevant to highlight that these devices are made to avoid
the use of the cataloged Critical Raw Material (CRM) or toxic/
hazardous materials. In this sense, diborane (which is usually
used for the HTL deposition on traditional amorphous silicon
devices) has been avoided. It is worth mentioning that all the layers
are deposited at relatively low temperatures (below 200 °C) result-
ing in a low thermal-budget fabrication process. Additionally, the
different deposition techniques involved in the device manufactur-
ing allow a conformal and controlled deposition of the different
nanometric layers that compose the solar cell structure.
2. Experimental Design, Results, and Discussion
Planar ultrathin TPV devices were fabricated with the follow-
ing architecture: SLG/FTO/AZO/a-SiCx:H(n)/a-Si:H/V2Ox/AZO.
This work focuses on studying the effect of varying the thickness
of the absorber layers, while AZO, a-SiCx:H(n) and V2Ox are kept
unchanged for a more accurate comparison. For this study, seven
samples were prepared with the following absorber layer thick-
ness: 22, 35, 56, 70, 76, 123, and 233 nm. The absorber deposi-
tion was carried out using a mixture of 36 sccm of silane (SiH4)
and 12 sccm of methane (CH4). Throughout the process, pres-
sure, temperature, and gas ﬂow conditions were kept constant,
with only the deposition time varied to adjust the absorber thick-
ness, as shown in Figure 1.
Once each sample was fully grown, the active area of the cells
was deﬁned and isolated using a conventional UV photolitho-
graphic process (Figure 2a), followed by wet chemical etchings.
In this way, an effective electrical isolation of the cells is achieved
and setting, at the same time, the active cell area (0.25 cm2), as
shown in Figure 2b.
