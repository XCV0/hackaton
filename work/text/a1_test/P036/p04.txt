away from the diagram center, indicating a move toward more
saturated, less neutral colors. Thinner absorbers, like sample S0
(22 nm), remain closer to neutral white, making them ideal for
applications where transparency and color ﬁdelity are crucial,
such as in windows or building facades. Conversely, thicker
absorbers, such as sample S6 (233 nm), enhance light absorption
but cause a noticeable shift in color perception, which may be
less desirable in visually sensitive applications. This analysis
highlights the importance of balancing energy efﬁciency with
esthetic considerations in the design of TPV devices, especially
those using absorbers with bandgap energy lying within the visi-
ble range.

The reﬂectance spectrum analysis in the CIE 1931 diagram
shows that the samples, despite varying absorber thicknesses,
reﬂect light in similar color ranges, primarily in bluish tones.
The samples exhibit minimal variation in color perception, with
thicker absorbers slightly enhancing reﬂectance intensity,
particularly in speciﬁc tones like green and blue. Luminosity
differences suggest that thinner absorbers, like S0, appear
brighter. These ﬁndings indicate that absorber thickness has
alesssigniﬁcant impact on reﬂectance compared to transmit-
tance, with the overall reﬂectance being more inﬂuenced by the
properties of the top layer. This is particularly relevant for appli-
cations where the visual appearance under natural light is
important.[22]

to those observed directly under daylight without the TPV
device.[22] It is a parameter measured between 0 and 100.[23]
