3.2.1. J–V Characteristics: Power Conversion Efﬁciency and Light
Utilization Efﬁciency
The analysis of these solar cells shows that increasing absorber
thickness enhances current generation and overall efﬁciency
(PCE) but reduces transparency (APT) and color neutrality
(CRI). J–V measurements were made under standard test condi-
tions using a AAA-class solar simulator (1 kW m/C02 AM1.5G solar
spectrum and 25 °C). Table 2 summarizes the main photovoltaic
parameters, including APT, CRI, and LUE (light utilization efﬁ-
ciency). This last parameter has been proposed as a suitable ﬁg-
ure of merit for TPV devices[20] and is given by the product of the
PCE by the APT. Additionally, the statistics for all the optoelec-
tronic parameters for each sample are presented in Figure S1,
Supporting Information.
Figure 6 shows that increasing the absorber thickness in TPV
devices improves Jsc (almost linear increase with the absorber
thickness as shown in Figure 7b) and, consequently the PCE also
increases. On the other hand, the LUE shows a more complex
behavior since it balances transparency with photovoltaic
performance. The LUE initially increases with the absorber thick-
ness, showing a maximum value in the range of LUE ≈ 1.3% for
thicknesses in the range of 56–70 nm, and decreases for higher
thicknesses. On the other hand, it is interesting to remark on the
relatively small changes of the LUE in the range of thicknesses
between 35 and 123 nm (S1–S5), with LUE values typically
between 1.21 and 1.33%. This indicates an optimal absorber
thickness range between 35 and 123 nm where PCE and trans-
parency are well balanced. Beyond this range, further increases
in thickness enhance PCE but drastically reduce transparency,
causing a higher decrease in the LUE. In addition, we also
observe a decrease in the ﬁll factor (FF) for the thicker absorbers,
which is likely related to increased internal resistance and recom-
bination losses.
On the other hand, it is also interesting to remark on the rele-
vant values of Voc that are achieved through the whole device
series, with values in the range 785–815 mV (with a record value
of 817 mV for the thicker absorber layer). These values are close
to the record Voc around 900 mV that has been achieved in stan-
dard (opaque) a-Si:H solar cells.
3.2.2. VIM Analysis
The measurement of the J(V ) curve under variable illumination
(VIM)[24] has proven to be an effective method to identify the
mechanisms limiting the efﬁciency of amorphous silicon p-i-n
solar cells and is expected to be equally effective in our devices,
whose structure is very similar. As in p-i-n devices, the collected
photocurrent depends on the drift of carriers by the internal elec-
tric ﬁeld generated by the contacts, and consequently, the collec-
tion efﬁciency (χc) must be a function of the applied voltage.[25]
For devices with voltage-dependent photocurrent, it is com-
mon to observe a range of illumination where the slope of the
J(V ) curve under short-circuit conditions (Gsc) is proportional
to the short-circuit current ( Jsc). Consequently, the product of
Jsc and the dynamic short-circuit resistance (Rsc = 1/Gsc) is con-
stant and is referred to as the collection voltage (VC).[26,27]
