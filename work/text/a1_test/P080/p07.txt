introduction of the PenAAc layer positively influenced the mor-
phology of the perovskite films. Contact angle measurements were
performed to explore the hydrophobicity of the perovskite films
[Figs. 4(c) and 4(d)]. The control device perovskite film showed a
contact angle of 65.88°, while the PenAAc-modified device perov-
skite film showed a contact angle of 72.80°. The results show that
the perovskite film of the PenAAc-modified device has improved
hydrophobicity in PSCs, which would increase the film stability
and the PSC stability. We further analyzed the film crystallinity
with an X-ray diffractometer (XRD) technique [Fig. 4(e)]. The dif-
fraction peaks at 14.4° and 28.6° in the XRD plots correspond to
the FASnI3 crystals with (100) and (200) lattice facets, respec-
tively.31 The diffraction peak intensities of PenAAc-modified
device films were significantly enhanced compared to the control
device films, which means that the PenAAc could improve the
crystallinity of tin-based perovskite films. This enhancement con-
tributes to the formation of denser perovskite films with fewer
pinholes.32 To investigate the defect states in the perovskite films,
we performed steady-state photoluminescence (PL) tests on perov-
skite films [Fig. 4(f)]. The photoluminescence intensity of the
PenAAc-modified film was higher compared to the control device,
which indicates that the defect states were suppressed.33
III. CONCLUSION
In conclusion, the deep-level defects and voltage loss in lead-
free PSCs were effectively reduced by introducing an interfacial pas-
sivation layer PenAAc. The PenAAc layer significantly suppressed
the density of defective states and facilitated efficient carrier separa-
tion and transport in the perovskite layer. The PenAAc-modified
device achieved a PCE of 7.0% and maintained above 89% of its
initial efficiency even after 1200 h storage in a nitrogen-filled glove-
box, exhibiting excellent interfacial stability. This work provided an
effective passivation scheme for the efficient lead-free PSCs.
