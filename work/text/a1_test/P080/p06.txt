As illustrated in Figs. 2(e) and 2(f ), the PenAAc-modified
device exhibited a smaller τtrans and a larger τrec compared to the
control device. Specifically, τtrans was measured at 0.54 μs for the
control device, while it was 0.48 μs for the PenAAc-modified device.
Meanwhile, τrec increased from 20.04 μs for the control device to
25.22 μs for the PenAAc-modified device (Table II). This indicates
that with the addition of the PenAAc layer, the charge extraction and
transfer in the perovskite layer were enhanced.
The deep energy level defect states generated at the perovskite
contribute significantly to the poor optoelectronic performance
observed. DLTS served as an effective method for detecting these
deep energy level defect densities and quantifying the defect states.
The DLTS profiles of the control device, along with the Arrhenius
curves fitted to these profiles, were shown in Figs. 3(a) and 3(b).
Here, the different defect energy level positions were labeled as D1
and D2, revealing two excitation peaks at varying temperatures
(D1: 267 K, D2: 224 K) for the control device, with the peak at D1
being notably more pronounced. Figures 3(d) and 3(e) presented
the DLTS profiles of the PenAAc-modified device along with the
Arrhenius curves derived from these profiles. Two excitation peaks
were identified at distinct temperatures (D1: 259 K, D2: 236 K) for
the PenAAc-modified device, with the peak at D1 being notably
more pronounced. The specific parameters were detailed in Table III.
Notably, with the introduction of the PenAAc layer, the main defect
energy level at D1 shifts from 0.372 to 0.334 eV [Figs. 3(c) and 3(f )].
This change indicates that the PenAAc layer facilitates an increased
emission rate of carriers from the deep energy level defect center,
thereby enhancing carrier separation and collection efficiency while
extending carrier lifetime.30 Furthermore, the density of states for
deep energy level defects and the defect capture cross-sectional
area (σ) have been significantly reduced from 9.58 × 1016 and
1.13 × 10−17 cm2 to 1.11 × 1015 and 4.48 × 10−18 cm2, respectively.
This reduction will effectively reduce the deep defect-assisted non-
radiative losses, leading to a notable improvement in Voc. These
findings are consistent with the results illustrated in the J–V char-
acteristic curve.
