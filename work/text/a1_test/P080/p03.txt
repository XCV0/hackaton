there is still a lack of systematic exploration of defects and voltage

In this study, an interfacial passivation strategy based on
PenAAc was proposed in tin-based perovskite, which achieved a
suppressed defect density and reduced voltage loss. The effects of
the PenAAc passivation layer on film morphology, crystallinity,
and device performance were systematically investigated, and the
passivation mechanisms in devices were revealed by combining
space charge limiting current (SCLC), electrochemical impedance
spectroscopy (EIS), and deep energy level transient spectroscopy
(DLTS). Ultimately, the PenAAc-modified tin-based perovskite devices
achieved a PCE of 7.0% and maintained above 89% of its initial effi-
ciency even after 1200 h storage in a nitrogen-filled glovebox.
II. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
We first fabricated a series of inverted structure PSCs using
the configuration ITO/PEDOT:PSS/PenAAc/PVSK/C60/BCP/Cu
[Fig. 1(a)]. The chemical structure of PenAAc and a schematic
diagram of PenAAc film preparation were shown in Fig. S1 of the
supplementary material, and the optimal concentration of PenAAc
was determined as 2 mmol/l (Fig. S2 in the supplementary material).
The champion PenAAc-modified device showed a notable increase
in overall PCE compared to the control device [Figs. 1(b) and 1(c)],
with an efficiency of 7.0% under a forward scan, an FF of 70.7%, a
Jsc of 18.7 mA/cm2,andaVoc of 0.53 V. During the reverse scan, the
device exhibited an efficiency of 6.4%, an FF of 67.9%, a Jsc of
18.4 mA/cm2,andaVoc of 0.51 V, yielding a hysteresis index of
8.6%. For the control device, we recorded a champion PCE of 5.5%,
an FF of 61.8%, a Jsc of 18.3 mA/cm2,andaVoc of 0.48 V during the
forward scan. In the reverse scan, the efficiency dropped to 4.8%,
with an FF of 56.5%, the same Jsc of 18.3 mA/cm2,andaVoc of
