Figure S3 is the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of Sn3O4
and Y-Sn3O4, which is consistent with our previous
results.16,38 The XRD peaks in the range from 20/C14 to 40/C14
shifted to a higher angle as shown in Figure 1D, indicating
a contraction of the lattice. Despite the lower accuracy of
the XRD test, the superposition of many crystalline signals
resulted in the observation of a larger crystalline spacing
not detected by the TEM. The ion radius of Y3+,Sn2+ and
Sn4+ are 95, 118 and 74 pm, respectively. Therefore, it is
likely that the Y3+ has taken the place of Sn2+, causing
the contraction of the lattice and also introducing an addi-
tional electron for n doping. Therefore, during the hydro-
thermal reaction, if the system has a large amount of Y3+,
too much Sn2+ is displaced, leading to the formation of
SnO, consistent with Figure S1.
The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) test was
executed for elemental composition analysis to identify
the existence of Y. Figure 2A is the XPS result for Y 3d.
Compared to the undoped Sn3O4, a significant Y element
signal was measured in Y-Sn3O4 at 158.8 and 160.5 eV.39
The actual Y-element content of the 5% Y-doped sample
was 2.4% (ratio of Sn elements) by quantitative calcula-
tion. The disappeared Y elements were probably lost dur-
ing the synthetic washing process. To investigate whether
the Y element was doped into the lattice, an angle-
resolved XPS (ARXPS) test was conducted to investigate
the distribution of yttrium elements.40,41 Since the escape
depth of electrons is finite, the longer the escape route of
electrons with angle compared to those without angle,
the weaker the signal that can be detected. The results of
ARXPS were shown in Figure 2B. By changing the inci-
dence angle, the amount of Y element was quantified for
various depths. The Y content at 0/C14,30/C14, and 60/C14 was
2.42%, 2.40%, and 2.62%, respectively. In addition, we
used Ar ions to etch the samples (Figure 2C), and the
measured Y-element contents before and after etching
were comparable 2.30% and 2.14%. These indicated that
Y was uniformly doped within the material at different
depths.
