Abstract
The two-dimensional mixed-valence tin oxide Sn3O4 with a low defect density
of states and suitable energy band structure has shown great potential as the
electron transport layer (ETL) for perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, the
electron density of Sn3O4 is low and should be modulated to demonstrate its
application for more efficient PSCs. Here, Sn3O4 is doped with yttrium (Y) to
optimize its electronic properties and improve the performance of PSCs based
on Sn3O4 ETL. The conductivity of Sn3O4 is improved from 6.6 /C2 10/C04 to
2.05 /C2 10/C03 Sm/C01 with increased electron density by Y doping. Meanwhile,
the Fermi level of Sn3O4 is also upshifted after doping. The increased electron
density and upshifted Fermi level enhance the electron extraction capability of
Sn3O4 and built-in potential in PSCs. By doping the Sn3O4 ETL with Y, the
power conversion efficiency of PSCs is successfully boosted to 23.05%.

1 | INTRODUCTION
The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite
solar cells (PSCs) has been improved rapidly from 3.8% to
25.7% in the past few years.1 Electron transport layer
(ETL), which plays a role in blocking holes and
extracting electrons, is an important functional layer in
PSCs.2–4 Recently, 2D electron transport materials
(ETMs) draw lots of attention to be applied as ETLs for
PSCs.5–10 The intrinsic confinement of a 2D material in
the out-of-plane direction can produce a perfect surface
without dangling bonds, providing a perfect substrate for
perovskite growth and charge transfer.11,12 Zhao et al.
used SnS2 as an ETL to achieve heterogeneous nucleation
of perovskite on sheet structure and reduced interfacial
defects, ultimately obtaining a PCE of 20.12%.13 Huang
et al. used TiS2 to modify the SnO2 interface, resulting in
energy level matching, a lower density of defect states
