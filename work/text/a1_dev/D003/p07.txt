and Y-Sn3O4 showed good stability, and the efficiency of
both remained 93% of the initial efficiency for 1000 h in
dry air (RH = 10%).
To further clarify the effect of Y doping on charge
transfer and built-in electric field, we performed correla-
tion analysis on films and devices. Transient photo-
luminescence spectroscopy (TRPL) was used to test the
ability of film to extract charges.57 The results are shown
in Figure 6A and the corresponding fitting results are in
Table S3. Two decay processes existed in perovskite on
ETL. The fast process corresponded to the carrier being
extracted at the interface and the slow process represen-
ted the radiative recombination in the film. Since the
extraction process did not exist in the sample on glass, a
first-order decay exponent fit was used. From the fitted
results, the carrier lifetime of perovskite decayed rapidly
from 1144 ns on glass to 104 and 85 ns on Sn3O4 and Y-
Sn3O4, respectively. The charge transfer rate (kET) and
extraction efficiency (ηET) was calculated. Compared to
Sn3O4, the charge transfer rates of perovskite films on Y-
Sn3O4 accelerated from 8.7 /C2 106 s/C01 to 10.8 /C2 106 s/C01
and the extraction efficiency raised from 91% to 93%.
Consistent results can be observed from the steady-state
photoluminescence spectroscopy (PL) (Figure 6B).
The fluorescence intensity of the perovskite films decays
a lot in the presence of ETL, indicating the effective
charge extraction.58 The magnified plot shows that the
fluorescence intensity of the perovskite on Y-Sn3O4 film
is lower compared to the perovskite on the Sn3O4 film.
These demonstrate that Sn3O4 and Y-Sn3O4 can effec-
tively extract charges from perovskite, and Y doping
enhances the charge extraction capability of Sn3O4.
Capacitance versus voltage analysis (Mott–Schottky
analysis) was used to verify the variation of the built-in
potential (Vbi) in PSCs based on Sn3O4 and Y-Sn3O4
(Figure 6C).59 The Vbi of the device based on Sn3O4 and
Y-Sn3O4 is 0.96 and 1.08 V. The enhancement of the Vbi
is attributed to the upward shift of the Fermi energy level
shown in Figure 3D, which ultimately enhances the sepa-
ration of carriers in the device. Electrochemical imped-
ance spectroscopy (EIS) was used to analyze the charge
transfer process in the device (Figure 6D). The semicircle
in the high frequency range corresponds to the imped-
ance of the charge transfer at the interface.60 Consistent
with the TRPL results in films, the semicircle of the
device based on Y- Sn3O4 ETL is reduced, implying the
more rapid charge transfer. The transient photocurrent
(TPC) decay shows consistent conclusions. In contrast to
Sn3O4, the transient photocurrent decay time of Y-Sn3O4
device decreased from 20 to 8 μs. (Figure 6E). In addition,
the transient photovoltage (TPV) decay time increased
from 70 to 80 μs for the device based on Y-Sn3O4 as ETL
(Figure 6F), which is attributed to the suppressed non-
radiative recombination.53
