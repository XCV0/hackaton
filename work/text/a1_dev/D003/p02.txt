and an improved PCE of 21.7%.14 Agresti et al. used
Ti3C2Tx with various terminated groups to tune the work
function, engineer the perovskite/ETL interface, and
finally achieve a PCE of 20.14%.15 In our previous work,
the mixed-valence tin oxide Sn3O4 was demonstrated to
be a suited 2D material as ETL for PSCs due to its low
defect density of states and suitable energy level arrange-
ment.16 Sn3O4-based PSCs exhibited higher efficiency
and stability than SnO2-based ones. However, the elec-
tron concentration of Sn3O4 is lower than that of SnO2,
limiting the conductivity of Sn3O4 and the performance
of corresponding PSCs.
Elemental doping is a practical strategy to modify the
electronic properties of metal oxides.17,18 Some doping
methods change the electronic properties by achieving
interposition doping or substitution doping of elements,
providing additional carriers.19 The other methods induce
the formation of oxygen vacancies, which ultimately affect
the electronic properties of metal oxide semiconductors.20–23
Noh et al. enhanced the conductivity of SnO2 ETL in PSCs
by Zr doping and the conduction band is shifted upward,
which favored energy level alignment with perovskite.24 Shi
et al. used B to dope TiO2, whose conductivity was
improved from 6.00 /C2 10/C03 to 1.81 /C2 10/C02 mS cm/C01.25 Al,
Sn, N, C, F, Zr, Y, Nb, Pt, and lanthanides have been suc-
cessfully doped into metal oxides ETMs and finally
improved the performance of PSCs.26–33 There are also
some attempts on doping Sn3O4 to improve its photo-
catalytic and sensor properties.34–37 Zeng et al. doped Sn3O4
with F to enhance its capability of degrading organic pollut-
ants.34 Balgude et al. used N to dope Sn3O4 and the doped
Sn3O4 showed a higher photocatalytic activity for hydrogen
production which was eight times that of pristine Sn3O4.36
Ma et al. doped Sn3O4 with Ce and better sensitivity to
formaldehyde was obtained.37
Herein, we doped Sn3O4 with Y to improve its elec-
tronic properties for enhanced performance of PSCs. The
introduction of Y element into the lattice increased the
electron carrier density and conductivity of Sn3O4. Mean-
while, the Y doping also upshifted the Fermi energy level
of Sn3O4. Y-doped Sn3O4 exhibited faster and more effi-
cient charge extraction from perovskites than the
undoped one. As a result, Y doping improved the PCE of
PSCs based on Sn3O4 ETL to 23.05% with an enhanced
open circuit voltage (VOC) of 1.14 V, an enhanced short
circuit current density (JSC) of 24.89 mA cm/C02, and an
enhanced fill factor (FF) of 81.2%.
