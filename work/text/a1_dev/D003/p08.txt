3 | CONCLUSION
In summary, we explored the n doping of Sn3O4 mixed
valence tin oxide with YCl3 to improve its potential as
ETL for PSCs. The doping amount influenced the proper-
ties of Sn3O4 and excessive YCl3 resulted in the phase
segregation of Sn3O4 with the formation of SnO. Five per-
ccent Y doping optimized the electronic properties of
Sn3O4 successfully by improving the electron density and
conductivity, together with up shifted Fermi level. The
optimized electronic properties strengthened the poten-
tial of Sn3O4 as ETL for PSCs by enhancing its electron
extraction and transport capability together with opti-
mized energy level alignment, and boosted the PCE of
PSCs to 23.05%. Our research further demonstrated the
promising application of mixed valence tin oxides for
efficient PSCs.
4 | EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
4.1 | Synthesis of Sn3O4 hydrosol
All synthesis processes were carried out in the ambient
condition. To synthesize Sn3O4, 282 mg SnCl2/C12H2O and
219 mg SnCl4/C15H2O were dissolved in 25 ml deionized
water and stirred for 10 min to form a clarified solution.
To synthesize Y doped Sn3O4, 5.69, 28.44, and 56.88 mg
YCl3/C16H2O were added into the clarified solution. One
milliliter ammonia water was then added into the solu-
tion with constant stirring and ice-water bath to lead to
precipitation of tin cations. The whole process requires
the removal of air from the container. The precipitation
was washed and centrifuged with deionized water for
three times. The washed precipitation was re-dispersed in
25 ml deionized water with addition of 1 ml of ammonia
water. The suspension was transferred to a hydrothermal
kettle. The kettle was heated at 180/C14 for 3 h. After the
kettle cooled to room temperature, the obtained
tin oxides in water was sonicated. The dispersions were
filtered with 220 nm hydrophilic filter before use.
4.2 | Device fabrication
ITO substrates were cleaned by sonication in ITO deter-
gent, water, and ethanol, and treated under ultraviolet
ozone for 20 min before use. Sn3O4 and Y-Sn3O4 films
are prepared by spinning coating corresponding disper-
sions onto ITO glasses at 4000 rpm. for 30 s for three
times. These substrates were then heated at 140/C14C for
30 mins on a hot plate. For perovskite deposition, a pre-
cursor solution was obtained by dissolving 484 mg PbI2,
146.2 mg FAI, 23.85 mg MAI, 12 mg CsI, 11 mg PbCl2,
4.5 mg MABr into 560 μl Dimethyl Formamide (DMF)
and 155 μl dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) at 50/C14C for
30 mins. The solution was deposited on the prepared tin
oxides substrates by a consecutive two-step spin coating
process at 1000 and 6000rpm. for 10 and 30 s with acceler-
ation rate of 200 and 2000 rpm/s. During the second step,
70 μl chlorobenzene (CB) was dropped onto the spinning
substrate within 1 to 1.5 s. Then, the substrate was dried
on a hot plate at 100/C14C for 30 min. For hole transporting
layer (HTL) deposition, a solution of Spiro-OMeTAD
(90 mg), tert-butylpyridine (35.5 μl), FK209 (200 mg/ml,
5 μl) and lithium bis (trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide
(17.5 μl, 520 mg/ml in acetonitrile) in 1 ml CB was pre-
pared. The solution was deposited on the perovskite/tin
oxides thin film at 4000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, an Ag coun-
ter electrode about 100 nm was deposited by thermal evap-
oration. The active area is 2 mm /C2 2 mm by superposing
ITO and Ag. The device structure used to measure conduc-
tivity is ITO/ETL/Ag and the ETL is spin coated 20 times
to a thickness of approximately 100 nm.
