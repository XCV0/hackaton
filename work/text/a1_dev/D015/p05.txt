Figure 3 shows the PL spectra and TRPL decay curves of the
devices measured by exciting them either through the frontside
or through the backside. For both front and back illumination,
the device with CuxCryOz buffer layer shows higher PL intensity,
indicating lower nonradiative recombination compared to the
reference cell which is Cu-doped by CuCl2 treatment. The
TRPL data (Figure 3c,d) show signiﬁcant increase in carrier life-
time when a CuxCryOz buffer layer is inserted at the back contact.
The carrier lifetime measured at the backside (Figure 3c) shows
over 130% increase (1.3 to 3.1 ns), while that estimated for the
front interface region is higher by 38% (22.5 to 31.2 ns) with
CuxCryOz buffer layer (Figure 3d). The carrier lifetime and cor-
responding yield parameters of the samples are presented in
Table 3. The increase in both carrier lifetime and PL intensity
conﬁrms the suppression of nonradiative recombination in
the presence of CuxCryOz back-buffer. Subedi et al. reported car-
rier lifetimes of 3.4 and 41.1 ns, respectively, from back and front
illumination with CuxAlOy as the back-buffer layer in identical
CdTe/CdS devices.[4] The beneﬁcial role of CuxCryOz buffer in
reducing the trap-assisted recombination is further revealed in
the light-intensity-dependent VOC data in Figure 4.
The charge extraction and recombination in a device can be
qualitatively analyzed from the dependence of JSC and VOC on
the incident light power. The JSC is governed by the power law,
JSC ∝ Iα, where I is incident light power and the value of exponent
α is related to the charge collection efﬁciency which is independent
of light intensity.[39] The dependence of JSC and VOC on light inten-
sity when illuminated from the back is shown in Figure 4. The
value of α close to 1 (Figure 4a) indicates efﬁcient charge transport
in the device; in other words, there is no signiﬁcant
recombination-assisted carrier loss. Figure 4b shows the incident
light intensity dependence of VOC, yielding a slope (1.31 kBT/q)for
device with CuxCryOz buffer layer and (2.45 kBT/q)fortheCu-
doped reference device. This again shows reduced trap-assisted
recombination in devices with CuxCryOz buffer.
3. Conclusions
We have demonstrated solution-processed CuxCryOz as a prom-
ising back-buffer layer for CdTe/CdS devices in mono- and bifa-
cial conﬁgurations. In a monofacial device, after curing the
buffer at the optimal conditions of 200 °C for 8 min of annealing
duration, the VOC and FF improved, obtaining 14.4% efﬁciency.
Annealing duration beyond 8 min resulted in J–V rollover, pos-
sibly a result of Cu migration from back contact region. With the
CuxCryOz buffer, the carrier lifetime increased signiﬁcantly both
at front and back regions of the device. In the case of bifacial
device, a notable improvement in device parameters was
observed for backside illumination in the presence of the
CuxCryOz buffer. The percentage increase in VOC, JSC, FF,
and PCE are, respectively, 5.5% (ΔVOC ¼ 42 mV), 100%
(ΔJSC ¼ 7.9 mA cm/C02), 18.4% (ΔFF ¼ 0.095), and 153%
(ΔPCE ¼ 4.6%) with respect to the reference device. The bifacial-
ity factors for the devices with and without the CuxCryOz buffer
are 0.60 and 0.34, respectively. The increase in minority carrier
lifetime at both frontside and backside of the device is attributed
to the reduction of interface recombination. The successful dem-
onstration of CuxCryOz as a back contact interfacial layer to CdTe
is a signiﬁcant step forward to realize bifacial thin-ﬁlm solar cells.
