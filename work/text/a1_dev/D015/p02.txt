bifacial performance.[25] Recently, we reported improved bifacial
performance in CdTe/CdS devices using a CuI nanoparticle-based
hole transport layer (HTL), achieving a bifaciality factor of 0.47.[26]
Subedi et al. demonstrated 8.0% efﬁciency for a back-illuminated
CdTe device with CuAlOx back-buffer, resulting in a bifaciality fac-
tor of 0.62.[4] Another interesting buffer is CuCrOx, which is a wide
bandgap (3.2 eV) p-type delafossite material with high optical trans-
parency in the visible region.[27,28] Crystal structure of CuCrO2 has
twostackingformsfortheCrO6 octahedron along the c-axis, such
as 3 R and 2 H forms; out of this, 3 R-CuCrO2 is more stable and
has better p-type conductivity. It is known that CuCrO2 releases Cu
ions which diffuse into the material[29] and the conductive proper-
ties of which can be improved by doping. For example, the Mg-
alloyed CuCrOx has high conductivity of /C25220 S cm/C01.[30]
Due to the high conductivity, CuCrOx is used as a HTL
in organic, perovskite, and dye-sensitized solar cells
(DSSCs);[29,31,32] the holes are due to the Cu vacancies, interstitial
oxygen, and other extrinsic acceptor defects. The work function
of CuCrOx is in the range 5.1–5.25 eV with median value of
5.19 eV.[31] In both DSSC and perovskite devices, it was observed
that the device parameters are slightly better with CuCrOx:Mg
than that with CuCrOx. The hysteresis was in general less for
CuCrOx-based HTLs and least for CuCrOx:Mg.[31] The formation
enthalpy of CuCrOx is much lower than many of the transport
layers such as CdS, CuI, and CrOx used in perovskites and slightly
lower than Cr2O3, indicating that CuCrOx has relatively higher sta-
bility or less likely to decompose, thus having minimal elemental
diffusion than its counterparts.[31] The superiority of CuCrOx over
Cr2O3 is that the Cu alloying increases the work function, electric
conductivity, and carrier mobility. Hence, in perovskite devices, it
was observed that a CuCrOx HTL optimized for the Cu content
has better hole collection ability and in turn higher performance.
Further, it was reported that the perovskite layer grown over
CuCrOx has better polycrystalline quality and surface morphology
compared to that grown over CrOx.[32] To the best of our knowl-
edge, this potential material has not been explored for CdTe solar
cells. In this work, we demonstrate solution-processed CuCrOx as
a transparent back-buffer layer in CdTe/CdS solar cells. Mono-
and bifacial devices were fabricated by using Au and ITO as back
electrodes, respectively. Efﬁciency of the monofacial device
improved by 3.6% over the reference device when a very thin
CuCrOx layer was inserted between CdTe and the Au electrode,
whereas the bifacial device demonstrated efﬁciencies of 7.6%
and 12.5% when illuminated from back and front, respectively.
2. Results and Discussion
Monofacial CdTe/CdS devices with Au as the back electrode were
fabricated with and without a buffer at the back contact. The
device structure is glass/FTO/CdS/CdTe/CuxCryOz/Au for
monofacial device and Au was replaced by ITO for bifacial cell
(Figure S1, Supporting Information). Contrary to the expecta-
tion, it was found from SEM images that only a discontinues
buffer layer was formed on the CdTe surface. An enlarged view
of the SEM image reveals CdTe grains decorated with nanopar-
ticles with little evidence of good coverage over the ﬁlm surface
(Figure S2b, Supporting Information). The material loading in
the buffer layer was so scarce that the EDXS counts of the
elements (Cu and Cr) are not above the noise level. Further,
the low-grazing-angle XRD does not show the signatures of
CuCrOx or the precursor compounds. Multiple spin coats of
the precursor followed by drying after each coating was not suc-
cessful in forming a thin layer over CdTe surface; similarly, there
were no encouraging results in depositing the buffer over plain
glass or conducting glass substrates. Since the material charac-
terization of the buffer layer on CdTe surface was unsuccessful,
instead, we performed the studies with powder samples prepared
using the same precursor solution. The precursor solution was
poured over a heated glass substrate, dried, and the powder was
annealed at 200, 300, and 600 °C under continuous air ﬂow to
remove the volatile matter. Annealing temperature 200 °C was
selected to replicate the original annealing condition of the buffer
layer applied over CdTe. XRD patterns show that the sample
annealed at 200 °C is amorphous and those annealed at higher
temperatures are a mixture of Cr2O3, CuCr2O4, and CuCrO2
(Figure S3, Supporting Information). The EDXS data in
Table ST1 show that the stoichiometry is far from the ideal empiri-
cal formula CuCrO2, coinciding with the XRD result. Based on the
aforementioned discussions, the proposed empirical formula of
the buffer layer is CuxCryOz, and here onwards we use the same
for describing the buffer layer developed in this work. The
bandgap and electron afﬁnity of CuCrOx reported in literature
are /C253.2 and /C252.1 eV respectively.[33] Based on the values reported
in literature for the band-edge positions of all the layers
(Table ST2), we constructed the noncontact band diagram which
shows that CuxCryOz is a potential hole-selective back contact to
CdTe (Figure S4, Supporting Information).
In devices with the buffer layer, no intentional Cu was applied
in the fabrication process. The reference device without the
buffer was Cu-doped with our standard CuCl2 treatment.[34]
The Cu content in the applied buffer layer CuxCryOz was varied
as discussed in the materials and methods section. The statistical
distribution of device parameters for each concentration of Cu
compound in the CuxCryOz precursor is presented in
Figure S5, Supporting Information. Buffer layer prepared with
30% Cu compound in the precursor solution resulted in higher
device performance, and this batch of devices was used for fur-
ther studies to elucidate the effect of thermal processing of the
back-buffer. Figure 1a and S6a, Supporting Information, show
the J–V curves in dark and under illumination of the CdTe/
CdS devices with the CuxCryOz/Au back contact annealed at
200 °C for different time durations. Photovoltaic (PV) parameters
of the devices annealed for different durations in the range of 6–
12 min are given in Table 1. Annealing duration of 8 min
resulted in better devices with an efﬁciency of 14.4%, VOC of
841 mV, JSC of 22.1 mA cm/C02, and FF of 78%. The correspond-
ing parameters of the reference cell are 13.9%, 835 mV,
22.2 mA cm/C02, and 75.0% (Table 1), which are comparable to
our previously reported values.[33,35] In general, after annealing
the buffer, the VOC, JSC, and FF all increased, thereby enhancing
the power conversion efﬁciency (PCE). The J–V curve for
V > VOC shows evidence of rollover and lower FF for devices
annealed for longer durations (>12 min), indicating the possible
formation of a back diode. The change in device parameters, and
particularly in the VOC, could be related to Cu migration from the
buffer layer, which can readily change the minority carrier life-
