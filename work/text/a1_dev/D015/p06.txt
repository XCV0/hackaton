devices discussed in this work were activated using the standard CdCl2 pro-
cess developed in our laboratory. The buffer layer was developed on the acti-
vated devices by spin coating 50 μLofCuxCryOz solution onto the CdTe
surface and later annealed at 200 °C for different time durations as per
the design of experiment (Table ST3). The annealing temperature was
selected based on the experimental data obtained from a series of devices
annealed at different temperatures in the range from 180 to 220 °C. For
monofacial devices, 45 nm of Au electrode was deposited by thermal evap-
oration using a shadow mask, and for bifacial devices the transparent contact
ITO (/C25250 nm) was deposited by RF sputtering at room temperature. In
both cases the active area of the devices was 0.08 cm2.Nointentional
Cu doping was performed on the devices with CuxCryOz buffer; however,
the reference samples, in both mono- and bifacial conﬁgurations, were
treated with 0.1 mM of CuCl2 solution in deionized water and later annealed
in air at 200 °C for 20 min, which was the standard Cu doping procedure in
our laboratory.[34] Current density vs voltage characteristics were measured
using a Keithley 2400 digital source meter under AM1.5G irradiance at
100 mW/cm2. The external quantum efﬁciency (EQE) measurements were
performed with a PV instruments (model IVQE8-C) system. The steady-state
photoluminescence (PL) was measured at room temperature using both 532
and 633 nm continuous-wave lasers while the time-resolved photolumines-
cence (TRPL) was measured with a 532 nm pulsed laser with beam diameter
of /C25150 μm. In TRPL, the incident power was 34.5 and 113 mW/cm2 respec-
tively for front and back illumination, and the measurements were performed
with a time-correlated single-photon counting module with integration time
of 900 ms. Capacitance–voltage (C–V ) measurement was performed using a
Solarton Modulab Potentiostat equipped with a frequency response analyzer
(Ametek, Inc.). The measurements were carried in the dark with an applied
AC modulation voltage of 45 mV at 10 kHz and the DC bias swept from /C03
to 1 V. The composition of the ﬁlm as a function of depth was measured
using glow discharge optical emission spectroscopy (GDOES; Horiba GD
Proﬁler HD). For these measurements, the samples were sputtered using
20 W at a repetition rate of 2.5 kHz and duty cycle of 0.125. The spectra
were acquired at 1 s intervals with 0.1 s integration time. The system
was not calibrated and was displayed in the voltage generated by the
photomultiplier tube.[40]
