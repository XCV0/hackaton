The carrier density of the CdTe device with CuxCryOz back
contact estimated from the C–V analysis is /C253.2 /C2 1013 cm/C03,
which is lower by a factor of /C251.7 compared to the reference
device (/C255.6 /C2 1013 cm/C03) with our standard Cu doping through
CuCl2 treatment (Figure 1b). However, it should be noted that
the carrier proﬁle estimated from C–V data is prone to the inﬂu-
ence of deep levels, back diode, and nonuniform distribution of
dopants, which may lead to an apparent change from actual val-
ues. The carrier density proﬁles show the typical U-shape seen in
CdTe devices, with distinct features at the three regions. The left
branch indicates the presence of a back diode, and the onset at
lower forward bias for device with CuxCryOz buffer is noticeable.
The early onset of the right branch together with an apparent
higher carrier density for the reference device indicates the con-
tribution from deep levels.[37] A clear distinction between the Cu
doping which changes the carrier concentration and the Cu ele-
mental diffusion into the CdTe is evident from a comparison of
Figure 1b,c. The GDOES proﬁle of Cu shows higher Cu incor-
poration in the device with the CuxCryOz buffer (Figure 1c); how-
ever, the net carrier density is higher for the device processed
with CuCl2 treatment (Figure 1b). This indicates that the Cu dop-
ing may not be proportional to the amount of elemental Cu in the
ﬁlm. The observed improvement in device performance in the
presence of the CuxCryOz buffer is related to the surface passiv-
ation and suppression of nonradiative recombination, which is
discussed in the PL section.
