Owing to the effective Cd diffusion, a thinner CdS layer is facilitated,
decreasing parasitic absorption and increasing the short-circuit current
density.11–15 (ii) The alkali treatment leads to a lower valence band max-
imum at the CIGS surface, repelling holes from reaching the buffer/
CIGS junction, thereby reducing interface recombination.16–19 (iii) It
increases the net hole concentration in the CIGS bulk due to substitu-
tion of donor-type (point) defects (InCu) and/or line defect (dislocations)
with alkali metals.20,21 An increased open-circuit voltage (VOC)ofthe
device is achieved due to the change in the net hole concentration
before (N0) and after (NA)thetreatment.20–23 (iv) Heavy alkali metals
passivate the CIGS grain boundaries by the formation of alkali com-
pounds.18,21 As a result, both the minority carrier lifetime and photolu-
minescence intensity increase,20,22,24 validating the material's suitability
for high-performance solar cells.20–25
To date, alkali-halides (e.g, KF, RbF, etc.) are used almost exclu-
sively as alkali sources, generally deposited on the surface of the CIGS
thin film immediately after deposition by vacuum evaporation, known
as the alkali-fluoride (AlkF) post-deposition treatment (PDT).11–26
There are few reports about solution processes to provide alkali
elements, which also improve device performance. The authors
spin-coated KF27 or KI28 after absorber growth followed by annealing
in N2 or Se atmosphere. However, a detailed investigation of the
solution-processed samples at grains and grain boundaries and about
the diffusion of the alkali elements into the absorber layer has yet to
be performed.
The present work demonstrates a low-cost solution process for
incorporating Cs into CIGS thin films using cesium carbonate (CsCO3).
This CsCO3 solution treatment (Cs-ST) modifies the CIGS surface and
changes the surface property from hydrophilic to hydrophobic nature. A
controlled amount of spin-coated CsCO3 improves the solar cell's open-
circuit voltage (VOC), fill factor (FF), and efficiency (ɳ) similar to the well-
established evaporation processes of alkali-halides. X-ray photoelectron
spectroscopy (XPS) measurements confirm the formation of a Cu-
depleted surface layer of the CIGS thin film after the Cs-ST process.
Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurements reveal a
lowering of the valence band, and high-resolution transmission electron
microscopy (HRTEM) measurements confirm the presence of Cs at the
grain boundaries close to the surface of the CIGS thin film but much less
deeper inside the absorber layer. The improvements in the photovoltaic
parameters upon the Cs-ST are similar to those observed with the stan-
dard CsF-PDT process in vacuum.29–31 On the other hand, CsCO3 has
been widely used in the past in the fabrication of various electronic
devices.32–37 It mainly induces n-type doping, alters the energy level of
semiconductors and lowers the work function of metal electrodes.32–39
These effects are typically attributed to the formation of an X-O-Cs
(X = In, Ga, Si, W, C) surface termination.40–42
