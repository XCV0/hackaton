6.1 Anatomy of Dataset Characteristics
We first analyze the intrinsic properties of the three
datasets. As shown in Figure 9 and Figure 10,
the prefill time and cache hit rate exhibit stark dif-
ferences across datasets. Under the CacheBlend
policy alone, RAGPULSE achieves a hit rate of
37.46%, which is substantially higher than Burst-
GPT’s 7.03% and MTRAG’s 10.12%. This dis-
crepancy stems from fundamental differences in
dataset characteristics. BurstGPT is a generic LLM
trace where requests lack structured prompt compo-
nents, limiting prefix based caching due to its strict
exact matching requirement. MTRAG is a syn-
thetic RAG dataset where request arrival patterns
are artificially generated, resulting in low document

6.2 Scheduling in PIC Systems
Recent RAG serving systems such as CacheBlend,
TurboRAG and EPIC (Lu et al., 2024; Hu et al.,
2024) have demonstrated that KV cache can be
reused at the document level independent of its
absolute position in the input sequence, a tech-
nique widely adopted in production RAG deploy-
ments. Building on this foundation, we evaluate
how scheduling strategies perform in this Position
Independent Caching (PIC) enabled serving sys-
tems using the three datasets. And we consider two
scheduling strategies. The first is hit rate aware al-
location which prioritizes caching documents with
the highest observed access frequency. The second
is Global Aware Dispatch (GAD) which consid-
ers both cache hit rate and request level document
overlap between dispatching requests. As illus-
trated in Figure 9 and Figure 10, applying GAD on
top of CacheBlend yields a dramatic improvement
only for RAGPULSE. Specifically, RAGPULSE’s
hit rate increases from 37.46% to 46.73%, while
its prefill time decreases by 13.33%. For Burst-
GPT, the hit rate improves marginally from 7.03%
to 9.03%, with prefill time decreasing from 0.24
to 0.21. For MTRAG, the hit rate increases from
10.12% to 15.45%, with prefill time decreasing
from 0.25 to 0.22. The limited improvement on
BurstGPT and MTRAG reflects their inherent lack
of authentic document overlap. BurstGPT has no
document retrieval mechanism, while MTRAG’s
synthetic nature produces documents that are in-
dependent and lack temporal correlations. Only
