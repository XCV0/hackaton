6.3 Cache Policy Sensitivity Analysis
In RAG serving, requests are queued before pro-
cessing. Can cache eviction policies leverage the
queue’s pending requests to improve performance?
A natural hypothesis is that a cache eviction pol-
icy considering pending requests’ document needs
could outperform temporal policies. To test this,
we evaluate Queue Aware Cache Eviction (QCE),
which uses queue order to prioritize KV pairs likely
reused by upcoming requests.
When combining GAD with QCE, as shown in
Figure 9 and Figure 10, RAGPULSE continues
to show significant gains while the other datasets
plateau. RAGPULSE’s hit rate further increases
from 46.73% to 54.48%, an additional gain of 7.75
percentage points and a 17.02 percentage point
absolute improvement from the CacheBlend base-
line of 37.46%. Its prefill time drops from 0.13 to
0.11, a reduction of 15.38%. For BurstGPT and
MTRAG, the hit rate changes from 9.03% to 8.53%
and from 15.45% to 16.93%, respectively, while
prefill time remains nearly unchanged.
The striking difference stems from RAG’s re-
quest queue dynamics combined with authentic
temporal locality. Unlike the native LRU of
CacheBlend, QCE leverages queue level demand
to retain document KV pairs that are contextually
relevant to requests waiting in the queue. In pro-
duction RAG deployments, requests arriving within
short time windows often share overlapping docu-
ment needs, creating queue level reuse hotspots that
persist within specific temporal windows. RAG-
PULSE captures this phenomenon through its au-
thentic temporal sequence data, where requests
with similar document needs naturally cluster in
time. BurstGPT lacks any document overlap,
while MTRAG’s synthetic documents are inde-
pendent and lack temporal clustering, rendering
QCE ineffective. The meaningful improvement
only on RAGPULSE confirms that authentic tem-
poral queue dynamics is crucial for RAG cache
policy design.
