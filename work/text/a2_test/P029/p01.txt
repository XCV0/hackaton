RAGPULSE: A Real-World RAG Workload Capturing Authentic Temporal
Reuse Patterns for Efficient Serving
Zhengchao Wang and Yitao Hu and Zhuxuan Chang and Jianing Ye
Guotao Yang and Tianhao Lai and Jiazheng Yu and Zhixin Zhao
Liang Zheng and Youpeng Deng and Sheng Chen and Keqiu Li
Tianjin University, China
Abstract
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has
emerged as a critical paradigm for deploying re-
liable and knowledge-intensive large language
model (LLM) applications. Its unique execu-
tion pipeline introduces large overhead in sys-
tem retrieval, such as massive context loading
and document dependencies. To reduce this
overhead, existing work on RAG serving sys-
tems often relies on generic LLM inference
traces or synthetic RAG datasets. However,
these approaches fail to capture authentic tem-
poral reuse patterns of RAG workloads, result-
ing in a disconnect between theoretical opti-
mization and real-world deployment efficiency.
To bridge this gap, this paper introduces
RAGPULSE, an open-source RAG workload
trace dataset that preserves authentic tem-
poral information missing in existing traces.
Collected from a production-grade university
Q&A system serving over 40,000 students
and faculty members since April 2024, the
dataset comprises 134K requests over 389
days. It unveils key RAG dynamics includ-
ing time-sensitive document reuse, skewed
access patterns over long time scales, and
bursty temporal locality. By capturing these
temporal reuse patterns, RAGPULSE empow-
ers the systems community to develop and
validate next-generation optimization tech-
niques, offering practical guidance for effi-
cient RAG serving. The trace is available at
https://github.com/flashserve/RAGPulse.

Although RAG significantly improves answer
accuracy, its system-level performance (Jin et al.,
2025; Shang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025d) re-
mains hindered by the lack of representative traces.
Both generic LLM (Wang et al., 2025c; shareAI,
2023; Wu et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025; Sreeram
et al., 2025) and synthetic RAG (Katsis et al., 2025;
Qiu et al., 2024; Zhong et al., 2021) datasets miss
RAG-specific dynamics. Most synthetic datasets
are generated via random independent sampling or
fixed-interval arrivals, destroying the bursty, time-
varying structure of real user behavior. Thus, three
temporal dimensions are missing: bursty arrival
patterns, document popularity drift, and multi-turn
session continuity. These are central to modern
serving optimizations like Position Independent
Caching (PIC) (Yao et al., 2025a; Hu et al., 2024),
prefix-aware attention (Yi et al., 2026), content-
aware scheduling (Qin et al., 2025), and KV cache
management (Touzeau et al., 2019; Shah and Sid-
diqui, 2023). Consequently, systems designed with
generic traces show a substantial performance gap
in production RAG workloads.

formation missing in existing traces. Through a
