Interpretation of context uncertainty. Context
uncertainty should not be interpreted as a direct
measure of disease uncertainty. It measures in-
stability in the retrieved keypoint distribution. A
high context-uncertainty score indicates that mul-
tiple keypoints are similarly plausible for the ques-
tion, which may reﬂect broad clinical scope, miss-
ing decisive evidence, or ambiguous wording. A
low context-uncertainty score indicates that the
ranking is concentrated on a small number of key-
points. Fusing this score with answer uncertainty
makes the ﬁnal uncertainty sensitive to both re-
trieval ambiguity and answer-generation ambigu-
ity.

Case analysis. The vanilla model chooses
contrast-enhanced CT despite the guideline-
speciﬁc preference for noncontrast CT in this
surgical-planning context. Fixed-5 RAG corrects
the answer, but its uncertainty remains high be-
cause the retrieved keypoints include several broad
TED assessment and referral statements that are
only weakly related to the imaging modality. UR-
Gen retrieves a smaller set of high-ranking key-
points and assigns a lower uncertainty score to the
same correct answer. This example illustrates an
important distinction: retrieving more keypoints
can increase topical coverage, but it can also in-
troduce redundant or weakly relevant context that
makes the model less certain. URGen’s adaptive
depth selection helps balance evidence sufﬁciency
and context compactness.
Qualitative takeaway. The case study supports
the quantitative trend in the main paper. When the
keypoint distribution is concentrated, URGen con-
tracts the context and avoids unnecessary guide-
line material. When the keypoint distribution is
diffuse, the method can expand the context to re-
duce the risk of missing key clinical information.
This behavior is especially important for guideline-
grounded medical QA, where several keypoints
may be semantically close but only one or two are
clinically decisive for the ﬁnal option.
G Prompt Templates
URGen is implemented in a training-free manner
using two prompt templates. The ﬁrst prompt
ranks candidate guideline keypoints for retrieval,
and the second prompt answers the multiple-
choice question using only the selected keypoint-
passage blocks. The prompts enforce structured
JSON output to make retrieval ranking and answer
extraction reproducible.
