D.1 Dense Retriever Baselines
Dense retrievers map the question and candidate
keypoints into an embedding space and select the
nearest keypoint. This setting is efﬁcient and sim-
ple, but it does not provide an explicit estimate
of retrieval ambiguity. In our setting, dense re-
trieval also faces a mismatch: the keypoints are
short clinical recommendations, while many ques-
tions require distinguishing between several se-
mantically related guideline statements. Table S11
shows that BioMedCLIP improves over generic
CLIP, but both remain below URGen.

The dense retriever results indicate that sim-
ply replacing the retrieval backbone is not enough.
BioMedCLIP is better aligned with biomedical ter-
minology than CLIP, but it still retrieves a ﬁxed
number of keypoints and cannot express whether
the relevance distribution is sharp or diffuse. UR-
Gen uses the ranking distribution itself as a signal,
allowing it to select fewer keypoints when one or
two guideline statements are decisive, and more
keypoints when the question requires broader clin-
ical context.
