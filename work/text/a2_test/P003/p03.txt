3.1 Framework Overview
As shown in Figure 3, we introduce Uncertainty-
guided Retrieval-augmented Generation (URGen),
a training-free framework that harnesses an off-
the-shelf frozen large language model (LLM) for
guideline-grounded medical multiple-choice QA.
We do not pursue the best possible backbone, but
instead focus on mitigating hallucinations and im-
proving reliability in specialized domains. UR-
Gen consists of four stages: multi-temperature
knowledge retrieval, zero-shot adaptive retrieval
depth (ZARD), multi-answer aggregation, and un-
certainty estimation.

3.2 Multi-Temperature Knowledge Retrieval
First, we parse clinical guidelines and consen-
sus documents into a structured collection of
keypoint–passage pairs C = {(ki; ti)}. As de-
tailed in Appendix A (Table S2), each pair com-
prises a guideline statement and its supporting sec-
tion, extracted deterministically from the public
XML structure. Ophthalmology experts authored
and veriﬁed the QA items and labels. We do not
evaluate learned or noisy automatic extraction in
this study. Here, ki is a concise statement captur-
ing the main claim or ﬁnding of a passage, and
ti is the full text content of that passage. Due to
the lengthy full content, we perform retrieval using
the question q and keypoint collection K = {ki}
instead of the full content T = {ti} as query.
Given a question q, the keypoint collection K and
a prompt tailored for ranking keypoints, we query
a frozen LLM M1 times with different tempera-
tures {τ1, . . . , τM1 } to generate M1 ranking lists
{l1, ..., lM1 } of keypoints in descending order of
relevance. This multi-temperature retrieval pro-
vides multiple “views” of relevance that later sup-
port adaptive depth selection. The detailed ranking
prompt is provided in Appendix G.
