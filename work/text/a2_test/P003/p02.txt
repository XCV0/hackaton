Vanilla (Non-RAG): The query is directly input into

Yet ﬁxed-context RAG is brittle in specialized do-
mains: large k can introduce irrelevant or even
abnormal passages that dilute key evidence, while
small k can omit critical guidance (Figure 2) (Izac-
ard and Grave, 2021; Shi et al., 2024). Meanwhile,
common uncertainty estimation methods, e.g., pre-
dictive entropy, dropout, and temperature scaling,
mainly operate at the token level and frequently
miss semantic errors or unsafe advice (Lakshmi-
narayanan et al., 2017; Gal and Ghahramani, 2016;
Guo et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2024a; Kruse et al.,
2025; Shelmanov et al., 2025; Feng et al., 2025).
We propose Uncertainty-guided Retrieval-
augmented Generation (URGen), a training-free
framework in which context uncertainty connects
evidence selection and reliability estimation. UR-
Gen (1) performs multi-temperature knowledge re-
trieval, (2) applies Zero-shot Adaptive Retrieval
Depth (ZARD) to adjust context size from the en-
tropy of aggregated relevance scores, (3) generates
multiple candidate answers under diverse temper-
atures and aggregates their option labels by major-
ity vote, and (4) fuses context and answer uncer-
tainty. Thus, uncertainty estimation and adaptive
retrieval form one pipeline: the ranking distribu-
tion determines the evidence budget and also con-
tributes to the reported uncertainty. Our contribu-
tions are:
• We propose URGen, a training-free,
uncertainty-guided retrieval-augmented genera-
tion framework that incorporates entropy-based
adaptive retrieval depth (ZARD) and fused
uncertainty calibration, tailored for guideline-
grounded medical multiple-choice QA.
• On the ﬁve-seed TED evaluation, URGen signif-
icantly reduces overconﬁdent errors against all
ﬁxed depths while maintaining accuracy compa-
rable to Fixed-1; a broader comparison covers
three backbones on TED and GD.
2 Related Work
Retrieval-augmented generation and adaptive
retrieval. Retrieval-augmented QA commonly
combines a retriever with a generator or reader,
where dense retrievers improve evidence re-
call compared to sparse baselines (Karpukhin
et al., 2020). Generative readers such as
Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD) condition on multiple
retrieved passages and fuse evidence in the de-
coder, enabling strong performance on knowledge-
intensive QA (Izacard and Grave, 2021). Sub-
sequent work improves robustness by inserting
reranking between retrieval and generation (Mao
et al., 2021; Glass et al., 2022) and by support-
ing black-box or medical RAG pipelines via plug-
and-play retrieval interfaces and evidence-aware
retrieval (Shi et al., 2024; Liang et al., 2025; Sohn
et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024; Hwang et al., 2025;
Choi et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025). A key practi-
cal issue is that ﬁxed retrieval depth can be brit-
tle: small depth misses necessary evidence, while
large depth introduces distractors. Prior work ad-
dresses this via learned or iterative information-
seeking policies that dynamically choose retrieval
actions or stop early (Zhu et al., 2021; Jiang et al.,
2023b). Our work follows this line but targets a
training-free, uncertainty-guided depth adaptation
for guideline-grounded medical multiple-choice
QA.
