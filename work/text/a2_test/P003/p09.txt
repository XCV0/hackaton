Limitations
URGen assumes that a ﬂatter keypoint-ranking dis-
tribution warrants more context. It may fail when
the model is consistently conﬁdent about wrong or
incomplete keypoints: ZARD can then assign low
entropy, shrink the context, and miss necessary ev-
idence. Appendix Table S10 quantiﬁes this failure
mode: 2 of 26 errors occur in the lowest uncer-
tainty quartile and 5 of 51 in the lower half. These
counts do not directly establish ranking correct-
ness because gold keypoint labels are unavailable.
URGen also cannot recover evidence absent from
its corpus. Our evaluation uses complete disease-
scoped galleries of 35 TED or 22 GD keypoints;
the pooled-gallery test in Appendix Table S8 adds
disease-speciﬁc distractors but does not establish
robustness for web-scale retrieval or automatically
extracted, noisy corpora.
Computationally, URGen’s multi-temperature
ranking and answer aggregation increase total in-
ference work. Appendix Table S3 reports 4.33
GPU-s/question, above Fixed-1 (3.78) but below
Fixed-5 (5.48); batching independent branches
does not remove this total-compute cost. Finally,
the present evidence is limited to multiple-choice
ophthalmology QA with authoritative guidelines.
Applying URGen to other specialties requires a
new veriﬁed corpus, and its behavior for open-
ended tasks remains untested.
