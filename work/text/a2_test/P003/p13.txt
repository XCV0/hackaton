URGen: Uncertainty-guided Retrieval-augmented Generation
for Guideline-Grounded Medical Question Answering
Supplementary Materials
A Dataset and Keypoint Corpus
Construction
Dataset overview. We construct two expert-
curated ophthalmology multiple-choice QA
benchmarks from long-form clinical guidelines
and consensus documents: Thyroid Eye Disease
(TED) and Graves’ Disease (GD). The TED
benchmark is based on the Management of Thy-
roid Eye Disease by the American and European
Thyroid Associations1 (Burch et al., 2022), while
the GD benchmark is based on the 2018 European
Thyroid Association Guideline for the Manage-
ment of Graves’ Hyperthyroidism2 (Kahaly et al.,
2018). These documents are substantially longer
and more specialized than the passages typically
used in general-domain reading comprehension.
They contain diagnostic criteria, activity and
severity deﬁnitions, treatment recommendations,
contraindications, referral criteria, and follow-up
guidance, all of which are important for clinically
grounded medical QA.
The resulting datasets are designed to support
several evaluation goals. First, they evaluate
whether a model can answer specialized medical
questions that require guideline-level knowledge
rather than broad medical commonsense. Second,
because every question is paired with a curated
keypoint corpus, the datasets allow controlled eval-
uation of retrieval quality, adaptive retrieval depth,
and evidence sufﬁciency. Third, they support relia-
bility evaluation through uncertainty metrics such
as expected calibration error (ECE) and overcon-
ﬁdent error rate (OC@0.8). Finally, they can be
used to study whether a model can avoid noisy
or redundant context when multiple clinically re-
lated but answer-irrelevant guideline statements
are available.
What the datasets contain? Each benchmark
item contains a medical question, multiple answer
options, a gold option label, and the corresponding
topic-speciﬁc keypoint corpus. The questions are
written to test clinically meaningful distinctions,

Keypoint corpus construction. The keypoint
corpus is not produced by an additional learned
model. It follows the public XML structure of
each guideline or consensus document: a guide-
line statement serves as the keypoint, and its sup-
porting section serves as the passage. This deter-
ministic conversion does not use later manual pair-
ing. In URGen, keypoints are used for ranking be-
cause they are compact and discriminative, while
the associated passages are provided to the LLM
only after adaptive depth selection. Table S2 sum-
marizes the construction protocol.
Public release. We plan to publicly release
the benchmark content, including the question
ﬁles, answer labels, keypoint identiﬁers, keypoint
texts, supporting passages, and evaluation scripts.
Since the data are derived from public guidelines
and expert-curated questions rather than patient
records, the benchmark does not contain protected
health information. The release is intended to
make the retrieval and calibration evaluation repro-
ducible, and to facilitate future work on guideline-
grounded medical QA.
