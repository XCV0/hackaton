5 Ablation Studies
Component removal. Table 2 isolates the main
components of URGen. Removing fused calibra-
tion leaves accuracy unchanged at 80.40%, while
OC@0.8 rises from 1.98% to 6.93% and ECE
from 6.80% to 12.31%. Accuracy is unchanged be-
cause this ablation preserves retrieval and answer
voting and changes only the reported uncertainty.
Replacing adaptive retrieval with Fixed-5 reduces
accuracy to 69.70% and increases OC@0.8 to
13.66%. Removing either retrieval- or generation-
temperature diversity produces the largest accu-
racy losses. Thus, adaptive depth and temperature
diversity primarily support correctness, whereas
fusing context and answer uncertainty improves re-
liability. The ﬁxed-depth comparison is reported
in Table 3, and temperature-count sensitivity ap-
pears in Appendix C.
Retrieval depth. Table 3 reports the ﬁve-seed
ﬁxed-depth comparison. URGen has the highest
mean accuracy and lowest OC@0.8 among the
evaluated depths. In 10,000 paired question-level
bootstrap samples, its accuracy difference is sig-
niﬁcant against Fixed-2 (p = 0.0318) and Fixed-
5 (p = 0.0052), but not Fixed-1 (p = 0.0630).
OC@0.8 is signiﬁcantly lower in all three compar-
isons (p ≤ 0.0016), whereas the ECE difference
is signiﬁcant only against Fixed-5 (p = 0.0110).

Case Study. We present one TED QA case in Ta-
ble 4 to illustrate adaptive evidence selection and
the potential effect of redundant context. For a
question about the most comprehensive TED as-
sessment system, vanilla prompting and Fixed-5
RAG produce incorrect answers. URGen retains
four passages centered on activity, severity, and
assessment, and correctly identiﬁes VISA. This ex-
ample illustrates how a compact evidence set can
preserve the decisive guideline information, but
it is qualitative and does not by itself establish a
causal effect of retrieval depth.
