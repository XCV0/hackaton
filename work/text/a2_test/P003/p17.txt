E Uncertainty Analysis
Relation to semantic graph uncertainty. Free-
form uncertainty metrics such as Deg, Ecc, EigV,
and word-sequence entropy (Lin et al., 2024) esti-
mate uncertainty from the lexical or semantic di-
versity of multiple generated answers. They are
most informative when generations are long and
can form multiple semantic clusters. Our task is
a constrained multiple-choice setting: each gener-
ation contains a discrete option choice and a short
rationale. Under this format, Jaccard- or NLI-
based similarity graphs are dominated by whether
two outputs select the same option, so these graph-
based metrics largely reduce to functions of the op-
tion vote distribution.
Why answer entropy alone is insufﬁcient?
The main-paper ablation already isolates this ef-
fect: replacing fused uncertainty with answer en-
tropy only leaves the ﬁnal predictions unchanged
but worsens OC@0.8 from 1.98% to 6.93% and
ECE from 6.80% to 12.31%. This shows that
disagreement among generated options is not suf-
ﬁcient for reliable calibration; context ambiguity
must also be modeled. In clinical QA, a model
can repeatedly emit the same answer while re-
lying on an incomplete or mismatched keypoint
set. URGen therefore combines answer uncer-
tainty with context uncertainty derived from the
keypoint ranking distribution.
