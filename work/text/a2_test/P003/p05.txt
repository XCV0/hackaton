4 Experiment
4.1 Datasets
We evaluate URGen on two expert-curated oph-
thalmology multiple-choice datasets, i.e., Thy-
roid Eye Disease (TED) and Graves’ Disease
(GD). Unlike broad medical QA benchmarks such
as MedQA and MedMCQA, which mainly as-
sess parametric medical knowledge across general
medical topics, our datasets pair expert-authored
questions and labels with authoritative guideline
passages. We focus on thyroid-related ophthal-
mology because its guidelines contain clinically
adjacent recommendations whose distinctions de-
pend on disease activity, severity, treatment stage,
and imaging or surgical context. This provides
a controlled test of evidence selection, but it
does not establish cross-specialty generality. The
keypoint–passage pairs are extracted determin-
istically from the public guideline XML struc-
ture. Ophthalmology experts participated in ques-
tion design and answer veriﬁcation. We will re-
lease the corpus to support reproducibility and fu-
ture research on guideline-grounded medical RAG.
Speciﬁcally, TED contains 101 questions and a
complete disease-scoped gallery of 35 keypoint–
passage pairs from Management of Thyroid Eye
Disease by the American and European Thyroid
Associations (Burch et al., 2022); GD contains 97
questions and 22 pairs from the 2018 European
Thyroid Association Guideline for the Manage-
ment of Graves’ Hyperthyroidism (Kahaly et al.,
2018). These are controlled guideline galleries,
not web-scale retrieval corpora.
4.2 Compared Methods
We compare our method with three widely-used
strategies, i.e., Vanilla, Fixed-1 RAG, and Fixed-5
RAG based on prevailing LLMs:
Vanilla. The model receives only the question
q with the instruction “Select the best answer for
the question.” No external context or uncertainty
estimation is applied. This conﬁguration measures
the LLM’s zero-shot recall of medical knowledge.
Fixed-k RAG. First, we retrieve the top-
k keypoint–passage pairs from C using multi-
temperature knowledge retrieval and concatenate
them with q in descending relevance order. Sec-
ond, we sample the LLM under multiple answer-
generation temperatures. Context size is ﬁxed at k,
and no context-uncertainty calibration is applied.
