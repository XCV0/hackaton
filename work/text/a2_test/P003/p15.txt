Analyses
Paired bootstrap comparisons. Table S5 gives
the complete 10,000-sample paired question-level
bootstrap results corresponding to the ﬁve-seed
comparison in Table 3. The accuracy interval
includes zero for Fixed-1 but excludes zero for
Fixed-2 and Fixed-5. OC@0.8 differences exclude
zero for every ﬁxed depth, whereas the ECE inter-
val excludes zero only for Fixed-5.
Temperature-count sensitivity. Table S6
jointly varies the numbers of retrieval and answer
temperatures. Five temperatures give the highest
mean accuracy and lowest OC@0.8; seven tem-
peratures give the lowest ECE but require two
additional samples at each stage. This comparison
motivates the ﬁve-temperature setting without
claiming that one setting dominates every metric.
Depth mapping. For the linear alternative, we
set h = ucontext/ log K and Dlinear = clip[1,5](⌊1 +
4h⌋). Table S7 shows that the exponential
mapping has higher mean accuracy and lower
OC@0.8, with similar ECE under the evaluated
protocol.
Pooled candidate gallery. Table S8 evaluates
TED questions after adding the 22 GD keypoints
to the 35-keypoint TED gallery. All three meth-
ods decline, but URGen remains strongest in the
57-keypoint pool: 4.55 percentage points above
Fixed-1 and 8.12 above Fixed-5. This is a con-
trolled disease-speciﬁc distractor test, not a web-
scale retrieval experiment.
Normalized uncertainty ranges. Only the nor-
malized entropies in Eq. 9 enter the fusion rule.
Table S9 records their observed ranges and con-
ﬁrms that the fusion inputs stay within [0, 1] on
both datasets.
Low-context-uncertainty errors. Table S10
counts wrong answers among questions with the
lowest context uncertainty. The nonzero counts ex-
pose the anticipated failure mode: a concentrated
ranking can still be wrong. Because the bench-
mark has no gold keypoint labels, these counts
characterize answer errors conditional on context
uncertainty rather than directly validating the rank-
ing itself.
