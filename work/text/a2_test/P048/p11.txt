D. Parameter Sensitivity Analysis
In this subsection, we analyse the parameter sensitivity
of AGRAG’s two unique hyperparameters: The maximum n-
gram b and the entity extraction threshold τ. As all other
hyperparameters of AGRAG also exist in other baselines, we
just align them with other baselines for fair comparison. We
carry out the analysis on GraphRAG-bench [34], which cover
most of the tasks of our experiment.
1) The maximum n-gram b: As introduced in Section III-B,
the maximum n-gram b is used as the entity boundary to
control the maximum allowed word length of the extracted
entities. Here, we conduct a test about the maximum n-gram
parameter b ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7}, as entity distribution in English
is, in general, less than 7 words according to [87]. From the
experiment results in Figure 3, AGRAG achieves the overall
best performance when the maximum n-gram b is set to 3,
this is because most of the entities usually consist of no more
than 3 words, according to the discovery of [87]–[89].
For Creative Generation task, AGRAG can achieve better
performance with larger maximum n-gram, because the correct
answer of this task is more flexible than other tasks that
have fixed answers or require answers to cover certain facts.
Therefore, the negative impact of noise introduced by high n-
gram phrases is overwhelmed by the additional context they
provide, leading to better performance. This also highlights
AGRAG’s adaptability to different tasks through its flexible
graph construction. In our experiments, we set the maximum

2) The entity extraction threshold τ: As introduced in
Section III-B, in the extraction procedure, we extract words
or phrases that have the entity extraction score ER(·) > τ
as an entity, and we have 0 < ER(·) < 1. Considering the
computational cost, we only test the entity extraction threshold
τ ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7}. According to the experiment results
in Figure 3, AGRAG typically achieves its best performance
when set to 0.3 or 0.5. This is because a large threshold tends
to extract less informative entities, and a small one tends to
cause insufficient entity extraction; both can affect the model’s
effectiveness. In our experiments, we set the entity extraction
threshold τ to 0.5 for all tasks.
V. CONCLUSION
This paper presents AGRAG, an Advanced Graph-based
Retrieval Augmented Generation framework addressing the
Inaccurate Graph Construction, Poor Reasoning Ability, and
Inadequate Answer issues of current RAG frameworks. By
providing explicit reasoning paths to help LLM inference,
AGRAG makes the LLM better focus on query-related
contexts within the retrieved context. Experiments on real-
world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of
AGRAG over state-of-the-art RAG models.
