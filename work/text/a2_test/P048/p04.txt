Step 2: Graph Retrieval. In this step, we firstly map
the query q to KG fact triples considering their semantic
similarities, after that, we calculate the PPR score [33] based
on these mapped fact triples F as the node influence score
s. On the other hand, we calculate the edge costs CE consid-
ering the semantic similarity between query and facts’ vector
representations. the higher their similarity is, the lower the
cost is. Compared with solely applying PPR to score nodes
for retrieval, the edge cost further provides constraints on the
involved nodes based on the semantic correspondence between
query and facts, removing the need for the human-defined
PPR retrieval threshold. After that, we initialize the MCMI
subgraph with the generated MCST, and apply our greedy
algorithm by iteratively expanding the MCMI subgraph by
selecting its neighboring node with the lowest edge cost-
influence score ratio (i.e., c/s). If the lowest ratio is larger
than the total ratio of the current MCMI, the iteration stops and
the current MCMI subgraph is returned. We prove the MCMI
generation problem to be NP-hard, and our greedy algorithm
in Algorithm 3 can achieve a 2-approximation. Please refer to
Section III-C for further details.
Step 3: Hybrid Retrieval. In this step, we perform hybrid
retrieval and obtain text chunks based on their BM25 sparse
similarity score, and dense vector representation similarity
score with the query. These chunks will be input together with
the MCMI subgraph to the LLM for the final query answering,
providing extra contexts.
B. Step 1: Data Preparation
In this subsection, we utilize a TFIDF [36] based entity
extraction approach to substitute the LLM-based approach of
other Graph-based RAG models, reduce LLM hallucination,
resulting in less noise generated from the early entity extrac-
tion step, and hence less error propagation, address the inac-
curate graph construction issue, leading to better effectiveness.
The pseudo code of AGRAG’s graph construction procedure
is shown in Algorithm 1.
To begin with, we split texts in T into fixed-length chunks
with a certain amount of token overlap between chunks:
