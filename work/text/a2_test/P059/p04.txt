Cluster knockout. Targeted deletion removes
one retrieved item or semantic evidence unit S
while leaving the rest of the context intact. In
redundant corpora, single-document deletion can
miss dependence because equivalent passages re-
main. We therefore cluster Rc(q) into evidence
units C(q) = {C1, . . . , Cm} via complete-linkage
hierarchical agglomerative clustering (HAC) and

Counterfactual additions. Each counterfactual
addition appends one synthesized document to
the retrieved context, leaving the existing docu-
ments unchanged. Distractor injection adds an
irrelevant chunk drawn from another query in the
same dataset and reports noise robustness NR =
E[Sim(a0, a+n)]. Update injection and contradic-
tion injection each append a 2–3 sentence passage
that asserts a synthesized counterfactual answer of
the same factual type as the reference: update in-
jection frames the counterfactual as superseding
new information (“Recent sources have established
that . . . ”), while contradiction injection frames it
as a competing claim (“one source claims u, not
the reference”). The two probes test complemen-
tary clauses of the same retrieved-state perturba-
tion: Upd = ⊮[UpdateFollow(au; u)] measures
adoption of the appended fact, while Contra =
⊮[ConflictAck(acon)] measures explicit acknowl-
edgment of the conflict; we also report contradic-
tion stability CS = Sim(a0, acon). The probes
are related to the entity-substitution and counter-
memory protocols of Longpre et al. (2021) and Xie
et al. (2024), but differ in operating by addition
rather than in-place substitution: the original re-
trieved context is preserved, and the counterfactual
enters alongside it.
