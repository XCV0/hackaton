Abstract
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is usu-
ally evaluated from one observed trace: the
query, the retrieved context, and the generated
answer. Such evaluations can tell us whether
the answer is consistent with the retrieved con-
text, but not whether the system would still
give the same answer if that context were re-
moved, updated, or contradicted. We intro-
duce COUNTERMEM, an evaluation protocol
for RAG systems that are expected to answer
from retrieved evidence: it keeps the RAG
configuration fixed, changes only the retrieved
context, regenerates the answer, and records
whether the answer changes in the expected di-
rection. Across RAGBench, ExpertQA, and
DelucionQA, COUNTERMEM complements
observed-trace faithfulness by exposing fail-
ures it is not designed to see. Configurations
that look equivalent under RAGAS faithfulness
behave very differently once their evidence is
removed, updated, or contradicted, and among
RAGChecker-approved answers more than half
silently miss coherent contradictions and over
a third ignore coherent updates. The failures
persist across generator and judge families, the
behavioral detectors agree with blind human an-
notators as closely as the annotators agree with
each other, and row-level intervention traces
localize failures to retrieval, reranking, or gen-
eration. Artifact (protocol, prompts, detectors,
scripts, audit traces): https://github.com/
DeltaLabTLV/CounterMem.
1 Introduction
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) combines
a parametric language model with non-parametric
memory retrieved at inference time (Lewis et al.,
2020; Gao et al., 2023b). Most RAG evaluations
ask questions about the observed run: was relevant
evidence retrieved, was the answer correct, and
was the answer supported by the retrieved context?
These are important questions, but they miss a dif-
ferent one: would the answer have changed if the
retrieved evidence had changed?
This distinction matters for evidence-bound
RAG systems—deployments that promise to (1)
answer only when retrieved evidence is sufficient,
(2) abstain when it is removed, (3) follow re-
trieved updates, (4) acknowledge unresolved con-
flicts, and (5) remain stable under irrelevant dis-
tractors. The corresponding behavioral failures
that fixed-context scoring can hide are no-evidence
persistence, update inertia, conflict ignorance, and
noise sensitivity, each a violation of one or more
of clauses (1)–(5); semantic redundancy is a mea-
surement complication that can mask dependence
under naive single-document deletion.
Observed-trace metrics are useful. Reference-
free judges such as RAGAS (Es et al., 2024) and
ARES (Saad-Falcon et al., 2024), and component
diagnostics such as RAGChecker (Ru et al., 2024),
score the realized (q, c, a) trace and can check
whether the answer is compatible with the context
that was actually retrieved. But two RAG systems
can produce the same answer to the same query and
retrieved context—one by using the passage, the
other by ignoring it and answering from paramet-
ric memory—and receive identical observational
scores; only an intervention on the retrieved state
can tell them apart.
We introduce COUNTERMEM, a counterfactual
evaluation protocol for RAG systems that are ex-
pected to answer from retrieved evidence. The pro-
tocol generalizes binary-mask context ablation to a
typology of retrieved-state edits (deletions, counter-
factual additions) applied at three explicit pipeline
points: post-retrieval, corpus-addition, and corpus-
deletion. Figure 1 gives the protocol overview.
