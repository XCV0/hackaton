The module is effective for three reasons.
Community-bridge intuition. The Girvan–
Newman algorithm (Girvan and Newman,
2002) detects communities by removing high-
betweenness edges. We invert this insight: edges
with high fe = |{(i, j) : e ∈ Pij }| serve as bridges
between semantic communities; prioritizing them
merges clusters of related evidence.
Filtering irrelevant chunks. An edge (i, j) /∈
E(0) whenever dij > αµ, preventing merging of
unrelated segments.
Avoiding redundant merges. Because Eu-
clidean distance satisfies the triangle inequality
dij ≤ dik + dkj , intra-community edges have low
betweenness and are deprioritized, selecting only
true inter-community bridges.
