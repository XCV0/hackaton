Table 22: Detailed components of safety-planning card SPC_001 for exploring suicidal thoughts and triggers.
Field Content
ID SPC_001
Title Exploration of Suicidal Thoughts and Triggers
Skill Type cognitive_restructuring
Risk Level elevated
Tone validating
Language en
Intent Fit validate, explore, reframe
Triggers – feelings of hopelessness and helplessness
– thoughts of self-harm and suicide
– uncertainty about the seriousness of thoughts
Goals – validate the patient’s distress
– explore potential triggers and obstacles
– ensure patient safety and well-being
Steps 1. Validate the patient’s feelings of distress and confusion.
2. Reassure the patient that suicidal thoughts do not always indicate imminent risk.
3. Explore external and internal factors contributing to distress.
4. Encourage identification of triggers and patterns in thoughts.
Say Examples – "I can only imagine how distressing it is for you to be dealing with these thoughts."
– "It’s crucial to remember that having suicidal thoughts doesn’t necessarily mean that you’re at imminent
risk."
– "I’d like to explore any potential obstacles that might make it more challenging for you."
Don’ts – Do not invalidate the emotion or the seriousness of the thoughts.
– Do not rush into solutions without exploring the patient’s context.
Evidence Notes Source: CBT Cognitive Restructuring
Tags suicidal thoughts, emotional validation, cognitive exploration, safety assessment, therapeutic engagement
