A.8 Safety Cards for High-risk Cases
We observed the contribution of safety cards in
high-risk cases (Figure 4). Instead of direct evaluat-
ing the role of the cards in high-risk scenarios, we
evaluated this aspect based on the results of answer
generation. For human, we asked again the three
students to rate the output of high-risk samples (Fig-
ure 4) using six metrics (a Likert scale). Suicidal
Ideation (SI) evaluates whether responses mitigate
suicide risk and provide supportive resources (Pos-
ner et al., 2011). Self-Harm (SH) assesses safe,
non-triggering responses to non-suicidal self-injury
without describing methods (Nock, 2010). Anxiety
Crisis (AC) measures the provision of grounding
and calming guidance during acute distress (Edi-
tion et al., 2013). Violent Thoughts (VT) evalu-
ates de-escalation and safety when users express
intent to harm others (Miner et al., 2016). Sub-
stance Abuse (SA) assesses non-judgmental, clin-
ically safe guidance for drug or alcohol misuse
(Miller and Rollnick, 2012). Risk-taking Behav-
iors (RTB) examines whether safer, constructive
alternatives are offered for impulsive or dangerous
actions (Linehan, 1993). The Fleiss’ Kappa on
