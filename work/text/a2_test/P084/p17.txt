The results suggest that standard semantic match-
ing remains an important component for effective
retrieval. Configurations that assign a predomi-
nant weight to the semantic component (α) tend
to maintain robust recall rates. This implies that
foundational lexical and semantic overlap is neces-
sary to establish baseline relevance between user
queries and relevant documents (Lewis et al., 2020).
When the retrieval relies on cognitive information,
either appraisal (β) or CBT (γ), IR performance
reduces. This indicates that while latent psycholog-
ical constructs are valuable for contextualizing a
query, they should be used as supplementary infor-
mation of semantic relevance. This points to a com-
plementary structure: semantic similarity provides
a necessary topical backbone, while appraisal and
CBT signals refine ranking by anchoring relevance
to latent cognitive states. The best configuration
([0.5; 0.3; 0.2]) shows that cognitive modeling is
most effective when combined with, rather than
substituted for, semantic retrieval.
A.5 Qualitative Error Analysis in Crisis
Scenarios
To better understand the models’ behavior in high-
risk situations, we conducted a qualitative error
analysis on 72 “HIGH” crisis cases filtered from
MentalChat (30) and CounselChat (42). We man-
ually categorized primary failures into six clini-
cal types: Mild Shortcomings (unnatural phras-
ing), Over-reassurance (unrealistic promises), In-
adequate Escalation (failing to recognize urgency),
Invalidating Language (dismissing pain), Failure
to Encourage Support (not suggesting professional
help), and Unsafe Specificity (suggesting harm-

configuration reduces the total number of flagged
errors from 47 to 24 compared to the Baseline.

to 11 cases with reduced severity, while Over-
reassurance and Failure to Encourage Support also
see consistent reductions. Neither configuration
generated text containing Unsafe Specificity. These
observations suggest that safety-planning cards as-
sist in mitigating context-specific risky responses
that average safety scores might obscure.

A.6 Card Quality Assessment Metrics
The experts rated each sample using four metrics
(Es et al., 2024). Faithfulness answers questions:
Does the card accurately reflect the actual strat-
egy and tone used in the Therapist Output? Did it
invent things not present? Actionability answers
questions: Are the ’steps’ and ’donts’ specific, ac-
tionable, and clear for a generative AI agent to
follow? Generalizability answers questions: Are
the ’triggers’ slightly generalized to catch similar
cases, or are they overly overfitted to the exact in-
put text? Safety answers questions: Are the ’donts’
effective guardrails? Is the ’risk level’ appropriate
for the patient input? Faithfulness and Actionabil-
ity use Likert scale rating; and Generalizability and
Safety use True/False rating.
