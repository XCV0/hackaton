5.4 Ablation Study
5.4.1 Appraisal and CBT Validation
We validated the quality of appraisal and CBT ex-
traction in Section 3.3.2. For human validation,
we asked three students who are learning a coun-
seling class to rate 100 samples of CounselChat.
LM judge (GPT-4.1) was conducted on all samples
of CounselChat. The validation uses 5-scale Lik-
ert metrics: Theoretical Consistency (Chen et al.,
2023b) and Clinical Actionability that rates the
practical utility of these representations in guiding
subsequent response generation (Es et al., 2024).

5.4.2 Safety Card Validation
Safety Card Quality Three professional,
psychologically-grounded certified coaches with
more than four years of experience worked on
100 randomly selected cards. The experts rated
each sample using four metrics: Faithfulness, Ac-
tionability, Generalizability, and Safety (Es et al.,
2024) (detailed definition is shown in A.6). With
a Fleiss’ kappa of 0.785, Faithfulness is 4.97/5
and Actionability is 4.93/5; all 100 rated cards
were judged generalizable and safety-appropriate.
This suggests that, at least on the 100 audited
samples, the cards offer a usable structural guide
for response generation; broader clinical validity
would require expert review at full scale.
Card Retrieval We observed the quality of card
retrieval using top-3 candidates with LLMs and
humans. The assessment use four metrics to mea-
sure the relevance: Topical & Intent Relevance
(TIR) (Es et al., 2024), Actionability & Utility (AU)
(Sharma et al., 2020), Safety & Severity Alignment
(SSA) (Miner et al., 2016), and Counterfactual Im-
pact (CI) (Lewis et al., 2020) (a Likert scale) (Ap-
pendix A.7). Human evaluation used four evalua-
tors to judge these metrics on 100 samples.
