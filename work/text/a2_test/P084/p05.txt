4.1 Datasets
Evaluation uses single-turn datasets that include a

5 Results and Discussion
5.1 Information Retrieval
Table 2 indicates a generally consistent pattern:
the incorporation of cognitive modeling, Appraisal
Theory and CBT constructs, enhances retrieval ef-
fectiveness across most evaluation metrics (typ-
ically +1 to +3 R@1 points) compared to ap-
proaches relying on semantic similarity only (par-
wise t-test of the best method with baseline). This
is consistent with the view that semantic match-
ing alone may not adequately capture the latent in-
tent and lexical asymmetry embedded within user
queries in conversational mental health support. By
explicitly modeling cognitive appraisals and CBT-
style reframing, the retrieval process better aligns
user intents with appropriate counseling knowledge
that bridges the lexical gap of standard embedding
models in mental health domains (Scherer et al.,
2001; Beck, 2020). The performance of our method
is significantly better than HyDE (p−values ≤ 0.05
with 95% CIs). Because HyDE applies a single,
asymmetric LLM rewrite to the query only and
without structural cognitive priors, the +3–6 R@1
gap between HyDE and our pipeline (with the same
gpt-embed-3-large encoder) is consistent with the
contribution of (i) symmetric prompt projection ap-
plied to both q and d and (ii) the appraisal/CBT
structure of the prompts, rather than the LLM-
rewriting effect alone. Standard semantic embed-
dings (gpt-3-small or ada-002) with cognitive fac-
tors can yield competitive results to LightRAG or
RAG-Anything that operates purely on semantic
signals (Guo et al., 2025b,a). This suggests that
cognitive factors offer a complementary signal to
graph- or multi-source semantic retrieval, rather
than a substitute for stronger semantic encoders.
