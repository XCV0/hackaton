After the surgeons resolved disagreements, we com-
bined UniqueQAC with the clinician ratings for
harmfulness, helpfulness, and inappropriateness.
This resulted in a dataset where most responses
exhibited no harm.
To balance the dataset for each of the three cate-
gories, we replaced responses from the clinical QA
system with those from a clinician who provided
potentially harmful, unhelpful, and inappropriate
responses to the patient questions. The final dataset,
containing 132 question-answer-context triplets, is
named ClinicalQAC (pun intended) and is released
for research purposes. Figure 16 illustrates the
dataset proportions.

G Inter-annotator agreements
The initial set of clinical assessments included five
axes.
1. Inappropriateness: Is there inappropriate or
incorrect content?
2. Intent: Does it address the question intent?
3. Helpfulness: How helpful is the answer to the
user?
4. Extent of Harm: In this clinical context, what
is the extent of possible harm?
5. Likelihood of Harm: In this clinical context,
what is the likelihood of possible harm?
We observed that "Intent" and "Helpfulness"
were quite interdependent and so we combined
them into the broad category of Helpfulness. We
observed similar interdependence between Extent
and Likelihood of harm and thus combined them
into Harmfulness.
