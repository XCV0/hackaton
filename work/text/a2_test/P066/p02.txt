2 Related work
2.1 Background to clinical QA evaluation
Clinical QA systems powered by LLMs have gen-
erated significant recent interest. Already, some
LLMs have demonstrated capabilities to generate
more accurate responses (Thirunavukarasu et al.,
2023; Bernstein et al., 2023; Samaan et al., 2023;
Xie et al., 2023; Van Bulck and Moons, 2024),
and sometimes even more empathetic than doctors
across various clinical contexts (Lee et al., 2024).
However, LLMs can generate plausible-sounding,
but factually incorrect responses, commonly re-
ferred to as "hallucinations" (Ji et al., 2023). More-
over, LLMs have knowledge cut-off date (Ovadia
et al., 2023) and this can pose significant safety
risks in healthcare. While these issues can be miti-
gated using RAG, risks still remain.
To address some of these risks specific to clinical
QA systems using RAG, various efforts have been
made to develop performance benchmarks. Cur-
rently, published benchmarks often utilize multiple-
choice or categorical ground-truth answers for re-
sponses (Xiong et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024; Wu
et al., 2024; Nori et al., 2023), which fail to capture
the complexities and risks associated with open-
ended response generations. Where open-ended
answers are evaluated, n-gram-based metrics such
as BLEU (Papineni et al., 2002) or ROUGE (Lin,
2004), historically used for machine translation,
have been used (Chen et al., 2019). However, these
evaluations have been criticized for failing to cap-
ture the nuanced requirements of clinical QA, and
even transformer-based metrics such as BertScore
(Zhang et al., 2019) have faced numerous semantic
limitations (Dada et al., 2024).
A key feature of these risks in the context of
open-ended clinical QA is their non-binary nature
(i.e., an answer is not simply "safe" or "unsafe" on
a single axis). Consequently, the gold standard for
assessing clinical inappropriateness remains human
evaluation. For instance, Google’s work in clinical
QA involved both clinicians and lay individuals, la-
beling responses based on various axes such as the
likelihood and severity of harm, alignment with sci-
entific consensus, and helpfulness (Singhal et al.,
2023a). Similarly, other studies have employed
multi-axis evaluations with human clinicians to as-
sess the overall appropriateness of responses for
open-ended clinical QA (Mukherjee et al., 2024;
Singhal et al., 2023b; Zakka et al., 2024; Chowd-
hury et al., 2023).
