An interesting observation is the performance gap between
LLM backbones across datasets. On 2WikiMultihopQA, which fea-
tures relatively structured reasoning paths, STRIDE with Qwen3-8B
achieves higher EM (0.692) than with GPT-4o-mini (0.661), suggest-
ing that Qwen3-8B’s strong instruction-following capability and
output controllability lead to more precise answer generation. In
contrast, on the more challenging MuSiQue, GPT-4o-mini signifi-
cantly outperforms Qwen3-8B (0.33 vs. 0.288 EM), indicating that
its superior language understanding and generalization are critical
when reasoning depth increases. This highlights the complemen-
tary strengths of different LLMs and the adaptability of STRIDE
across model families.
Furthermore, the fine-tuned variant (i.e., FT) provides consistent
and substantial improvements. For instance, on 2WikiMultihopQA
with Qwen3-8B, FT boosts EM from 0.692 to 0.738 (+0.046), and
on MuSiQue, it increases F1 from 0.401 to 0.448 (+0.047). Notably,
FT with Qwen3-8B closes the performance gap with GPT-4o-mini
and even surpasses it on two datasets, validating that our modular
fine-tuning strategy effectively leverages self-generated trajectories
to enhance reasoning quality without external supervision.
Together, these results demonstrate that STRIDE enables robust
and scalable multi-hop reasoning in complex scenarios.

Ablation on the Framework. We first examine the impact of
removing key modules from the inference pipeline. w/o Meta-
Planner disables the abstract reasoning strategy by removing all
General Strategy-related prompts, forcing the planner to directly
output a concrete plan without high-level intent modeling. This
leads to significant performance drops across all datasets (e.g.,
−0.197 EM on 2WikiMultihopQA with Qwen3-8B), demonstrat-
ing that abstract planning provides crucial scaffolding for robust
multi-hop decomposition. w/o Supervisor replaces our process-
