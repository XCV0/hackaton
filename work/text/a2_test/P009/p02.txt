To support this process, we introduce a
novel Retrieval-Augmented Generation frame-
work, called Personalized-Topic RAG (PT-RAG),
which enables Topic-Initiator to retrieve new
and interesting topics effectively. Existing RAG
methods (Zhu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2023;
Wang et al., 2024; Zerhoudi, 2025) are optimized
to answer user queries by retrieving information
similar to those queries and therefore tend to re-
produce content already present in past interac-
tions, which limits their usefulness for new topic
discovery. PT-RAG addresses this gap by using
LLM inference to identify topic candidates that are
absent from the user’s existing knowledge data
while constraining retrieval to a relevant topic cat-
egory to keep inputs tractable.
Topic-Initiator initiates conversations using top-
ics retrieved by PT-RAG, updates the user’s inter-
ests and knowledge based on the conversation,
and then gathers new topic candidates. By re-
peating this cycle, Topic-Initiator can continuously
provide users with novel and interesting topics.
To demonstrate the effectiveness of Topic-
Initiator and PT-RAG, this study addresses the fol-
lowing research questions:
RQ1: Can we obtain new and interesting top-
ics for a user by categorizing and retrieving
them solely from categories that align with the
user’s interests?
RQ2: Can a chatbot stimulate a user’s willingness
to converse by initiating a conversation using
a topic that is new and interesting to the user?
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose Topic-Initiator, a proactive
chatbot that continuously provides users
with new and interesting topics along with
Personalized-Topic RAG (PT-RAG), a novel
RAG framework speciﬁcally designed to re-
trieve such topics.
• Through automatic evaluations, we demon-
strates that PT-RAG retrieves topics more ac-
curately than existing methods.
• Through human evaluation, we show that
Topic-Initiator signiﬁcantly stimulate users’
willingness to converse compared with exist-
ing chatbots.
2. Related Work
2.1. Proactive Chatbots
Extensive research has been conducted on proac-
tive chatbots and dialogue systems (Deng et al.,
2023a). Tang et al. (2019); Wu et al. (2019);
Wang et al. (2023a,b); Deng et al. (2023b) pro-
posed dialogue systems that set conversational
goals and actively guide users toward achieving
them. Xie et al. (2021) introduced a method in
which dialogue systems proactively generate ut-
terances to switch topics during casual conversa-
tions. Although these studies aim to build proac-
tive chatbots similar to ours, they overlook sce-
narios in which the chatbot itself initiates the con-
versationʕa key distinction in our work. Li et al.
(2023) proposed a method where chatbots en-
gage in conversations by exploring trending news.
While this approach enables a chatbot to proac-
tively initiate conversations, it differs from our work
in that it disregards user interests and relies on
ﬁxed topics.
2.2. Personalized Chatbots
Numerous studies have also explored personal-
ized chatbots and dialogue systems (Chen et al.,
2024). The term ”personalized” has two def-
initions (Chen et al., 2024): (1) generating re-
sponses consistent with the system’s own per-
sona, and (2) generating responses tailored to the
user’s persona. Our work focuses on the latter.
Zhong et al. (2022) proposed a method that
generates responses using proﬁles of a tar-
get user and similar users. Jang et al. (2023)
presented a method that enables a chatbot to
adapt its responses over time by training it on
a dataset encoding elapsed time and multiple
types of interpersonal relationships. Xu et al.
(2022); Zhong et al. (2024); Packer et al. (2023);
Bae et al. (2022); Lee et al. (2023); Lim et al.
(2022) introduced approaches that extract per-
sonal data from user utterances, store it, and gen-
erate responses based on that information. While
these methods personalize chatbot responses,
they do not address the proactive initiation of con-
versations, which is a key aspect of our research.
