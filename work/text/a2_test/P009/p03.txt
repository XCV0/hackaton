2.4. Conversational Recommender
Systems
Conversational recommender systems (CRS)
are designed to actively elicit user prefer-
ences through conversation and recommend
items based on those insights (Friedman et al.,
2023; Deldjoo et al., 2024). Several studies
have focused on improving user engagement
and recommendation success. For instance,
Hayati et al. (2020) integrated sociable recom-
mendation strategies into CRS, while Wang et al.
(2022) combined recommendation and conver-
sation modules. Other studies have evaluated
whether LLMs can accurately recommend items
in zero-shot settings. Kang et al. (2023); Liu et al.
(2023); Sanner et al. (2023); Sileo et al. (2022);
He et al. (2023) constructed prompts that merge
user preferences with instructions to recommend
or evaluate items. Although these studies share
our goal of understanding user preferences for
proactive recommendations, they do not estimate
user knowledge or assess the user’s familiarity
with the recommended items.
3. Proposed Method: Topic-Initiator
3.1. Architecture
As introduced in Section 1, Topic-Initiator follows
an iterative process to continuously provide new
topics that align with a target user’s interests. The
overall procedure consists of three mains steps (A
to C), illustrated in Figure 2.
A. Information Gathering: After interacting with
a user, Topic-Initiator proactively gathers relevant
information based on the conversation content.
Speciﬁcally, it employs an LLM to generate search
queries from the conversation and conducts web
searches to collect topic candidates. Additionally,
Topic-Initiator stores past conversations with non-
target users as topic candidates, allowing them to
be reused to enrich future conversations.
