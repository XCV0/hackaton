Vanilla-RAG : A standard RAG method imple-
mented by the llama-index5. It partitions infor-
mation into ﬁxed-sized chunks and vectorizes
each chunk, then retrieves the top k (K = 5
in this experiment) most similar chunks to the
query vector for response generation. Since
Vanilla-RAG does not estimate user knowl-
edge, a summary list of the speaker’s conver-
sation was added as alternative knowledge
data to {user_data}.
PersonaRAG :
Introduced by (Zerhoudi, 2025) and de-
scribed in Section 2. Since this method
does not include an original retriever, we used
the retriever of Vanilla-RAG. Like Vanilla-
RAG, it also lacks user knowledge estima-
tion, so the same information was provided
to {user_data}.
4.1.4. Metrics
We evaluated the performance of each method
using the following three metrics:
Interest: Following the LLM-as-a-judge frame-
work (Zheng et al., 2023), ChatGPT-4o was
used to determine whether each generated
response would be interesting to the target
speaker. Each response was scored on a 10-
point scale (1-10) reference to the speaker’s
persona data. The prompt used is provided
in Appendix A.4. ChatGPT-4o was chosen to
avoid self-preference, where an LLM favors
its outputs over those from other LLMs and
humans (Panickssery et al., 2024).
Novelty: Using the same LLM-as-a-judge ap-
proach, ChatGPT-4o rated the novelty of each
responses on a 10-point scale, based on
whether the content was new or already fa-
miliar to the target speaker. A summary of

the speaker’s past conversations was used to
represent as their prior knowledge. Abstract
responses lacking speciﬁc details or proper
nouns were treated as known information and
penalized accordingly. The prompt used is
provided in Appendix A.4.
Interest-Novelty-F1 (IN-F1): To evaluate the bal-
ance between ”Interest” and ”Novelty”, we
calculated the harmonic mean of the two
scores. A higher IN-F1 score indicates that
the method can retrieve information that is
both new and interesting to the speaker.
To verify the reliability of the LLM-based evalua-
tions, we compared these scores with human as-
sessments. Twelve evaluators participated in this
validation. We selected 20 samples using strati-
ﬁed sampling based on average LLM scores to en-
sure balanced coverage. Each sample was inde-
pendently rated by at least three evaluators, and
the human score was calculated as the average of
their ratings. Finally, we computed the Spearman
correlation coefﬁcient (Spearman, 1961) between
the human and the LLM scores.
4.1.5. Results
The results are presented in Tables 3. From this
table, PersonaRAG achieved the highest Interest
scores among all methods. On the other hand,
PT-RAG achieved signiﬁcantly higher scores in
both Novelty and IN-F1 compared to the other
methods. These results demonstrate that Vanilla-
RAG, PersonaRAG, and PT-RAG-KO are less ef-
fective at retrieving new information for the target
speaker. This limitation arises because Vanilla-
RAG and PersonaRAG primarily retrieve content
that is semantically similar to the query without
considering whether the information is already
known to the user. PersonaRAG, in particular,
tends to generate more interesting responses be-
cause its multi-agent reﬁnement process focuses
on content closely aligned with user interests.
