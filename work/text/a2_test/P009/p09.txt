4.2.4. Results
The results are presented in Figure 6. The hor-
izontal axis in Figure 6 represents the n-th con-
versation, while the vertical axis shows the win-
ning rate in the A/B tests. As shown in Fig-
ures 6(a) and 6(b), a signiﬁcantly larger num-
ber of participants preferred conversing with either
the Topic-Initiator chatbot or the proactive chat-
bot compared to the baseline. Furthermore, Fig-
ure 6(c) indicates that during the ﬁrst conversa-
tion, signiﬁcantly more participants preferred the
proactive chatbot over Topic-Initiator. However,
as the interactions progressed, more participants
favored Topic-Initiator over the proactive chatbot,
with a signiﬁcant preference emerging from the
third conversation onward. These results highlight
that the importance of taking initiative in conver-
sation to stimulate user’s willingness to converse.
Additionally, they emphasize the role of topic se-
lection. When the proactive chatbot asked about
information previously disclosed by the user, it in-
creased the user’s willingness to converse. This
is likely because it demonstrated that the chat-
bot remembered past interactions. This ﬁnding is
consistent with Tsunomori et al. (2021). However,
maintaining user’s willingness solely through such
methods proved difﬁcult, as indicated by the re-
sults. In contrast, providing users with fresh and
interesting topics helped sustain and further en-
hance their willingness to continue conversations.

5. Conclusion
We proposed Topic-Initiator, a chatbot that con-
tinuously and proactively provides users with new
and interesting topics. To obtain such topics, it
collects both web-crawled data and conversations
with other users. To enable Topic-Initiator to re-
trieve new and interesting topics for users, we
also introduced a novel RAG framework called PT-
RAG. PT-RAG estimates user interests and knowl-
edge, organizes both user data and collected data
into topic categories, identiﬁes relevant categories
based on user interests, and retrieves new topics
within those categories based on user knowledge
by leveraging the inference capabilities of LLMs.
Our ﬁndings addressed the research questions as
follows: RQ1: Through automatic evaluations, we
demonstrated that PT-RAG more accurately ob-
tains new and interesting topics for users com-
pared with existing RAG methods. RQ2: Through
human evaluation of the Topic-Initiator chatbot, we
showed that users were more willing to converse
with our system than with chatbots that either talk
known topics or remained passive.
Topic-Initiator focuses on stimulating the willing-
ness during subsequent conversation initiations.
In our future works, we intend to extend our
method to enhance the willingness during other
phases of interaction.
