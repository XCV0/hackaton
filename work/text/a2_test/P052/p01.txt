When the Correct Law Still Fails: A Diagnostic Benchmark for
Law-in-Force Retrieval in Versioned Legal RAG

Abstract
Legal RAG systems often retrieve passages
by semantic relevance, lexical score, or ci-
tation match. In versioned legal corpora,
however, a passage can be topically relevant
and citation-matched while being legally in-
valid for the operative date. We study law-
in-force retrieval: retrieving evidence that
matches the authority, is valid at the opera-
tive date, and supports the answer. We build
LawInForce-eCFR from 805 human-validated
eCFR/CFR amendment pairs and 3,220 con-
trolled answer-flipping yes/no questions. We
also present AuthorityFirst-RAG, a transparent
diagnostic retriever that decomposes retrieval
into authority identification, temporal scoping,
and rule-support ranking. AuthorityFirst-RAG
improves Recall@1 from 2.80% for BM25-
date-filter to 30.90%, but generation remains
difficult even with GoldOracle evidence. These
results show a dual bottleneck in versioned le-
gal RAG: retrieving the law in force and apply-
ing numeric legal rules.
1 Introduction
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds
language-model outputs in retrieved evidence
(Lewis et al., 2020) and has become a common
architecture for legal question answering, retrieval,
and regulatory analysis (Ma et al., 2021; Hou et al.,
2025; Kalra et al., 2024). Existing legal NLP and
legal retrieval work often evaluates broad legal un-
derstanding, topical relevance, citation-like author-
ity, or evidence retrieval (Chalkidis et al., 2022;
Guha et al., 2023; van Opijnen and Santos, 2017).
These objectives are important, but they do not
by themselves establish that a retrieved passage is
legally applicable.
Versioned law exposes a stricter requirement.
A passage may be semantically relevant, citation-
matched, and located in the correct section, yet still

be legally invalid. Legal rules are amended over
time, while old and current versions often coexist
in the same corpus or index. For a legal question,
the operative date is not ancillary metadata; it de-
termines whether a rule can govern the facts at all.
The central claim of this paper is therefore that
versioned legal RAG requires retrieving legally ap-
plicable authority, not merely relevant text.
This distinction matters because wrong-version
evidence can have near-zero utility. In many do-
mains, a topically related document may still pro-
vide background information. In legal application,
however, an inapplicable version can support the
opposite answer with high surface plausibility. A
retrieved passage can look correct to both the re-
triever and the generator because it shares the same
citation, heading, vocabulary, and paragraph struc-
ture as the valid rule. Its defect is not topical irrele-
vance; its defect is legal invalidity.
For example, suppose an older regulation re-
quired an application to be filed within 60 days,
while a later amendment changed the period to 90
days. If the applicant filed on day 75, the answer
is no under the old rule and yes under the new rule.
Both versions may mention the same application,
use the same section number, and differ only in a
local numeric threshold. A retriever that returns the
later version for an older operative date has found
the right topic and citation, but the wrong legally
applicable evidence. The example is diagnostic
precisely because lexical, semantic, and citation
signals point toward both versions while only one
is authority for the facts.
This paper studies that failure mode directly. Le-
gal time is not merely a factual timestamp; it is a
normative applicability condition. We define law-
in-force retrieval as retrieving evidence that be-
longs to the relevant authority, is valid at the opera-
tive date, and contains the rule support needed to
answer the question. The retrieval target is there-
fore authority-validity-support evidence rather than
