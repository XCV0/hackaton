We compare lexical, citation-assisted, ColBERT-
style, and authority-first retrieval, alongside
an oracle evidence condition. BM25-only
uses Okapi BM25 scoring (Robertson and
Zaragoza, 2009) as version-blind lexical retrieval.
BM25-date-filter is BM25 retrieval with tem-
poral filtering. citation-hybrid date filter is a
metadata-assisted diagnostic baseline: it uses ci-
tation/authority cues and date filtering, with gold
title/section fallback metadata for uncited queries
to test version selection under partially oracle
authority information. The ColBERT-style late-
interaction baseline ranks passages without validity
filtering in its version-blind condition; the post-
date-filter condition removes temporally invalid
passages after retrieval, retaining the remaining
ranking order. GoldOracle is an oracle evidence
condition with 100% retrieval and is used as an
upper bound for generation, not as a deployable
retriever.
5.2 Generation evaluation
Generation is evaluated by feeding a question and
retrieved evidence to open-weight LLMs and pars-
ing a yes/no answer. The prompt supplies the ques-
tion and evidence; the model must decide whether
the fact pattern satisfies the applicable rule. This
setup reflects a common legal RAG pipeline while
keeping retrieval and answer generation analyti-
cally separable.
Generation models receive only the top-1 re-
trieved paragraph-level passage rather than the full
CFR section. Citation and heading information are
embedded in the retrieved text; validity intervals
are used for retrieval filtering but are not separately
provided to the generator.
Generation evaluation is reported for completed
model-condition runs. The reported evaluations
contain 1,200 examples for Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct
(Yang et al., 2025b); 96 examples each for Mistral-
7B-Instruct-v0.3 (Jiang et al., 2023), Qwen3-8B,
and Qwen3-14B (Yang et al., 2025a); and 2,576 ex-
amples each for Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-
7B-Instruct (Yang et al., 2025b), Zephyr-7B-beta
(Tunstall et al., 2024), Mistral-Nemo-Instruct-
2407 (Mistral AI, 2024), and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-
Qwen-7B/14B (Guo et al., 2025). Because sample
sizes differ, cross-model comparisons should be
interpreted cautiously. We emphasize evidence-
condition and failure-mode comparisons rather
than model ranking.
6 Results
6.1 Authority-first ranking improves top-rank
legal applicability
Table 4 shows that authority-first ranking sub-
stantially improves top-rank legal applicability.
AuthorityFirst-RAG reaches 30.90% Recall@1,
compared with 2.80% for BM25-date-filter, 9.16%
for citation-hybrid date filter, and 0.31% for
BM25-only. The improvement reflects modeling
legal authority and temporal validity before ranking
local rule text. On the full 3,220-instance bench-
mark, AuthorityFirst-RAG preserves the strongest
overall ranking pattern but with lower absolute Re-
call@1, indicating that the full benchmark is harder
than the 644-instance test split.
The contrast with citation-hybrid date filter is in-
formative: it reaches 77.17% Recall@10 but much
lower Recall@1, so citation/date signals often find
the right candidate without ranking it first. In RAG,
wrong top evidence can dominate the answer; law-
in-force retrieval therefore requires top-rank rank-
ing of legally applicable authority.
ColBERT-style retrieval remains weak even with
post-retrieval date filtering, suggesting that se-
mantic matching alone does not resolve author-
ity/version selection.
On a balanced 100-instance subset, a tool-using
LLM agent reaches 15.0% R@1 versus 23.0%
for AuthorityFirst-RAG; both obtain 0% R@1 on
uncited queries. Agent traces indicate that can-
didate generation/authority discovery is the main
bottleneck (Appendix D).
