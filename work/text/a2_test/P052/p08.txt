7 Discussion
7.1 Law in force is different from semantic
relevance
A relevant passage is not necessarily legally appli-
cable. Semantic and citation-aware retrieval reward
topical and authority-like signals; law-in-force re-
trieval adds validity and rule support. Wrong ver-
sions are hard because they often share section
numbers, headings, definitions, and most wording;
in LawInForce-eCFR, the decisive difference may
be a single threshold or deadline.
7.2 Wrong versions have near-zero utility
Wrong versions can be more harmful than irrel-
evant text because they appear authoritative and
answer-bearing. This is why Recall@1 matters: if
the first passage is the wrong version, the system
may rely on authority that cannot govern the facts.
7.3 The dual bottleneck
AuthorityFirst-RAG improves top-rank retrieval,
but downstream answer gains remain modest. Ver-
sioned legal RAG therefore has a dual bottleneck:
selecting the law in force and applying its numeric
rule. Better evidence shifts failures from semantic
grounding toward rule application, showing that
retrieval and rule application are distinct failure
modes. Controlled numeric amendments make this
split diagnosable because old/new versions, fact
values, answer flips, and gold evidence are verifi-
able.

Legal NLP benchmarks. LexGLUE (Chalkidis
et al., 2022), LEGAL-BERT (Chalkidis et al.,
2020), ContractNLI (Koreeda and Manning, 2021),
and LegalBench (Guha et al., 2023) evaluate broad
legal-language tasks; they do not test whether re-
trieved law was in force for an operative date.
Legal retrieval and legal RAG. BM25 is a stan-

2009), and RAG combines retrieval with genera-
tion (Lewis et al., 2020). LeCaRD (Ma et al., 2021),
CLERC (Hou et al., 2025), and Hypa-RAG (Kalra
et al., 2024) study legal relevance and evidence re-
trieval, while legal IR work emphasizes structured
relevance (van Opijnen and Santos, 2017). They
do not directly test operative-date validity when
multiple versions share the same authority.
