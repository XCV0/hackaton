3.4 Human validation
Human validation removes cases where determin-
istic matching is not enough. Our checklist verifies
rule equivalence between old and new text, a sin-
gle numeric threshold change, unit compatibility,
comparator clarity, and deterministic answer flips,
and rejects transition clauses, retroactivity, tolling,
exceptions, and non-deterministic drafting changes.
The 94% acceptance rate reflects that rule-based
filters remove most unsuitable candidates before
human review. Appendix A gives the checklist and
field schema.
All 805 amendment pairs were independently
reviewed by two annotators using the valida-
tion checklist. The annotators achieved 95.28%
agreement; disagreements were discussed, with
unresolved cases adjudicated by a third experi-
enced annotator. The same dual-annotation and
disagreement-resolution procedure was used for
error-taxonomy annotation.
3.5 Benchmark purpose
LawInForce-eCFR is a diagnostic benchmark, not
a comprehensive model of legal advice. Its purpose
is to stress a failure mode that ordinary relevance
evaluation can hide. A system receives credit only
when it retrieves evidence that is authoritative, tem-
porally valid, and answer-supporting. This makes
the benchmark useful for measuring whether re-
trieval systems can distinguish legally applicable
text from plausible but invalid nearby versions.
4 AuthorityFirst-RAG: A Diagnostic
Retriever
4.1 Method overview
AuthorityFirst-RAG is a transparent authority-first
retrieval procedure. We do not present it as a univer-
sal neural retriever or as a deployable legal search
engine. Instead, it is designed to test which legal-
structural signals matter for law-in-force retrieval.
The pipeline first extracts query structure, then iden-
tifies candidate authorities, applies temporal scop-
ing, retrieves within the candidate authority scope,
and finally ranks passages by rule-support signals.
AuthorityFirst-RAG is implemented with deter-
ministic rule-based parsing and heuristic feature-
based ranking rather than a learned retriever. Ci-
tation cues, operative dates, fact values, units, and
comparator signals are extracted using regular ex-
pressions and rule-based normalization. After tem-
poral filtering, ranking combines authority/citation
match, BM25 relevance, lexical overlap, thresh-
old/unit compatibility, and comparator cues; no
learned parameters are used.
