4.2 Query parsing
The query parser extracts four types of information.
First, it identifies citation cues when the question
explicitly mentions a title, part, section, or other
authority marker. Second, it extracts the opera-
tive date that determines which version is in force.
Third, it extracts the fact value and unit, such as
a number of days, dollars, employees, percent, or
years. Fourth, it detects whether the question is
cited or uncited. This distinction affects how much
weight the retriever gives to exact citation matching
versus broader lexical and legal-context cues.
4.3 Authority identification
When citation cues are present,
AuthorityFirst-RAG prioritizes exact or nor-
malized matches to the cited authority and
section. When the question is uncited, it uses
lexical overlap and legal-context cues to identify
candidate sections. This stage is deliberately
section-first: it tries to locate the legal authority
before ranking individual paragraphs. The design
reflects the structure of legal retrieval, where the
correct paragraph is often meaningful only within
a particular authority.
4.4 Temporal scoping and section-first
retrieval
After candidate authorities are identified,
AuthorityFirst-RAG filters or prioritizes passages
whose validity intervals cover the operative date.
Temporal scoping is applied before fine-grained
rule ranking so that the system does not treat
obsolete and current versions as interchangeable.
Section-first retrieval then ranks paragraphs within
the authority scope rather than across the entire
corpus. This reduces the chance that a globally
similar passage from the wrong version displaces
the legally applicable passage.
Stage Operation
1 Parse citation cues, operative date, fact value/unit,
and cited/uncited status.
2 Identify candidate authorities, using exact citation
matches or legal-context cues.
3 Scope candidates to validity intervals covering the
operative date.
4 Retrieve within candidate sections before paragraph
ranking.
5 Rank by authority, date validity, lexical overlap,
threshold/unit match, and comparator compatibility.
6 Return the highest-scoring legally applicable
evidence.

4.5 Rule-aware ranking
The final ranking stage combines lexical relevance
with rule-support features. These features include
threshold match, unit compatibility, comparator
compatibility, citation or section match, and date
validity. Comparator compatibility is crucial be-
cause expressions such as within, not more than, at
least, shall not exceed, and between encode differ-
ent comparison directions. A passage that contains
the right number but reverses the legal comparison
does not support the answer.

ask whether the local rule text supports the answer.
This decomposition lets the experiments separate
failures caused by authority identification, temporal
