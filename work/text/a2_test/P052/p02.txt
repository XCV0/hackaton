We construct LawInForce-eCFR, a diagnostic
benchmark derived from real amendments in ver-
sioned eCFR/CFR regulations. The benchmark
starts from legal changes rather than naturally col-
lected user questions. This is intentional: by align-
ing old and new versions of the same rule, extract-
ing numeric threshold changes, and placing fact
values between the old and new thresholds, we cre-
ate controlled answer flips. The design isolates the
wrong-version failure mode: an incorrect passage
can be topically close and citation-wise close while
still being invalid for the facts.
LawInForce-eCFR contains 805 human-
validated amendment pairs and 3,220 yes/no
questions across cited and uncited formulations.
We also implement AuthorityFirst-RAG, a trans-
parent diagnostic retriever that separates authority
identification, temporal scoping, section-first
retrieval, and rule-support ranking. Retrieval
improves sharply at the top rank, but generation
improves much less, revealing a dual bottleneck:
systems must retrieve the law in force and apply
its numeric rule.
This setting is distinct from neighboring prob-
lems. Temporal QA asks time-specific factual ques-
tions, whereas law-in-force retrieval asks whether
a legal norm was valid authority for a fact pattern.
Citation-based legal retrieval can still fail because
the same citation may refer to multiple historical
versions. Numeric reasoning benchmarks test arith-
metic or comparison in isolation; our setting re-
quires applying a numeric comparison only after
the valid legal rule has been selected.
Our contributions are:
1. We formalize law-in-force retrieval as authority-
validity-support retrieval: the evidence must
match the authority, be valid for the operative
date, and contain answer-supporting rule text.
2. We build LawInForce-eCFR from real
eCFR/CFR amendments, yielding 805 human-
validated amendment pairs and 3,220 controlled
answer-flipping QA instances.
3. We introduce AuthorityFirst-RAG, a transpar-
ent diagnostic retriever that separates author-
ity identification, temporal scoping, and rule-
support ranking.
4. We show empirically that retrieval improves sub-
stantially but generation remains hard, revealing
a dual bottleneck in versioned legal RAG.
2 Task Definition
2.1 Problem setting
Let C be a versioned legal corpus containing pas-
sages from multiple versions of legal authorities. A
passage d ∈ C is represented as
d = (text, citation, section, paragraph,
valid f rom, valid to).
The fields citation and section identify the le-
gal authority. The interval [valid f rom, valid to)
represents the period during which the passage was
in force, with an open-ended valid to indicating
continued validity.
A query contains a legal question q and an oper-
ative date t. The operative date is the date at which
the facts are evaluated. In a regulatory filing ques-
tion, for example, it may be the filing date, deadline
date, conduct date, or effective-date-relevant fac-
tual date specified by the prompt. A passage is
temporally valid for the query if
valid f rom(d) ≤ t < valid to(d).
This condition is not a convenience filter. It deter-
mines whether the passage can serve as valid legal
authority.
2.2 Legally applicable evidence
We define a passage as legally applicable evidence
for a question when it satisfies three conditions:
1. Authority match: it belongs to the relevant
legal authority, such as the correct citation, sec-
tion, or regulatory provision.
2. Temporal validity: the operative date falls
within the passage validity interval.
3. Rule support: the text contains the threshold,
comparator, unit, exception, or local rule lan-
guage needed to answer the question.
These conditions are jointly necessary. Authority
match without temporal validity can retrieve the
wrong historical rule. Temporal validity without
authority match can retrieve a contemporaneous but
irrelevant provision. Authority and validity without
rule support can retrieve a nearby paragraph that
does not answer the question.

tion has a gold passage and a gold answer. Retrieval
evaluation asks whether the correct in-force pas-
