9 Conclusion
We introduced LawInForce-eCFR, a diagnostic
benchmark for law-in-force retrieval in versioned
legal RAG, together with AuthorityFirst-RAG, a
transparent retriever for authority, temporal valid-
ity, and rule support. The central lesson is that
topically relevant or citation-matched law can still
be legally wrong when it is not in force.
The results show a dual bottleneck: retrieve
applicable authority, then apply its numeric rule.
Wrong versions can support the opposite answer,
while correct evidence can still fail when models
misapply thresholds, deadlines, or comparator di-
rection. Versioned legal RAG must therefore be
both applicability-aware and rule-faithful.
10 Limitations
LawInForce-eCFR covers U.S. federal eCFR/CFR
regulations and focuses on clean numeric thresh-
old amendments. It does not cover other jurisdic-
tions, statutes, case law, contracts, agency guid-
ance, or legal sources with different versioning
practices. The benchmark also excludes transi-
tion clauses, retroactivity, tolling, cross-references,
multi-condition rules, exceptions, and difficult le-
gal interpretation.
Numeric thresholds, deadlines, caps, percent-
ages, counts, penalties, ages, and frequencies are
useful for controlled diagnosis because they sup-
port deterministic answer flips across versions.
Many legal questions, however, require broader
doctrinal reasoning, distributed definitions, factual
uncertainty, agency discretion, and multi-step statu-
tory interpretation. The results should therefore
be interpreted as evidence about a controlled law-
in-force retrieval failure mode, not as a complete
evaluation of legal reasoning or legal advice.
AuthorityFirst-RAG is a transparent diagnostic
retriever, not a deployable legal search engine. It
assumes structured citation, section, paragraph, and
validity-interval metadata, and metadata-assisted
baselines should be interpreted diagnostically. De-
ployed systems may require more robust citation
normalization, version alignment, and authority res-
olution; uncited authority inference remains weak.
Although the corpus preserves title,
part, section, and paragraph path metadata,
AuthorityFirst-RAG does not explicitly navigate
subsection/clause/subclause hierarchies (Blair-
Stanek et al., 2023). Authority inference remains
a major limitation: the uncited setting is largely
unsolved, so the strongest results primarily reflect
version selection when the relevant authority is
known or partially specified.
Generation sample sizes differ across mod-
els and conditions, so we emphasize evidence-
condition and failure-mode comparisons rather
than model rankings. Future work should cover
broader legal sources and more complex temporal
legal phenomena.
