completeness of upstream metadata. Its guarantees
apply only to retrieved candidate documents and
cannot recover evidence missed by retrieval or re-
construct missing supersession relations. The con-
clusions of the 50-document experiment are limited
to the controlled setting in which the target evi-
dence is retrieved; system behavior under retrieval
misses remains to be evaluated in realistic end-to-
end retrieval pipelines. Our experiments use coun-
terfactual regulatory-style datasets and API-served
models; future work should evaluate TrustRAG on
larger authentic policy collections, noisier organi-
zational metadata, and deployment-time retrieval
and audit workflows.
Societal impact. TrustRAG can reduce evidence-
governance risks in compliance-oriented QA, but
incorrect metadata or permission configurations
may still cause information exposure or inappro-
priate refusals, while excessive reliance on auto-
mated routing may lead users to overestimate the
system’s compliance capabilities. Deployments
should therefore integrate TrustRAG into, rather
than substitute it for, existing organizational gover-
nance and accountability processes, while retaining
continuous monitoring, human audits, and mecha-
nisms for reviewing or appealing refusals and con-
flict reports.
