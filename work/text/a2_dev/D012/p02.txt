During generation, self-reflection and verification
mechanisms help correct unreliable outputs (Asai
et al., 2024), while automated frameworks evalu-
ate whether generated answers are faithful to the
retrieved context (Niu et al., 2024; Saad-Falcon
et al., 2024). Although these methods improve rel-
evance and factual accuracy, they typically treat
retrieved evidence merely as flat contextual text.
Even studies addressing access control, outdated
evidence, or knowledge conflicts (Chen et al., 2025;
Ouyang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025a; Lee et al.,
2025) often lack deterministic mechanisms to ex-
clude inadmissible or superseded evidence prior to
generation based on structured metadata.
To bridge this gap, we propose TrustRAG, a
metadata-governed RAG framework designed for
compliance-oriented QA. Rather than replacing
the retriever, TrustRAG introduces a metadata-
governed routing layer between the retriever and
the generator to explicitly determine which re-
trieved evidence enters the generation context. We
focus on mitigating three core governance failures:
(1) Unauthorized leakage, where the model dis-
closes facts inaccessible to the current user; (2)
Stale citation, where the model references outdated,
explicitly superseded facts; and (3) Unsupported
answers, where the model forces a fabricated con-
clusion when faced with multiple valid but conflict-
ing active documents. Figure 1 illustrates the differ-
ences in evidence processing between TrustRAG
and Naive RAG.
To systematically evaluate post-retrieval evi-
dence governance, we construct AeroTrustBench.
It encompasses scenarios with restricted access, su-
perseded documents, conflicting facts, and their
combinations, providing user permissions, docu-
ment metadata, candidate evidence, and ground-
truth answers for each query. We evaluate
TrustRAG across three strong general-purpose
models (Qwen-Max, DeepSeek-V3, and GLM-4-
32B-0414) against Naive RAG, governance base-
lines (Prompt RAG and Metadata Filter), and re-
trieval baselines (BM25 and Dense RAG). Com-
prehensive experiments involving component ab-
lations, robustness tests, and human audits demon-
strate that TrustRAG significantly reduces unau-
thorized leakage, stale citations, and unsupported
answers in the main evaluations, with consistent
trends observed in the supplementary experiments.
Our main contributions are as follows:
• We formalize post-retrieval risks in
compliance-oriented QA into three core
governance failures: unauthorized leakage,
stale citation, and unsupported answers.
