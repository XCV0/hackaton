5.5 Boundary Analysis under Metadata Noise
Figure 2 shows TrustRAG’s boundary behavior
when metadata is corrupted. Access-label noise
increases both leakage risk and over-filtering risk,
while missing supersession links increase stale-
citation risk. This pattern is consistent with
TrustRAG’s design assumption: TrustRAG deter-
ministically enforces the provided metadata con-
straints, but its routing guarantees depend on the
accuracy and completeness of upstream metadata.
Table 5 compares routing-level risks across meth-
ods under 30% mixed metadata noise. Permission-
Aware RAG reduces access risk but retains sub-
stantial supersession risk, whereas Metadata-Aware
RAG and TrustRAG further reduce supersession
risk and perform identically on these diagnostics
because they share the same access and superses-
sion filtering predicates. Beyond these shared filter-
ing operations, TrustRAG additionally defines de-
terministic refusal and conflict-reporting response
states. In the final-response evaluations, these
states allow the system to avoid defaulting to a sin-
gle answer when no admissible evidence remains
or unresolved conflicts persist among admissible
evidence, thereby further reducing unsupported-
answer rates, as shown in Tables 1 and 4.

Limitations

governance metadata associated with the retrieved
candidate set, including access labels, user-role
mappings, and supersession relations, is correct
and complete. In deployment, access-control and
document-lifecycle metadata should be maintained
by authoritative identity and access management
(IAM) and document-lifecycle systems, with ver-
