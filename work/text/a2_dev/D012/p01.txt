Abstract
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) en-
hances large language models with external
evidence, but in compliance-oriented QA, se-
mantic relevance alone does not determine
whether evidence should be used for genera-
tion. A retrieved document may be relevant
and factually correct, yet inadmissible because
it is inaccessible to the current user, explic-
itly superseded, or in unresolved conflict with
other active evidence. We formalize three post-
retrieval governance failures in this setting:
unauthorized leakage, stale citation, and un-
supported answers. To address them, we pro-
pose TrustRAG, a RAG framework that incor-
porates a metadata-governed routing layer be-
tween the retriever and the generator. Given
correct and complete governance metadata for
the retrieved candidate set, this layer filters
inadmissible evidence before generation, re-
turns a deterministic refusal when no admis-
sible evidence remains, and preserves unre-
solved conflicts for conflict-reporting genera-
tion. We construct AeroTrustBench, a coun-
terfactual compliance QA benchmark for eval-
uating evidence governance in RAG. Across
Qwen-Max, DeepSeek-V3, and GLM-4-32B-
0414, TrustRAG consistently reduces the three
failures relative to unguided RAG and baselines
based on prompting, filtering, routing, verifica-
tion, and recency. Mechanism-matched and 50-
document comparisons show benefits beyond
filtering under larger candidate pools, while
metadata-noise tests and human audits charac-
terize dependencies on upstream metadata and
evaluator choice.
1 Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have been widely
adopted for knowledge-intensive QA, yet their re-
liance on static parameters makes them difficult
to update as real-world facts evolve (Brown et al.,
2020; Ouyang et al., 2022; Liska et al., 2022; Ka-
sai et al., 2023). Retrieval-Augmented Generation
(RAG) effectively mitigates this issue by incorpo-
rating external documentary evidence (Lewis et al.,
2020). However, compliance-oriented QA requires
RAG to not only consider the semantic relevance
of retrieved evidence but also determine whether
that evidence can be utilized to generate an answer
for the current task. Unlike open-domain RAG, a
document may be semantically relevant and factu-
ally correct, yet remain inadmissible under strict
compliance policies.
In compliance-oriented QA, the relevance of re-
trieved evidence does not imply its admissibility.
Regulatory and compliance QA necessitates gener-
ating reliable answers from complex policy texts
(Gokhan et al., 2025). In sensitive document sce-
narios, RAG also requires explicit access control to
prevent the model from generating answers based
on documents that the current user is unauthorized
to access (Hu et al., 2014; Chen et al., 2025). At the
same time, RAG systems suffer from multiple fail-
ure points across the retrieval, context construction,
and generation stages (Barnett et al., 2024); even
with external evidence, models may still generate
answers that are inconsistent with the retrieved
context, unsupported, or contradictory (Niu et al.,
2024). Furthermore, the coexistence of multiple
document versions significantly degrades the relia-
bility of RAG, as time-sensitive QA demands strict
temporal validity (Ouyang et al., 2025; Zhang et al.,
2025b). In addition, RAG responses are highly sus-
ceptible to knowledge conflicts, whether among dif-
ferent retrieved documents or between the retrieved
text and the model’s parametric knowledge (Wang
et al., 2025a; Lee et al., 2025). Collectively, these
intersecting challenges indicate that compliance-
oriented QA necessitates determining whether re-
trieved evidence is admissible prior to generation.

through various mechanisms. Advances in retrieval
