assumes questions have correct answers and eval-
uates systems on their ability to converge toward
them.
This bias becomes consequential as RAG deploy-
ments expand into domains where understanding
human opinion is the primary goal (Zhao et al.,
2024a; Brown et al., 2025; Fan et al., 2024): online
community forums, consumer reviews, customer
service, policy analysis, healthcare patient feed-
back, and political discourse. In these settings, in-
formation is inherently subjective, and collapsing it
to a single answer misrepresents reality. Yet recent
work actively reinforces this mismatch by framing
subjectivity as noise: RAGuard (Zeng et al., 2025)
treats Reddit discussions as naturally occurring mis-
information to be filtered, CONFLICTS (Cattan
et al., 2025) focuses on conflict resolution rather
than preserving perspective diversity, and robust-
ness approaches (Chen et al., 2022; Choi et al.,
2025; Zhang et al., 2025a) emphasize filtering con-
tradictory information. While valuable for factual
domains, these approaches are misaligned with sce-
narios where diverse perspectives are the goal.
The boundary between factual and opinion con-
tent is blurred in practice. Even a factual query,
such as “What are the shipping rates for small
parcels?”, will surface opinion-laden forum dis-
cussions alongside authoritative sources when the
knowledge base includes community content. A
semantic similarity-based retriever cannot distin-
guish an official rate schedule from a seller’s com-
plaint about rate increases. Current systems have
no mechanism to handle this mixture, treating all
retrieved content as equivalent evidence. Exist-
ing diversity methods do not solve this: Maxi-
mal Marginal Relevance (Carbonell and Goldstein,
1998) and Determinantal Point Process-based meth-
ods promote diversity by penalizing redundancy in
embedding space, but semantic diversity and opin-
ion diversity are fundamentally different objectives.
Two documents expressing opposing views on the
same topic may use similar vocabulary and land
close together in embedding space. MMR would
penalize retrieving both; opinion-aware retrieval
should specifically surface both.
Additionally, current RAG-based metrics can as-
sign high scores to biased outputs. A response to
“What leadership qualities are most important for
startup CEOs?” that emphasizes only traditionally
masculine-coded traits could score excellently on
ROUGE-L, context relevance, and answer faithful-
ness. However, it would underrepresent collabora-
tive and inclusive leadership perspectives (Wang
et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b; de Oliveira et al.,
2025; Thakur et al., 2025) (see Appendix K for
a concrete example). Traditional metrics tend to
reward convergence and cannot detect what was
excluded.
Related techniques like argument mining and
stance detection have matured considerably (Al-
Dayel and Magdy, 2021; Hardalov et al.,
2022), with work spanning argument-based re-
trieval (Li et al., 2025), sentiment analysis on
decentralized platforms (Chhetri et al., 2025),
and multimodal/comment-level opinion analy-
sis (Sharma et al., 2020; Li et al., 2018; Weiß-
mann et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2024), yet these ca-
pabilities remain isolated from RAG frameworks.
RAFTS (Yue et al., 2024) synthesizes contrastive
arguments but targets claim credibility, not opinion
diversity. LLMs’ inability to reliably detect con-
tradictions (Lee et al., 2025; Gokul et al., 2025)
compounds the challenge. OpinioRAG (Nayeem
and Rafiei, 2025) bridges this gap via RAG-based
opinion summarization but focuses on condensing
opinions into narratives rather than ensuring the
retrieved set faithfully represents the opinion spec-
trum.
Latest work on perspective benchmarks is a step
in this direction, but is incomplete. PIR (Zhao et al.,
2024b) and BERDS (Chen and Choi, 2025) confirm
that current retrievers are perspective-blind: PIR
shows systematic bias toward supporting stances
(∼58.5%), while BERDS finds full perspective cov-
erage in only 40% of cases. However, both evaluate
categorical perspective presence (is viewpoint X
retrieved?) rather than opinion distributions - the
intensity, affect, and demographic grounding that
characterize real-world subjective content.
Pluralistic alignment is another relevant body of
work which is complementary to opinion-aware
retrieval. A growing body of work addresses plu-
ralism at the generation layer: Sorensen et al.
(2024) formalize pluralistic value alignment for
LLMs, Bakker et al. (2022) fine-tune models to
find agreement among diverse preferences, and Ar-
gyle et al. (2023) frame LLMs as “silicon sampling”
of human opinions. However, pluralistic alignment
trains the generation model to preserve opinion dis-
tributions, while opinion-aware retrieval ensures
the retrieved context faithfully represents the dis-
tribution before generation. These are complemen-
tary: a pluralistically-aligned LLM given biased
retrieved context still produces biased responses.
